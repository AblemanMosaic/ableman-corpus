About the Ableman Corpus

The Ableman Corpus is a growing body of research by Adam Ableman on constitutional computing: how intelligent and computational systems can make meaning, authority, evidence, and effects inspectable and governable.

This Atlas is a map of the papers rather than a single linear book. Begin with a guided tour or a question, explore relationships among papers, or open any work directly. Individual papers can stand alone, but many are designed to carry context for one another.

AI disclosureAuthorship, review maturity, and novelty+

The Ableman Corpus was developed with substantial assistance from artificial intelligence. AI systems have contributed to research discovery, comparison, synthesis, formalization, drafting, cross-linking, and revision. Adam Ableman directs the conceptual program, adjudicates its architecture, and assumes authorial responsibility for the published works.

These papers function as evolving context artifacts. Some have undergone extensive internal review and integration; others are less mature, and they will benefit from revisions or deeper explication.

Many of the papers began as attempts to make complex ideas or syntheses explicit where no sufficiently precise concept was available to the AI systems being used. They are intended to make those ideas inspectable, criticizable, reusable, and open to further development.

Novelty most often arises through constitutional meta-mapping of conceptual space: bringing existing and emerging ideas into a governed relational architecture. Some entries may also contain stronger candidate novelty claims, but those claims must be evaluated paper by paper.

This disclosure is intentional. Readers should not be asked to accept an argument because it appears authoritative, nor to dismiss it merely because AI helped produce it. The corpus asks to be evaluated through its reasoning, sources, evidence, provenance, limitations, and capacity to survive criticism.
