# Bridge site

A proposed replacement front end for the corpus, built from the "Bridge" layout.
Buildless HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, like `site/`, which is left unchanged.

## Run it

```powershell
python tools/serve.py
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/site-bridge/` (the server prints the `/site/` address;
both sites are served from the same root).

## What it reads

| File | Role | Standing shown to readers |
|---|---|---|
| `metadata/corpus.json` | Titles, dates, territories, files | Corpus record |
| `site-bridge/data/atlas.json` | Map positions, related-paper links, concept counts | Links: generated. Concepts: provisional |
| `site-bridge/data/questions.json` | Question → concept → paper bindings | Question wording: interface text |
| `curation/routes.json` | Reading routes | Per route: draft or author's own |
| `curation/depth.json` | Front-to-back tiers | Per paper: draft or author's own |
| `curation/clusters.json` | Galaxy and family names | Per group: draft or author's own |
| `curation/tours.json` | Guided camera tours and opening sequences | Per tour: draft or author's own |

The two `data/` files are generated by `python tools/build_bridge_data.py`. Re-run it
whenever `metadata/corpus.json`, the semantic contracts, or `SEMANTIC_USE_CONTRACT.json`
change. The build is deterministic: the same inputs give byte-identical outputs, and
each output records its input SHA-256 values.

The four `curation/` files are read at page load, so edits need no build step.
Changing paper text changes the Galaxy layout, so rerun the build after editing papers too.

## Pages

- `#home`: the map (Galaxy by default; three layouts, three switchable layers, lenses) with
  the selected paper at five depths, then the six ways in (guided tour, route, questions, related map,
  orderings, search). The map column comes first in the page, so it is on the left on wide
  screens and on top on narrow ones (below 1000 px).
- `#route-R01`: a route with the reason for each stop.
- `#paper-AC-0001`: one paper at five depths, its route position, related papers,
  and the full Markdown text rendered in place.
- `#question-Q01`: the concepts bound to a question, their papers, and nearby papers.
- `#papers`: every paper, with whole-word search, ordering, and territory filters.
- `#about`: how the ways in, layers, and depths work, plus known data gaps.

## Guided tours and offline presentation

The home page offers a Steward Tour across the whole corpus and one tour for each of the four
territories. Highlights use the explicit opening order in `curation/tours.json`; Full sequence
continues through every eligible paper. The player opens directly into a reduced, cinematic map:
each paper title appears as horizon text while a natural-language educational subtitle explains
the stop below it. Bespoke opening narration remains an interface draft; later full-sequence stops
fall back to source-derived descriptions. Video-style controls stay faint until hovered or focused.
The circular timer drains once per stop and shows whole seconds remaining. Hovering or focusing it
reveals pause or resume, plus previous and next controls; both directions wrap across the ends.
The rainbow sun mark and the Ableman Corpus title open the compact About dialog, which also opens on
initial load. Its AI disclosure is collapsed by default and expands in place to explain AI
participation, authorship, uneven review maturity, and novelty posture.

Build the self-contained presentation with:

```powershell
python tools/build_offline_atlas.py
```

The result is `site-bridge/Ableman Corpus Atlas Offline.html`. It inlines the interface, map,
curation, metadata, logo, the locally vendored KaTeX renderer and fonts, and all current Markdown
papers; the tour and paper reader work without a server or network connection.

## Decisions you may want to revisit

1. **Map links.** The map draws `internal_work_ids`: the [A1]–[A7] cross-links in each
   paper, which the papers themselves describe as "selected by corpus profile and
   similarity review". The site calls them "related papers", not references.
   `internal_reference_count` is not used (a bare count, non-zero for 15 papers).
2. **Depth levels.** The five names come from the answer units in
   `SEMANTIC_USE_CONTRACT.json`. How each is filled for a paper is this interface's
   interpretation, set out on the `#about` page and in `depthBody()` in `app.js`.
3. **Questions.** A question shows only the papers its concepts are bound to (1–4 per
   question), then generated neighbours. Concepts with no paper binding are shown with
   a warning, not hidden and not backfilled by keyword search.
4. **Theme.** Dark only, matching the existing brand.
5. **Markdown rendering.** A small built-in renderer handles headings, paragraphs, emphasis,
   code, lists, quotes, tables, and links. A repository-local KaTeX distribution renders inline
   and display math after a paper opens; it requires no CDN or network connection. Anything else
   the renderer does not understand is shown as escaped text. Every paper page also links to the
   raw file.

## The map

- **Three layouts.** *Galaxy* (default) groups papers into galaxies and families (below).
  *Jurisdictions* puts each paper inside the exact zone of a four-way Venn diagram for its
  territories (all-four papers in the centre); within a zone, related papers sit together.
  *Similarity* comes from the related-paper links. All are precomputed in `data/atlas.json`
  (`gx,gy,gz`, `vx,vy`, `x,y`), and the toggle glides between them (`m.w` in `app.js` holds
  the blend weights).
- **Venn geometry** is fixed in `tools/build_bridge_data.py` (`VENN`, `VENN_TERRITORIES`):
  Intelligence and Computation are the outer ellipses, Possibility and Coordination the
  inner ones, an assignment chosen so every zone has room for its papers. The build
  fails with a message if the corpus outgrows a zone, and verifies every paper ends up
  inside its own zone.
- **Camera.** A perspective camera drawn on a canvas (no library). Click to engage: the map
  then takes the wheel and keys and the page does not scroll; Esc or a click elsewhere releases
  them, and typing in a form field is never captured. Two modes, switched with the **Fly** button:
  - *Orbit* (default): right-drag or Shift-drag circles a paper — the one under the pointer when
    the drag starts, otherwise the selected one, otherwise the current look point. Selecting a
    paper makes it the pivot without moving the view.
  - *Fly*: right-drag or Shift-drag turns the view in place, like a spaceship (drag right looks
    right, drag up looks up); the camera does not move.
  - Both: left-drag pans, the wheel zooms toward the pointer (steps shrink near papers), W/S fly
    forward and back, A/D sideways, Q/E roll around the screen centre (Q left, E right; Shift
    faster), arrow keys turn. Rotation is a quaternion, so there are no stops or flips.
- **Boundaries follow the papers.** The camera stays inside a sphere around all papers (see
  "Zooming out" for its size), so you can fly to any
  paper, through the corpus and around behind it, but not off into empty space. Panning stops
  when the look point leaves the inner 80% of the papers' extent along any axis (a box, not a
  sphere, so chronology depth doesn't widen how far you can drag sideways). Flight speed scales with the distance to the
  nearest paper, so you slow down as you arrive. When fewer than 6 papers are in view, an arrow
  at the edge points back toward them.
- **Space.** Near papers grow as you approach (up to 260 drawing units) and are shaded as
  spheres; labels grow with them. A fixed star field (420 stars from a fixed seed, so the same
  sky every visit) sits far outside the zoom-out limit and gives turning and flying something to move
  against. "Move to" parks at most 520 units from the paper.
- **Buttons.** "Fly" toggles the mode; "Front view" squares up on the map plane near its middle;
  "Reset view" returns to the start (also the Home key); "Full screen" fills the screen
  (browser full screen where allowed, otherwise the whole window). Touch: one finger pans, two
  fingers pinch and turn. The browser's right-click menu is blocked in the map, including when
  a right-drag ends outside it.
- **Selecting and moving are separate.** Hover shows a card. A click (or tap) selects a
  paper: it and the seven papers it lists are labelled, papers that list it show as
  dots, and the camera does not move. Clicking the selected paper again, or right-
  clicking any paper, opens a menu with "Move to <title>" (flies the camera there) and
  "Open paper". Esc closes the menu, then clears the selection. Changing layout or lens
  never moves the camera either. Motion is skipped for reduced-motion readers.
- **Keyboard.** The labelled papers have invisible, focusable buttons positioned over
  them, so Tab shows their cards, Enter selects, and Enter on the selected paper opens the
  menu with focus on "Move to" (arrow keys move between items). The full list is on
  `#papers`.
- **Performance.** Measured in a headless browser without graphics acceleration:
  2.1 ms of drawing work per frame and 44–54 fps while flying (an SVG version managed
  30 fps, which is why the view moved to canvas).

## Galaxy layout

- **Closeness** between two papers is half wording, half cross-links: cosine similarity of
  TF-IDF vectors over each paper's main text (the generated cross-link and reference
  sections are removed first, so the links aren't counted twice), scaled to 0–1, plus 1 if
  either paper lists the other. Each paper keeps its 7 closest (`GAL_K`), giving a network.
- **Groups.** Deterministic Louvain community detection finds galaxies in that network, then
  families inside each galaxy; families under 3 papers merge into their closest sibling.
  Result today: 10 galaxies, 20 families, modularity 0.515. A paper is a **bridge** when at least 40% of its closeness lies
  in one other galaxy (15 papers); bridges lean toward that galaxy and say so on their card.
- **Placement.** Galaxy centres are laid out in the map plane with empty space between
  them (closer galaxies sit nearer); families sit around their galaxy's centre; papers form
  a 3D shell around their family, most-listed papers nearest the core; a final pass pulls
  close papers together and pushes overlapping ones apart. In 3D, 47.9% of each paper's
  cross-linked neighbours and 32.1% of its wording neighbours are among its 7 nearest papers.
- **Drawing.** Each galaxy has a soft glow. Its name shows from afar and fades as it fills the
  view; its family names appear as you approach (not for one-family galaxies). Labels for
  groups that are off screen are skipped, not pinned to the edge. Galaxy depth (`gz`) is used
  only while depth is off: a lens with depth replaces it with that lens's planes.
- **Pinned galaxies.** `curation/clusters.json` can pin papers into a hand-made galaxy
  (`pinned_galaxies`; today "Physical World", 8 papers, and "Cryptography", 10 papers). The build groups every paper as
  usual, then moves pinned papers into their galaxy, so no other paper changes galaxy; families are
  then computed inside every galaxy, the pinned one included. The build reads only this section of
  the file, records a hash of the sorted pins, and stops with a message on an unknown accession ID,
  a paper pinned twice, a malformed id, or fewer than 3 members. Pins change positions, so rerun the
  build after editing them; until then the site says the pins and the map disagree. A pinned
  galaxy holds together: its papers feel no pull from papers outside it and, though they are still
  reported as bridges where most of their closeness lies elsewhere, they don't lean toward that
  galaxy. With both current pins, modularity is 0.515.
- **Names** come from `curation/clusters.json` (see `curation/README.md`). The build never
  writes names. The colour channel can also be set to *Galaxy* in the Custom lens.
- **Cost.** Pure Python, no new dependencies; the build takes longer because it reads all 196
  paper texts. Output is byte-identical on rerun.

## Lenses and 3D

- **Channels.** Depth (off, author tiers, chronology, closeness to selected), colour
  (role, galaxy, territory mix, date confidence, tier), and size (listed as related, concept
  bindings, uniform). The visible lens choices are Classic (default), Newcomer, and Custom;
  Custom exposes all three channels.
- **Camera** is view state only; positions stay precomputed. From the starting view the
  map matches the flat layout. Nearer papers draw larger (true perspective) and distant
  ones fade, so the legend says to compare sizes from the front view.
- **Default lens.** Classic: chronology depth (newest in front), role colours,
  size by how often a paper is listed. With depth on, the starting view and "Reset view" use the
  same gentle tilt a depth lens applies; "Front view" squares up. In Galaxy, depth on means the
  galaxies spread by date rather than by their own 3D shape.
- **Zooming out.** The camera can pull back to three times the starting distance (or the papers'
  radius plus 3,600 units, if larger), far enough to see the whole galaxy as one structure about a
  quarter of the view across. Flight speeds up to 2,200 units per second when far from any paper.
  The star field sits 9,000 to 12,000 units out, beyond that limit.
- **Depth range** is 1,040 map units front to back (`ZS = 520` in `app.js`), slightly more
  than the map's width, so depth layers read as separate planes.
- **Depth planes** are drawn and labelled for each level (tiers, three dates, or link
  distance). In Jurisdictions, the Venn diagram is the floor at depth zero. In Galaxy, the
  galaxies flatten onto those planes while the lens is on.
- **Tiers** come from `curation/depth.json`, read at page load (no build step). Unknown
  accession IDs, unknown tiers, duplicates, and unplaced papers are reported on the
  `#about` page and in the legend; unplaced papers sit on a "Not yet placed" plane.
- **Date confidence** is high exactly for the published papers (dated by first
  publication) and medium for the rest (dated by file times), so today it doubles as
  publication status.

- **Names up close.** Once a paper draws at least 1.45× its size at the starting distance (about
  two-thirds of that distance away), its name floats above it, fading in over the next 0.4.
  Nearest papers are named first (at most 45; 15 on small maps). A name is left out when it
  would overlap another label, the map's buttons, status line or hint, or run off the map, and
  when its paper is hidden behind a nearer one. A route stop's label is forced to show only when
  the stop itself is on screen.
- **Full titles.** Map labels show the title without its subtitle (the part after a colon or
  parenthesis). They try it in full first, in each placement: below or above the paper, beside
  it, then slid inside the map near an edge. Only when the full title fits nowhere does the
  label fall back to the first 36 characters plus "…".

## Known limits

- From the starting view, labels show for the route and the ten most-linked papers; more names
  appear as you approach. On small maps only the selected paper's label is guaranteed.
- A floating name can cross a nearby sphere it doesn't belong to; it always sits directly above
  (or, failing that, beside) its own paper.
- Route stops and the selected paper draw at a minimum size so they can hold a number
  and be clicked. That can make one touch a close neighbour.
- 15 concept-binding titles do not match a current paper title exactly and are not
  counted; they are listed on the `#about` page.
