## Abstract

Many theories begin with entities and then explain action, coordination, identity, and persistence through their interactions. The Continuation Primacy Thesis proposes a deliberate inversion: first model the possible and admissible continuations from a state, then ask which participant boundaries, identities, coordinated trajectories, and forms of agency remain intelligible within that structure.

The inversion is powerful but does not by itself prove that the future is metaphysically prior to entities. This paper distinguishes formal, explanatory, constitutive, and ontological priority. It specifies the first two, treats the third as a domain-dependent hypothesis, and leaves the fourth open. It replaces several type-invalid equations with a profile over states, conceivable and admissible transitions, viability predicates, participant trajectories, identity criteria, and joint continuation.

On this account, continuation structure can scaffold, constrain, and partly explain agency without automatically generating an agent. Shared possible futures do not alone constitute coordination, trajectory overlap does not alone establish identity, and persistence does not establish legitimacy. Continuation Primacy is presented as a focused research thesis and entry point into Dynatology, not as a duplicate of its broader formal architecture.

## 1. The Explanatory Inversion

An entity-first account begins with agents and derives a system from their actions. A continuation-first account begins with a structured set of transitions and asks which persistent participants, coordinated paths, and action capacities can exist within it.

The second perspective can reveal constraints that disappear when the agent is treated as primitive. An institution, protocol, ecological niche, or market may outlast particular participants. A policy or physical boundary may shape behavior before a particular actor chooses. Yet this observation underdetermines ontology: durable structure can be caused, reproduced, interpreted, or contested by entities even while constraining them.

Continuation Primacy should therefore begin as an explanatory reordering rather than a declaration that entities are unreal or the future exists independently.

## 2. Four Meanings of Priority

The word **primary** can express different claims:

1. **Formal priority:** a model defines states and transition conditions before defining participant trajectories over them.
2. **Explanatory priority:** some phenomena are explained more compactly by continuation constraints than by isolated actor properties.
3. **Constitutive priority:** a participant's identity depends on continuity under a declared identity criterion.
4. **Ontological priority:** continuation is more fundamental in reality than participants.

The first is a modeling choice. The second is empirically comparable. The third may hold in selected domains. The fourth is a metaphysical hypothesis that neither notation nor examples establish.

The paper's defensible central claim is therefore:

> In some domains, admissible-continuation structure may provide a more powerful explanatory basis for participation, coordination, and persistence than an entity-first model.

## 3. Typed Continuation System

Define a profiled system at time $t$:

$$
\mathcal{D}_t=(X_t,\Omega_t,A_t,C_t,V_t,O_t),
$$

where $X_t$ is the modeled state space, $\Omega_t\subseteq X_t\times X_{t+1}$ the conceivable transition relation, $A_t\subseteq\Omega_t$ the transitions admitted under constitution or constraint profile $C_t$, $V_t$ a viability predicate, and $O_t$ the observation and evidence regime.

"Admissible" is never ambient. It is admissible under a named profile, authority, time, and interpretation. Physical possibility, logical consistency, legal permission, institutional authorization, technical availability, and ethical acceptability are different predicates and may conflict.

The one-step forward continuation field is:

$$
F^{+}_{A,t}(x)=\{x'\in X_{t+1}\mid(x,x')\in A_t\}.
$$

The field is a model-relative successor set. It is not an independent substance, an intention, or proof that every represented transition will occur.

## 4. Participation

A participant cannot be intersected directly with a transition relation. Let participant $i$ have a boundary rule $B_i$, a local-state projection $\pi_i$, and a trajectory:

$$
\gamma_i=(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_n).
$$

Participant $i$ is constitutionally participating under profile $C$ when the projected transitions relevant to $B_i$ satisfy the declared participation predicate. In the simplest case:

$$
(x_k,x_{k+1})\in A_k\quad\text{for all assessed }k.
$$

This defines admitted participation, not physical existence. If $A_t=\varnothing$, the profile admits no immediate successor; existing objects need not disappear. A deadlock may instead reveal an incomplete model, terminal state, conflicting constraints, or genuine continuation collapse.

## 5. Derived Agency

Stable participation is insufficient for agency: a rock, log record, or passive component can persist through admitted transitions. An operational agency profile should test at least boundary maintenance, sensing or state discrimination, option selection, intervention, feedback sensitivity, and pursuit or preservation of a declared objective or viability condition.

Continuation structure may enable or constrain these capacities. Agency can then be **continuation-scaffolded** or **continuation-constituted under a profile** without claiming that every stable trajectory is an agent.

A comparative test should ask whether the continuation-first model predicts interventions, adaptation, failure, and persistence better than an entity-first alternative after accounting for model complexity.

## 6. Identity and Persistence

Trajectory overlap at adjacent times is too weak to establish identity: unrelated processes can share states, while one persisting process can undergo radical change. Identity requires an explicit equivalence or continuity criterion:

$$
\gamma_a\sim_{I,p}\gamma_b,
$$

where $p$ identifies the domain profile and its evidentiary tests. Possible criteria include causal continuity, organizational invariants, legal succession, cryptographic lineage, memory, bodily continuity, functional organization, or accountable reconstruction.

Continuation can support identity judgments, but it does not choose the correct criterion. Identity claims should record boundary, invariants, tolerated change, breaks, observer, evidence, and competing criteria.

Persistence may be measured by more than set overlap: reachable-set similarity, invariant retention, path viability, lineage continuity, or robustness under perturbation. No single scalar should be treated as universal.

## 7. Coordination

Two participants having overlapping successor sets does not prove coordination. They may independently reach the same state, compete for it, or make each other's paths impossible.

Coordination requires a joint model. Let $J_t$ describe joint candidate transitions and $A^{J}_t\subseteq J_t$ those jointly admissible under declared compatibility, resource, authority, and timing constraints. Participants coordinate when their coupled actions produce or preserve a joint trajectory satisfying a coordination predicate that neither local trajectory alone establishes.

This connects Continuation Primacy to **The Optional Coordinator**: coordination can arise from compatible continuation, while hierarchy may become useful when individually admissible paths are jointly inconsistent. It does not follow that hierarchy is always necessary or that shared futures are sufficient.

## 8. Intelligence, Meaning, and Consciousness

**Constitutional Intelligence** asks whether a continuation structure preserves viable paths under perturbation. That is a system-level performance property, not evidence that a field literally possesses a mind. Participants may benefit from an intelligent design or adaptive ecology without inheriting agency or intelligence automatically.

**Constitutional Meaning** treats a distinction as meaning-bearing when it alters admissible continuation under a profile. Causal influence, modeled relevance, authorized binding, and human semantic content should remain separable.

The **Ableman Consciousness Ontology** adds integrated binding, situational stakes, recursion, and self-authored revision. Continuation participation alone does not establish consciousness, moral standing, or subjective experience.

These papers specialize different questions. Continuation Primacy supplies an explanatory inversion; it does not settle their stronger criteria.

## 9. Continuation and Legitimacy

Persistence is not legitimacy. A process may continue through coercion, capture, inertia, exploit, or outdated authority. **Corruption as Illegitimate Continuation** supplies this essential distinction: continuation must remain attributable to declared warrant, invariants, evidence, renewal, and contest.

Accordingly:

$$
continued\neq admissible\neq legitimate\neq desirable.
$$

The relation $A_t$ represents admission under a profile. Whether that profile is lawful, just, current, or properly constituted is a separate question.

## 10. Time and Future Geometry

A continuation field represents structured successor possibilities from a present model. Calling this "constitutional time" is useful only if it yields measurable distinctions from ordinary transition-system analysis. It should not imply that represented futures already exist or govern causally from the future.

The productive claim is geometric: admissibility can induce reachability, neighborhoods, boundaries, components, bottlenecks, and path costs over modeled possibility. This is developed generally in **Dynatology**.

## 13. Research and Evaluation Program

Choose bounded domains with observable states, participants, interventions, and outcomes. Specify competing entity-first, continuation-first, and hybrid models before evaluation. Require each to predict transitions, perturbation responses, participant persistence, coordination failures, and boundary cases.

Compare predictive accuracy, intervention success, explanatory compression, out-of-sample transfer, calibration, sensitivity to profile choice, identity disagreement, joint-admissibility errors, measurement cost, and ethical consequence. Use ablations to determine whether continuation structure adds information beyond participant features.

Metaphysical priority is not directly validated by predictive success. The strongest empirical result would support explanatory priority under stated conditions.

$$

$$

This corrects a latent bias in continuation language. A lawful refusal, completed task, safe shutdown, retirement, or deliberate non-reproduction can be a successful constitutional outcome. A system that maximizes continuation cardinality without representing terminal legitimacy can preserve harmful, coercive, or resource-exhausting trajectories.

## Modeling Futures First Does Not Make the Future Metaphysically Primary

### Public orientation

Continuation Primacy reverses the usual explanatory order: model possible and admissible transitions first, then ask which participant boundaries, identities, coordinated paths, and agency capacities remain intelligible. This is a formal and potentially explanatory perspective. Constitutive priority is domain-dependent; ontological priority remains open.

### Adversarial tests

1. **Ontology-from-notation attack.** Defining transitions before agents in a model is treated as proof that continuations are more fundamental in reality.
2. **Model-completeness attack.** Represented futures are treated as all futures despite unknown states, innovation, adversarial action, observer limits, and open environments.
3. **Admissibility-legitimacy attack.** A permitted or persistent transition is treated as desirable, lawful, fair, or authorized.
4. **Agency-erasure attack.** Institutions, protocols, and environments explain behavior so completely that participant choice, resistance, interpretation, and causal contribution disappear.
5. **Identity-overlap attack.** Trajectory continuity, state similarity, or shared reachable futures is treated as personal or artifact identity.
6. **Coordination-overlap attack.** Compatible or overlapping continuations are treated as joint intention, cooperation, consent, or shared purpose.
7. **Survivorship-legitimacy attack.** What persists or remains viable is treated as what deserves to continue.
8. **Dynatology-duplication attack.** The focused thesis expands until it duplicates the wider Dynatology architecture and loses a distinct falsifiable role.

### Claim and evidence ceiling

The paper establishes a typed modeling inversion and research thesis. It does not establish metaphysical priority, complete continuation spaces, derived consciousness, automatic agency, identity, coordination, intelligence, meaning, legitimacy, or empirical superiority to entity-first accounts. Its proper corpus role is a bounded projection and entry point into Dynatology.

Evaluation requires paired entity-first and continuation-first models, predictive and explanatory comparisons, negative domains, observer and uncertainty bounds, explicit identity and coordination criteria, affected-party interpretation, and current external research. None was performed in this pass.

### Reader-facing consequence

The thesis is strongest when it reveals which futures a system makes available or impossible before naming an agent - while keeping open who created those conditions, who contests them, and whether any admissible continuation should be chosen.

## 16. Conclusion

Continuation Primacy is most valuable as an inversion of explanatory order. Instead of assuming agents and asking what they do, it asks which continuations are possible, which are admitted, which remain viable, and what participant boundaries and identities those structures can sustain.

That inversion does not erase entities or prove a metaphysics of the future. It offers a research program: define the continuation profile, compare it with entity-first alternatives, preserve the difference between persistence and legitimacy, and test whether the structure of admissible futures explains more with less distortion.
