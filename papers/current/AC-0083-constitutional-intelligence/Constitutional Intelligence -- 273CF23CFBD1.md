## Intelligence as a Property of Admissible Continuation

### The intelligence of a system may reside less in its participants than in the futures they permit.

## Abstract
This paper proposes a constitutional theory of intelligence.

Most theories locate intelligence within agents, representations, models, or optimization procedures. Intelligence is treated as a property possessed by an entity. The constitutional perspective begins elsewhere.

The framework proposes that intelligence may be a property of admissible continuation itself.

Within this view, agents are participants in constitutional structures that shape the space of possible futures. Intelligent behavior emerges when constitutional structure systematically preserves viable continuation across changing conditions. The object of analysis becomes neither representation nor optimization but the geometry of admissible futures.

The paper introduces:

* constitutional intelligence,
* continuation intelligence,
* intelligence density,
* constitutional cognition.

The central claim is that many systems commonly described as intelligent possess no identifiable intelligent component. Their apparent intelligence arises from participation within constitutional structures whose admissible continuations are themselves adaptive, coherent, and robust.

The framework does not deny the existence of intelligent agents.

It proposes that intelligence may exist at a deeper organizational level than agents themselves.

> Intelligence may be a property of continuation before it becomes a property of participants.

## 1. Introduction

The dominant question of intelligence research has historically been:

> What makes an agent intelligent?

The answers vary.

Intelligence has been attributed to:

* reasoning,
* optimization,
* planning,
* memory,
* prediction,
* representation,
* self-modeling,
* computation.

Despite disagreement regarding mechanisms, most theories share an assumption.

Intelligence belongs to an entity.

The constitutional perspective begins elsewhere.

It asks:

> What if intelligence is not primarily a property of agents?

Across nature and engineering, coherent adaptive behavior often appears in systems whose individual components possess little apparent intelligence:

* ant colonies,
* immune systems,
* ecosystems,
* markets,
* neural populations,
* routing networks,
* distributed software systems.

The collective behavior frequently exceeds what appears available to any individual participant.

This paper proposes a framework for describing that phenomenon.

## 4. Constitutional Intelligence

A system exhibits constitutional intelligence when admissible continuation systematically preserves viability across changing conditions.

Let:

$$
V_t
$$

denote viability.

Then constitutional intelligence corresponds to the preservation of:

$$
V_t
\rightarrow
V_{t+k}
$$

through admissible continuation.

The intelligence resides not in selecting optimal states but in maintaining viable futures.

This distinction separates constitutional intelligence from optimization.

Optimization seeks preferred outcomes.

Constitutional intelligence preserves continuation.

## 5. Continuation Intelligence

The framework defines continuation intelligence as:

> The capacity of a constitutional structure to preserve viable continuation across perturbation.

Formally:

Let:

$$

$$

denote perturbation.

$$

$$

denote the viability set.

A constitutional structure exhibits continuation intelligence when:

$$
P(\mathcal V)
\subseteq
\mathcal V'
$$

and:

$$
\mathcal V'
\neq
\emptyset.
$$

Continuation remains possible despite disturbance.

The system therefore behaves intelligently without requiring centralized reasoning.

## 17. Propositions

### Proposition 1 (Constitutional Dominance)

Assume two systems:

$$
X
$$

and

$$
Y.
$$

If:

$$
A_X
\supset
A_Y
$$

with respect to viable continuation and participants are equivalent, then:

$$
I(X)
>
I(Y).
$$

#### Proof Sketch

The richer admissibility structure preserves a larger viability region.

Continuation intelligence therefore increases.

---

### Proposition 2 (Participant Insufficiency)

Participant capability alone does not guarantee constitutional intelligence.

#### Proof Sketch

A system may contain highly capable participants while possessing no admissible continuation from critical states.

Continuation collapses despite participant capability.

Therefore constitutional intelligence and participant intelligence are distinct quantities.

## Conclusion

This paper proposes a constitutional theory of intelligence.

Intelligence is not treated as a property exclusively possessed by agents.

Instead, intelligence is treated as a property of admissible continuation.

Participants instantiate constitutional structure.

Constitutional structure preserves viable futures.

The resulting behavior appears intelligent because continuation itself is organized intelligently.

The framework introduces constitutional intelligence, continuation intelligence, constitutional cognition, and intelligence density as tools for describing this phenomenon.

The central hypothesis:

> Intelligence corresponds to the structure of viable continuation rather than the computational capability of individual participants alone.

If correct, intelligent agents may be one manifestation of a more general phenomenon.

The deeper object is not the participant.

The deeper object is the continuation relation.
