GOORRT: The Governed Object-Operation-Realization Representation Triad

A Jurisdictional Representation Architecture for Governed Semantic Computing

GOML addresses governed object and state meaning; GSOL addresses governed operations and transitions; MCIR addresses machine-oriented realization representations.

Author
Adam Ableman
Constitutional Computing
Contact: ableman.research@gmail.com

Abstract

Governed semantic systems repeatedly collapse three different questions: what an object or state means, what operations may change it, and how a selected operation is computationally realized. GOORRT proposes a three-family representation architecture to keep those jurisdictions distinct while connecting them through typed correspondence, authority, evidence, gap, refusal, and standing records. GOML addresses governed object and state meaning; GSOL addresses governed operations and transitions; MCIR addresses machine-oriented realization representations. The triad is best treated as a candidate primary projection for the current corpus, not as a proof that every representation concern reduces to three axes. A representation is not the object, operation, execution, authority, or evidence it denotes. The paper identifies native-jurisdiction, projection, and co-representation cases; locates supporting records without promoting them automatically into peer languages; maps GOORRT into the universal Governed Semantic Model and the Possibility-Specification-CM1-CM2-IC-IR-Evidence-Revision funnel; and states counterexamples that would require extension or demotion.

Keywords: governed representation; object models; operation contracts; intermediate representation; correspondence; semantic projection; Constitutional Computing; GMB; universal GSM.

Purpose

GOORRT – the Governed Object–Operation–Realization Representation Triad – names three primary representation families proposed for a constitutionally governed semantic/computational substrate:

  1. GOML – Governed Object/Model Language
  2. GSOL – Governed Semantic Operation Language
  3. MCIR – Meta-Compilation Intermediate Representation

Together, these form the core representational scaffold for Jnana, MCIR migration, MCRM comparison, and later MosaiCOS work.

GOORRT exists to prevent representation collapse. It keeps object semantics, operation semantics, and computational realization distinct while allowing them to be bound by explicit correspondence, admission, receipt, gap, and refusal records.

The slogan form:

GOML says what object or state is represented.
GSOL says what operation or transition is represented as admissible,
refused, or observed.
MCIR says how a computational realization is represented.

Or, more formally:

GOML = governed object/state representation
GSOL = governed operation/transition representation
MCIR = governed computational realization representation

Core Claim

GOORRT is a candidate primary representation triad for the current GOML/GSOL/MCIR/Jnana architecture. It proposes that three recurring jurisdictions should remain distinct:

  1. governed object and state meaning;
  2. governed operations and transitions;
  3. computational realization representation.

The claim is architectural and falsifiable, not a completeness theorem. A fourth peer family is unnecessary only when an additional concern can be represented without loss as a typed profile, relation, evidence object, context, or bridge record attached to one or more of the three families. Authority, provenance, time, intent, evidence, uncertainty, embodiment, interaction, and institution remain possible counterexamples. If any requires independent syntax, semantics, validity rules, evolution, and irreducible transformations that the triad cannot carry without distortion, GOORRT must expand or narrow its scope.

GOORRT therefore prevents a useful class of representation collapse without claiming that three files, languages, or schemas exhaust semantic reality.

The Three Representation Families

1. GOML – Governed Object/Model Language

GOML is proposed as the governing representation family for governed objects, states, institutions, roles, authorities, identities, and semantic structures.

GOML answers:

What is being represented?
What object, institution, role, state, or standing exists?
What boundaries and identity conditions define it?
What jurisdiction or authority profile applies?

Examples of GOML-native structures include:

object model
institution model
role model
authority model
jurisdiction model
standing state
epistemic position
semantic object boundary

GOML may be native for a claim when the thing being represented is primarily semantic, institutional, legal, constitutional, or authority-bearing. In those cases, MCIR may serve as a computational projection, but not as the native home of meaning.

2. GSOL – Governed Semantic Operation Language

GSOL is proposed as the governing representation family for governed operations, transitions, effects, refusals, gaps, receipts, standing ceilings, and admission rules.

GSOL answers:

What operation is being proposed?
What transition is allowed?
What effects are permitted or forbidden?
What receipts are required?
What gaps or refusals must be recorded?
What standing may this operation earn?

Examples of GSOL-native structures include:

operation contract
admission operation
refusal operation
gap certificate
transition rule
receipt requirement
standing ceiling
nonclaim ledger
anti-loop rule
blocker triage rule

GSOL may be native for a claim when the core meaning lies not in an object or program, but in the governed act itself: an admission, refusal, delegation, migration, comparison, rebuild, or standing transition.

3. MCIR – Meta-Compilation Intermediate Representation

MCIR is proposed as the governing representation family for machine-oriented descriptions of computational realization, executable structure, compilation, replay, transformation, and checkable behavior.

MCIR answers:

How is this computationally realized?
How can it execute, compile, replay, transform, or compare?
What identified computational form is controlling under the selected
profile?
What receipts or traces does execution produce?

Examples of MCIR-native structures include:

program representation
intermediate representation
compilation unit
execution graph
replay form
transformation pipeline
binary/source relationship surface
computational witness

MCIR may be native for a claim when the thing being represented is fundamentally computational. In such cases, GOML and GSOL describe the object and governance structure around it, while MCIR carries the identified machine-oriented realization representation.

GOORRT Is Not a Hierarchy

GOORRT should not be modeled as:

GOML -> GSOL -> MCIR

or:

MCIR as the only real canonical representation

Instead, the relationship is a governed correspondence loop:

GOML object/state
  is acted on by
GSOL operation/transition
  which may be realized or checked through
MCIR computational form
  which emits receipts/traces/gaps
  which update
GOML standing/state

The loop form is important because different cases have different native homes.

Three Correspondence Cases

Case 1 – GOML/GSOL as Source of Truth

The object or operation is primarily semantic, institutional, legal, constitutional, or governance-native.

In this case:

GOML/GSOL are native for the identified semantic or operational claim.
MCIR is a computational projection or checking realization.

Example:

A jurisdictional standing change is not reducible to MCIR.
It can be checked or carried by MCIR, but its native meaning is
GOML/GSOL.

Case 2 – MCIR as Source of Truth

The object is fundamentally computational.

In this case:

MCIR is native for the identified computational claim.
GOML/GSOL describe the object boundary, operation contract, and
governance conditions.

Example:

A compiled fixture or transformation pipeline may treat an
identified MCIR artifact as the controlling representation for a
bounded computational claim.

Case 3 – Co-Represented Claims

Neither side fully reduces to the other.

In this case:

GOML, GSOL, and MCIR are bound by an explicit
correspondence/admission record.

This is the expected default for Stage B MCRM/Jnana migration work.

The primary triad is complete, but Stage B requires typed supporting records. These should not be treated as fourth, fifth, or sixth peer languages. They are profiles or bridge records inside the GOORRT architecture.

A bridge record binding GOML, GSOL, MCIR, MCRM, receipts, gaps, and epistemic state.

Relationship to MCRM

MCRM is not a fourth representation family. It is a comparison method or comparison regime used to relate representations without collapsing them.

Within GOORRT:

GOML defines the object/state.
GSOL defines the operation.
MCIR defines the computational realization.
MCRM compares witnessed realizations.
CAR records the correspondence/admission result.
RTR supplies the receipts.
GRR records gaps/refusals.
EPR records the updated standing.

MCRM should never emit unqualified equivalence. Its strongest honest claim is bounded correspondence under declared inputs, receipts, and gaps.

Why Three Families May Be Sufficient

The triad covers three high-value questions for governed computation:

Object / State: What is represented as existing or holding?
Operation / Transition: What change is proposed, admitted, refused,
or observed?
Realization Representation: By what machine-oriented structure is a
selected computation described or carried?

Supporting concerns remain necessary. JAR, EPR, GRR, RTR, and CAR are candidate profiles or bridge records only if their own semantics can be expressed compositionally and queried without erasing their jurisdiction. The classification cannot be decided by naming convention. A receipt schema may be attached to an operation or realization while still requiring a distinct evidence ontology. An authority record may profile an object while authority validation remains a separate operation. “Not a peer language” is therefore a design proposal to test, not a status that GOORRT can grant by assertion.

The strongest present claim is: the triad is a compact organizing projection that exposes object/operation/realization confusion across the reviewed sources. Its sufficiency remains contingent on schemas, examples, loss analysis, round-trip behavior, and competing decompositions.

Recommended Bounded Definition

GOORRT is the Governed Object-Operation-Realization Representation Triad: a candidate three-family architecture for keeping governed object/state meaning, governed operation/transition meaning, and machine-oriented realization representation distinct while binding them through explicit, versioned, and evidence-bearing correspondence relations.

In this bounded definition:

GOML represents governed objects, states, roles, institutions, and
semantic structures.
GSOL represents governed operations, transitions, effects, refusals,
and obligations.
MCIR represents machine-oriented realization, compilation,
transformation, replay, or comparison structures.

Each family may be native for a claim without becoming the only real representation. A representation’s canonical identity, if granted by an applicable profile, does not make it identical to its referent or guarantee that a parser, runtime, or effect conforms to it. Correspondence, translation, admission, and evidence remain separate relations.

Representation Is Not Reality

GOORRT names representation families. GOML is not the object or institution; GSOL is not the operation or authority to perform it; MCIR is not the running system or effect. Each representation requires an exact identity, schema/version, interpretation environment, and claim boundary. A correspondence record must identify the source and target representations, relation type, translation or comparison procedure, preservation obligations, known loss, evidence, authority basis, and standing ceiling.

The three source cases are clearer when phrased as native jurisdiction rather than unqualified “source of truth”:

  • In a semantic-native case, GOML or GSOL controls the intended object or operation meaning, while MCIR is a projection or checking representation.
  • In a computation-native case, MCIR controls the identified computational structure, while GOML and GSOL describe its semantic boundary and governed use.
  • In a co-representation case, no family is globally controlling; a typed correspondence contract states which properties must agree and which differences are permitted.

Co-representation is not co-identity. Round-trip stability, observational equivalence, refinement, simulation, trace correspondence, and bounded semantic preservation are different claim types and require different evidence.

GMB, Universal GSM, and the Realization Funnel

The Governed Meaning Base (GMB) is the current name in the author-confirmed CCF -> CMB -> GMB lineage. GMB is a governed meaning and lineage substrate in which GOML, GSOL, MCIR, their schemas, relationships, receipts, gaps, standing, and revision histories may be stored and queried. GOORRT is a representation pattern within that wider fabric, not a replacement for GMB and not proof that stored meanings correspond to reality.

Within the universal Governed Semantic Model (GSM), the triad can project across the realization funnel:

$$ \begin{aligned} &P \\ &\rightarrow S \\ &\rightarrow CM_1 \\ &\rightarrow CM_2 \\ &\rightarrow IC \\ &\rightarrow IR \\ &\rightarrow E \\ &\rightarrow R \end{aligned}. $$

Possibility contains candidate objects, operations, representations, translations, and realizations. Specification defines their types, invariants, semantics, and correspondence obligations. Configuration Manifold 1 (CM1) contains all admissible representation profiles, schemas, translators, and realization choices. Configuration Manifold 2 (CM2) selects preferred admissible choices under declared objectives such as fidelity, inspectability, cost, portability, and assurance. IC means Instance Constitution or Instance Configuration interchangeably and binds the selected schema versions, relations, authorities, and evidence profile. IR means Instance Realization or Implementation Realization interchangeably and contains the actual documents, graphs, code, binaries, runtimes, and effects. Evidence evaluates syntax, semantics, correspondence, execution, and observed effects. Revision changes any earlier layer while retaining lineage.

No family maps one-to-one onto one funnel stage. GOML, GSOL, and MCIR can all appear in Specification, both manifolds, IC, IR descriptions, evidence, and revision records. The funnel concerns realization and standing; GOORRT concerns representation jurisdiction.

Primitive and Invariant Kernel

The integrated primitive set is: representation artifact, representation family, schema/version, referent, native jurisdiction, projection, translation edge, correspondence contract, preservation obligation, loss/gap declaration, comparison method, evidence bundle, authority basis, standing ceiling, and revision lineage.

The governing invariants are:

  1. A representation is not identical to its referent.
  2. Execution evidence does not grant execution authority or representational canonicity.
  3. Syntactic validity does not establish semantic correspondence.
  4. Correspondence does not imply equivalence; equivalence does not imply identity.
  5. A translation must state preserved, transformed, omitted, and unknown properties.
  6. A bridge record cannot award itself standing.
  7. A family declared native for one claim does not control every claim about the artifact.
  8. Schema, interpreter, comparator, and runtime identities belong to the evidence boundary.
  9. Supporting profiles remain distinct even when encoded inside a shared carrier.
  10. Revision must identify invalidated correspondence and dependent claims.

Jurisdictional Representation Decomposition

The completed P1 corpus sharpens GOORRT into a jurisdictional decomposition of object/model, operation, and realization representations with explicit correspondence records. The triad prevents one representation family from silently governing questions outside its jurisdiction. It is not a hierarchy, a complete language standard, or proof that exactly three families suffice in every domain.

A co-representation case should bind:

$$ \begin{aligned} R= (&object\_representation, operation\_representation, \\ &realization\_representation, jurisdictions, \\ &source\_of\_truth, correspondence, residuals, evidence, \\ &standing, revision) \end{aligned}. $$

GOML describes governed objects or models. GSOL describes governed operations or transitions. MCIR carries canonical machine-legible commitments across transformations. A domain may designate one source of truth, use co-representation, or adopt another decomposition. Every choice must state what happens when representations disagree.

Correspondence is not equality. A valid object model can have several admissible operation sets; one operation can have several realizations; a realization can diverge from both model and operation; and evidence can defeat a previously admitted correspondence. JAR, EPR, GRR, RTR, and CAR remain supporting records rather than new sources of authority by name.

Representation-to-Effect Jurisdiction Boundary

The fixed-seed comparison with SMT retrieval, Meta-Binding, communal computing, and APD clarifies that co-representation ends before physical or institutional effect unless a separately governed correspondence is supplied:

$$ \begin{aligned} J= (&object\_representation, operation\_representation, \\ &realization\_representation, correspondence, \\ &effect\_surface, authority, evidence, remainder) \end{aligned}. $$

SMT can admit nodes and paths from several representation families while refusing or leaving unknown their correspondence. Meta-Binding can test whether a represented operation over object constraints changes later behavior, but that functional result does not prove that the realization conforms or that the effect was contained.

Communal computing can project governed objects, proposed operations, and realized public artifacts into shared physical space. Placement, gesture, possession, or majority attention does not establish source-of-truth status or correspondence. Each public interpretation needs a declared representation family and appealable mapping.

APD applies at the realization and effect boundary. A complete GOORRT triad can describe a containment plan while leaving bypasses, hidden channels, provider effects, or irreversible remainder outside the representation. Representation conformance is therefore not an APD claim.

CORRESPONDS_TO_REALIZATION_UNDER_EVIDENCE, ENTERS_APD_EFFECT_SURFACE,
and
DOES_NOT_PROVE_EFFECT_CONTAINMENT.

This randomized review establishes four editorial commitments: add the effect surface and authority boundary to co-representation cases; keep retrieval admission below correspondence; make communal projections representation-explicit; and prohibit representational completeness from implying APD containment.

Claim-Relative Representation Jurisdiction

Ten downstream uses apply GOORRT to communal objects and shared operations, agentic courts, controlled-language object models, operational constraints, retrieval, legacy migration, binding-quality evaluation, sovereignty, enforcement, and APD containment. They confirm the value of separating object/model, operation, and realization representations while also exposing the danger of treating the triad as complete by declaration.

The correspondence case is:

$$ \begin{aligned} C^*= (&claim, families, native\_jurisdiction, source\_of\_truth, \\ &representations, correspondences, translations, \\ &preservation, loss, evidence, authority, standing, \\ &disagreement, invalidation, effect\_boundary, remainder) \end{aligned}. $$

Source of truth is claim-relative. GOML may govern one object-state claim, GSOL one operation contract, and MCIR one machine-oriented realization description. Co-representation requires an explicit disagreement policy; no file, graph, or compiler output can silently become native authority for every claim. JAR, EPR, GRR, RTR, and CAR remain candidate profiles or bridge records unless independent semantics justify peer-family standing.

Representation completeness is not effect closure. A full triad can omit bypasses, actors, evidence ontologies, time, interaction, institutional law, provider behavior, or irreversible external remainder. The architecture should be compared with a unified typed graph and alternate decompositions, with independent classification, translation-loss, round-trip, and multi-or-no-family fixtures.

Counterevidence and Competing Decompositions

GOORRT should be expanded, narrowed, or rejected if: (1) authority requires an irreducible peer language rather than a GOML profile plus GSOL validation; (2) evidence and provenance require independent semantics that cannot be expressed without distortion; (3) time, interaction, context, institution, or uncertainty repeatedly crosscuts all three families; (4) GOML and GSOL cannot be separated without duplicating most syntax and semantics; (5) MCIR varies so widely by runtime that it is a family label without stable obligations; (6) co-representation records cannot prevent semantic drift; (7) two independent modelers cannot classify the same artifacts consistently; (8) round-trip translations systematically lose properties the architecture calls preserved; (9) supporting records evolve as independent languages; (10) the triad encourages implementers to mistake encodings for effects; (11) the same result is achieved more simply by a typed graph without three named families; (12) the three-family boundary impedes domain-specific representations; (13) MCRM comparisons cannot state useful relations short of equivalence; or (14) a claimed complete triad conceals excluded actors, evidence, or authority.

Evaluation should compare GOORRT with at least one unified typed-graph model and one alternative decomposition. Required tests include independent classification, schema validation, translation loss, round-trip behavior, claim-specific correspondence, dependency invalidation, and adversarial examples that fit multiple or no families.

Separate Representation Roles Without Forcing Every System Into Three Boxes

Public orientation

GOORRT distinguishes representations of objects, operations, and realizations so a system does not silently substitute what exists, what may be done, and how it is computationally enacted. The triad is a disciplined decomposition and comparison tool. It is not proof that every representation belongs exclusively to one family or that correspondence between families is lossless.

Adversarial tests

  1. Forced-triad attack. A mixed or reflexive representation is assigned to one family merely to preserve the model. Multi-role records and justified alternate decompositions must remain possible.
  2. MCRM-layer-creep attack. The correspondence relation becomes an unacknowledged fourth representation family or a universal semantic oracle. Its inputs, outputs, and authority must remain explicit.
  3. Correspondence-as-equivalence attack. A mapping is treated as identity, substitutability, or behavioral equivalence without a declared property set and direction.
  4. Lowering-loss attack. Object meaning or operation obligation disappears during realization, while syntactic success is reported as semantic preservation.
  5. Authority-propagation attack. Permission attached to an object or operation is assumed to authorize every realization that can encode it.
  6. Incomplete-operation attack. Preconditions, refusals, side effects, concurrency, exception behavior, or recovery semantics are absent from the operation representation.
  7. Representation-reality attack. A complete representation is mistaken for the represented system, environment, person, or institution.
  8. Vocabulary-collision attack. Realization-stage IR is confused with Intermediate Representation, causing a stage, artifact, or assurance claim to be silently renamed.

Claim and evidence ceiling

The paper proposes three representation jurisdictions plus explicit correspondence machinery. It does not prove ontological completeness, exclusive classification, semantic identity, lossless translation, executable correctness, or authority conservation. MCRM is a bounded relation among declared representations, not independent proof that the relation is adequate.

Claims about compiler design, formal semantics, interoperability, verification, cognition, institutional modeling, or domain portability require external comparison and exact worked realizations. Here IR in the realization funnel means Instance Realization or Implementation Realization; IR in a named representation such as MCIR means Intermediate Representation. No external research was added in this pass.

Reader-facing consequence

The accessible question is simple: are we talking about the thing, the allowed transformation, or the machinery that performs it - and what evidence shows that meaning and obligation survived movement between those views?
