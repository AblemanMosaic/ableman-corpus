## Abstract

What does it mean for two people to share an experience without possessing identical experiences? This addendum extends the contextual experiential-binding proposal from the constitution of individual qualitative episodes to capability, mind attribution, experiential access, and intersubjective participation. Its central claim is that shared experience can be modeled as a higher-order, consequential binding among distinct participants and their encounters, preserving both warranted commonality and intelligible difference. The shared organization is not presumed to be a third phenomenal subject.

Six developments organize the argument. First, a cognitive capability is treated as a sustained achievement dependent on organization, constraints, resources, and conditions, rather than on complexity alone. Second, named faculties may compose overlapping formative capacities; the proposal does not posit a single dedicated detector or a specific evolutionary origin. Third, the realization of activity is separated from an observer's attribution of capability or experience. Fourth, experiential access is indexed by distinction, route, conditions, and occasion, avoiding a global ranking of consciousness from a local perceptual limitation. Fifth, semantic correspondence is formalized relative to a comparison contract and representational resolution: shared reference, category agreement, functional correspondence, and phenomenal identity remain different claims. Sixth, interpersonal understanding is distinguished from agreement. A more adequate account of another standpoint can preserve, clarify, or even reveal disagreement rather than eliminate it.

The formal development introduces partial semantic projections, resolution refinement, directional perspective bridges, standpoint-tagged difference accounts, and interaction-sensitive encounter records. Elementary propositions show that coarse equivalence need not imply finer equivalence, invariance need not imply truth, synchronized outputs need not imply reciprocal interaction, and predictive understanding need not increase with agreement. Proposed studies distinguish shared component reuse from generic task effects, attribution from target performance, alternative access from identical phenomenology, and contingent interaction from common exposure. The contribution is a source-grounded research architecture for making experiential commonality and difference answerable to inquiry, not a solution by definition to phenomenal identity or the existence of consciousness.

**Keywords:** shared experience; intersubjectivity; semantic binding; formative capability; realization shell; mind attribution; experiential access; co-projection; objectivity; perspective; mutual understanding; difference preservation.

## 1. Introduction: The Same Encounter, Not the Same Standpoint

Consider two people attending to a returning melody. Both identify its recurrence and its relation to an earlier passage. One hears it through an association with homecoming; the other through an association with farewell. Neither association has to cancel the common musical recognition. Nor does their common recognition establish that the encounters feel identical. When they discuss the difference, something further becomes possible: a shared account of how the same passage has entered their histories differently.

This hypothetical example exposes several meanings of *sharing*. The participants can share an event, a referent, a category, a structural recognition, a practical activity, a conversation, or an account of their differences. These need not coincide. A theory that reduces sharing to identical internal content loses the significance of distinct standpoints. A theory that treats standpoints as wholly incommensurable loses the possibility of warranted comparison.

The Locus framework provides an intermediate architecture. Meaning is constituted through typed, constrained, consequential bindings; semantic identity depends on preservation of specified invariants; and co-projection coordinates local descriptions without requiring their complete equivalence [L1, Sections 4 and 7.4; L2, Sections 5 and 8.5]. The companion qualia paper extends that architecture into a hypothesis about qualitative specificity while keeping semantic organization, physical realization, and phenomenal constitution distinct [Q1, Sections 2 and 4].

The present addendum asks what follows when the relevant binding includes more than one situated participant. The result is not merely a larger inventory of private experiences. It is a proposal about relations among encounters: what survives comparison, what remains particular, how recognition occurs, and what interaction itself contributes.

The central thesis is:

> Sharing an experience can consist in making both commonality and difference mutually intelligible, rather than eliminating the difference between experiencers.

This thesis concerns a family of intersubjective relations. It does not require that every shared encounter include explicit reflection, verbal exchange, agreement, or equal contribution. It also does not infer phenomenal experience from interaction alone. Where the participants are treated as experiencers, that status is a premise of the example or a separately warranted research assumption, not a conclusion manufactured by the binding notation.

### 1.1 The progression of the argument

A sustained activity first requires a working realization. That activity may then be detected or interpreted by another system. The interpreting system has its own capacities, access limitations, expectations, and history. Two systems can establish a common semantic distinction even when their realization routes differ. Their interaction can produce an additional relational organization in which those differences become explicit and consequential.

These are explanatory dependencies, not a compulsory developmental sequence. A standing relationship can precede an encounter; recognition can alter attention; a conversation can change a participant's later interpretation; and stored representations can support renewed participation after an interruption. The expanded Locus manuscript similarly treats its binding hierarchy as structural rather than universally chronological [L2, Appendix D].

### 1.2 Contribution and limits of priority

The addendum's contribution is a particular integration: capability contracts, attribution models, route-specific access, resolution-relative semantic correspondence, difference-preserving perspective bridges, and interaction-sensitive realization tests. It does not claim to originate relational theories of meaning, intersubjectivity, perspective-taking, or interactional accounts of social understanding.

The source corpus already identifies participatory sense-making, distributed cognition, and enactive approaches as relevant antecedents [L1, Section 3; L2, Section 3]. Those acknowledgments are retained. This document does not present a new systematic literature review or imply that the primary works cited by those manuscripts were independently re-examined for this addendum. A publication submission would require a dedicated comparison with that literature before making a stronger novelty claim.

## 2. Source Lineage and the New Explanatory Target

The companion papers already supply many of the required distinctions. They separate category, source, episode, and record identity; distinguish content coordinates from regime, competence, and evidence profiles; require independently justified cross-person transport; and deny automatic promotion from operational equivalence to phenomenal identity [Q1, Sections 5.5, 7.6, 8.2, and 11.5; Q2, Sections 4 and 5]. The present work extends rather than replaces those commitments.

- **Inherited commitment:** Constitutional, standing, and realization loci  
  **Source role:** Distinguish invariants, preserved enabling structures, and current support  
  **Extension developed here:** Separate possible access, operating support, and actual participation

- **Inherited commitment:** Recursive versus reflexive Meta-Binding  
  **Source role:** Distinguish higher-order composition from causally effective self-representation  
  **Extension developed here:** Model shared encounters and participants' representations of the relationship

- **Inherited commitment:** Co-projection  
  **Source role:** Preserve common content and standpoint-specific claims  
  **Extension developed here:** Construct a shared account that includes warranted differences

- **Inherited commitment:** Multidimensional grounding  
  **Source role:** Separate perceptual, inferential, practical, and other routes  
  **Extension developed here:** Define distinction- and route-specific access profiles

- **Inherited commitment:** Semantic Manifold Competence  
  **Source role:** Characterize operations over structured meaning without identifying their hardware  
  **Extension developed here:** Formulate shared formative-capability and observer-attribution hypotheses

- **Inherited commitment:** Dynatological continuation and joint admissibility  
  **Source role:** Distinguish describable states from jointly attainable continuations  
  **Extension developed here:** Model interaction paths without inferring joint realization from marginal possibilities

- **Inherited commitment:** Typed evidence, continuity, and admission  
  **Source role:** Keep observation, audit, and status distinct  
  **Extension developed here:** Prevent attribution or interpersonal agreement from becoming a phenomenal certificate

The table assigns responsibilities; it is not evidence that all sources already define one interoperable formal system. In particular, ACO defines *meaning-binding* as constraint relevance rather than Locus-style semantic constitution and does not make qualia a definitional criterion [C2, Sections 2 and 10]. The companion paper explicitly retains that difference [Q1, Section 3.7]. This addendum uses ACO for its non-scalar, local regime perspective, not as an independent proof of phenomenal experience.

Likewise, SMT is used in its main manuscript's diagnostic sense: auditing declared continuity under an authority regime [G2, Abstract and Section 21]. It does not establish whether an experience exists, determine what a person feels, or render an empirical claim true by authorizing it. CSC distinguishes canonical representation from admitted binding status, and explicitly denies that admission guarantees correctness [G3, Sections 4 and 5]. Those boundaries remain in force.

### 2.1 The new hypotheses

The research proposals introduced here can be stated compactly:

**H-F: formative reuse.** Some higher-level cognitive and social-perceptual capabilities are supported by reusable formative operations whose composition, support conditions, and role assignments differ across tasks.

**H-A: attribution dependence.** Attributions of capability or experience depend partly on the observer's evidence interface, access profile, and learned expectations, not solely on properties of the target.

**H-I: interactional contribution.** Some shared encounters have relational properties and continuation effects that depend on actual contingent interaction, beyond the effects of common exposure considered alone.

**H-U: difference-preserving understanding.** Explicit representation of commonality, local commitments, and unresolved differences can improve bounded interpersonal correspondence beyond agreement-seeking or unqualified similarity models.

These hypotheses do not imply the companion paper's H-P hypothesis that a realized binding organization constitutes qualitative specificity. They identify additional structures and measurements through which that broader research program can be developed [Q1, Section 4.1].

## 3. A Capability Is an Operating Achievement

### 3.1 Organization, constraints, and supply

The motivating engine analogy distinguishes a collection of parts from an operating system. The point is not that cognition literally is an engine, nor that mechanical operation explains phenomenal existence. It is that a capability cannot be specified adequately by listing components while ignoring their organization and enabling conditions.

A proposed capability contract is:

$$
\mathcal K=\langle T,X,Y,\Gamma,\mathcal R,\mathcal I,J,W\rangle,
\tag{1}
$$

where $T$ specifies the task, $X$ the permitted input family, $Y$ the required outcomes, $\Gamma$ the organizational and operational constraints, $\mathcal R$ the resource and support conditions, $\mathcal I$ the evaluation interval, $J$ the scope, and $W$ the witnessing procedure. The contract is an investigator's specification. It is not presumed to be a representation stored by the organism or machine performing the task.

Three predicates should remain separate:

$$
\operatorname{Capable}_J(a,\mathcal K),\qquad
\operatorname{Enabled}(a,\mathcal K,t),\qquad
\operatorname{Exercising}(a,\mathcal K,\mathcal I).
\tag{2}
$$

The first concerns a disposition under specified conditions; the second concerns whether relevant enabling conditions currently hold; the third concerns actual activity over an interval. None automatically establishes a fourth predicate, phenomenal experience. Evidence of a disposition also does not have to take the form of successful performance on every occasion.

A system can retain a capability while not exercising it. A failed performance may reflect unavailable inputs, exhausted resources, an unsuitable interface, or a genuine competence limitation. The explanation must be tested rather than inferred from failure alone. Conversely, an investigator should not preserve an unfalsifiable capacity claim by inventing an unmeasured enabling condition after every failure.

This operational emphasis extends the source distinction between a selected Instance Constitution and its actual realization in an environment [L2, Sections 12.4-12.5]. It also follows the SMC manuscript's distinction between complexity and coherence under transformation [C1, Section 2.3]. An elaborate structure that cannot sustain its required relationships does not acquire the specified capability merely by being elaborate.

### 3.2 Realization shell

The term *realization shell* is introduced here as a bounded explanatory description of the operative organization through which a capability or experiential distinction becomes available to a particular system. It includes relevant state, dynamics, interfaces, support conditions, and temporally effective history. It is not merely a visual exterior or a cosmetic user interface.

For a chosen scale, a candidate model may be written:

$$
\Lambda_a=\langle Z_a,F_a,U_a,O_a,\mathcal R_a,\Gamma_a\rangle,
\tag{3}
$$

where $Z_a$ is the state domain; $F_a$ the transition law or relation; $U_a$ and $O_a$ the effective input and output channels; and $\mathcal R_a,\Gamma_a$ the support and boundary conditions. Relevant history can be represented in $Z_a$ only when its sufficiency is justified. Otherwise the model remains explicitly history-dependent.

Equation (3) defines a model format, not a complete account of any existing nervous system. The investigator's measurements of $Z_a$ are further objects and should not be confused with complete access to the underlying state. Nor does a stored instance of this format realize the activity it describes.

Different shells can support a common semantic task. Whether they support equivalent qualitative character is a separate question. The shell language relocates the realization inquiry without declaring either substrate independence or substrate exclusivity in advance.

### 3.3 Composition is not a bag of capabilities

Let $\mathcal K_1$ and $\mathcal K_2$ be capability contracts. Their proposed composition,

$$
\mathcal K_2\circ\mathcal K_1,
\tag{4}
$$

is partial. Output and input roles must align; timing and resource obligations must be jointly satisfiable; and side effects must not defeat required invariants. Successful isolated demonstrations do not guarantee simultaneous or sequential composition. The companion paper's complete-path requirement applies when the intermediate realizations are incompatible [Q1, Sections 9.7-9.9].

A further distinction concerns substitution. Replacing one constituent with an alternative implementation may preserve the high-level task while changing cost, timing, robustness, or phenomenal interpretation. The substitution contract must state which properties matter. It cannot infer complete equivalence from one preserved output label.

## 4. Formative Capacities Beneath Named Faculties

### 4.1 The compositional hypothesis

The discussion motivating this addendum proposed that operations contributing to an uncanny impression may also enter judgments about an encountered agent's condition, emotion, capability, or identity. The claim concerns overlapping formative capacities, not a dedicated detector with one ancestral purpose.

A candidate formulation is:

$$
\mathcal K_i=\operatorname{Compose}_{J_i}
(f_{i_1},\ldots,f_{i_n};\rho_i,\Gamma_i,\mathcal R_i),
\tag{5}
$$

where the $f_j$ are candidate operations, $\rho_i$ assigns their roles, and the remaining parameters specify constraints and support. Two higher-level capabilities may share some $f_j$ while differing in composition. The equation does not identify a fixed neural module for each symbol.

Possible operations include differentiating signals, tracking persistence, integrating cues over time, learning contingencies, assigning sources, comparing an encounter with expectations, and updating an attribution. These are provisional decomposition candidates. They are not a discovered minimal basis, and some may themselves require further composition. The Locus account already locates thinner operations such as difference, individuation, persistence, attribution, orientation, addressability, and recognition beneath more elaborate conceptual structures [L2, Section 7.4].

The SMC source expressly distinguishes shared representational organization from identical biological or computational hardware [C1, Section 6]. That distinction prevents an architectural resemblance from becoming an unsupported neuroanatomical claim.

### 4.2 Cross-species learning as a motivating case

The user's example of a dog learning human emotional signals motivates a particular question: how can an organism's existing capacities participate in learning the significance of signals produced by a differently embodied organism? Within the proposed model, learning could establish new bindings among recurring cues, contexts, interactions, and consequences without requiring the two species to present identical interfaces.

This is a research interpretation of the example, not a report of a study in this addendum. No particular animal's emotional understanding, phenomenal state, or neural decomposition is inferred. A dedicated empirical treatment would need appropriate primary evidence and controls for simpler cue-learning explanations.

The same distinction applies to the hominin example raised in discussion. Encounters among unfamiliar agents are conceivable settings in which reusable capacities could operate. This does not identify the evolutionary origin of those capacities, establish a species-specific detection mechanism, or imply that an observed modern response was selected for that setting. The addendum's argument does not depend on an evolutionary reconstruction.

### 4.3 What would support reuse?

A common vocabulary across tasks is weak evidence. A stronger account identifies a constituent independently, specifies how its role differs across compositions, and predicts a pattern of transfer or interference. A trained discrimination might improve selected tasks but not matched tasks that lack the proposed dependency. A controlled disruption in a computational model might selectively affect several compositions while sparing others.

Alternative explanations include general attention, motivation, response speed, shared task instructions, common data leakage, and overall difficulty. Matching these factors matters. Redundant implementations also complicate ablation: removing one pathway need not abolish a function that another pathway can support. A failed ablation prediction challenges the registered dependency model, not every possible account of formative reuse.

The useful scientific object is therefore a tested dependency structure with alternatives and limits, rather than an ever-expanding list of names for cognitive faculties.

## 5. The Observer's Attribution Is Another Realized Process

### 5.1 Target, interface, evidence, attribution

Consider a target $a$ and an observer $b$. A schematic model separates target dynamics from their presentation and interpretation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^a_{t+1}&=F_a(x^a_t,u^a_t;\lambda_a),\\
y^a_t&=G_a(x^a_t),\\
o^b_t&=H_{b}(y^a_t,v_t,c^b_t,\eta_t),\\
\widehat k_{b\to a,t}&=A_b(o^b_{\leq t},h^b_t,\theta_b).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

Here $v_t$ specifies presentation conditions; $c^b_t$ the observation context; $\eta_t$ measurement or transmission uncertainty; $h^b_t$ the observer's relevant history; and $\theta_b$ the observer's current interpretive model. $\widehat k$ is an attribution, such as judged responsiveness or experience, not a direct measurement of the target's phenomenal state.

This operationalizes SMC's observation that an evaluator encounters behavior rather than cognition directly [C1, Section 3.2]. It does not assert a strictly feedforward brain architecture. In live interaction, the observer acts back on the target, and both histories change.

The following objects must remain separate: target capability, target performance on a trial, evidence available to the observer, attributed capability, attributed experience, and the observer's own affective response. Their empirical relationships can be studied without turning one into the definition of every other.

### 5.2 Uncanniness as a possible output of composition

Within H-A, *uncanniness* names a candidate observer response arising from a configuration of recognition, expectation, discrepancy, and evaluation. It does not name an assumed faculty that directly measures consciousness. A different interface can invite different expectations even when a selected behavior trace is held fixed. Conversely, the same presentation can produce different judgments in observers with different interaction histories.

A perceived mismatch need not imply an unhealthy, unintelligent, or nonexperiencing target. It may concern the observer's model, an unfamiliar communicative pattern, missing context, or a presentation artifact. Nor is the absence of mismatch a certificate of actual competence. A well-designed presentation can make a limited system easier to interpret.

The proposed model can also study the observer's unease as an experiential event. That is a different target from studying the state of the entity toward which the unease is directed. An experience of uncanniness in the observer is not evidence that the target is only partially conscious.

### 5.3 Holding a controller fixed is not holding an encounter fixed

There is an important experimental constraint. If an interface changes how an observer behaves, the same controller may receive different inputs and follow a different trajectory. Controller identity therefore does not establish constant target behavior during a live encounter.

An open-loop presentation of a fixed trace can isolate a presentation effect more cleanly. A live interaction can instead estimate the total effect of presentation on the coupled encounter. These are different estimands. Reporting the latter as though the target trajectory were held fixed would obscure precisely the relational dynamics the framework seeks to explain.

This distinction also prevents a simplistic inference that every attribution change is merely superficial. A presentation change can first alter the observer and subsequently alter the interaction. Its initial target-side mechanism may be unchanged while its realized consequences become different.

## 6. Experiential Access Is Local to a Distinction and a Route

### 6.1 Access does not collapse into possession

A local capability limitation does not specify the overall phenomenal status of a person. The research question should identify which distinction is at issue, through which route it can become available, under which conditions, and whether the claim concerns a capacity or a current episode.

A proposed access record has the form:

$$
\mathcal A_{a,\delta,m,J,\mathcal I}
=\langle C,E,D,U,P,W\rangle,
\tag{7}
$$

where $a$ is the participant, $\delta$ the specified distinction, $m$ the access route, $J$ the scope, and $\mathcal I$ the interval. $C$ records evidence about standing capacity; $E$ current enabling conditions or exercise; $D$ discrimination performance; $U$ semantic uptake; $P$ the status of a phenomenal hypothesis; and $W$ the evidence and limitations. These fields are not numbers to be summed into a consciousness score.

For example, a task can concern distinguishing two selected samples visually under controlled presentation. A participant may lack that discrimination while being able to learn which sample belongs to a category through a reliable label or instrument. That supplies a different route of access to a fact, not an inference that the original visual distinction has become available in the same way.

The example is deliberately task-specific. It does not define a medical class, assume that all forms of color-vision difference remove the same distinctions, or diagnose a person's experience. The conceptual distinction requires no such generalization.

### 6.2 Discrimination, knowing, and feeling

Three questions are especially easy to collapse:

1. Can the system discriminate the selected alternatives under this procedure?
2. Can it understand or use the proposition that a particular alternative obtains?
3. Is that distinction present in a particular phenomenal episode?

A discrimination result is operational evidence. Semantic uptake concerns the role of a distinction in inference, expectation, or action. A phenomenal interpretation remains a further hypothesis with its own limits. A report of noticing may provide relevant evidence without being an infallible instrument.

The source framework's multidimensional grounding supports this separation. Perceptual, practical, inferential, social, institutional, historical, normative, and autobiographical grounding are distinguished rather than collapsed into a single binary [L2, Section 17.4]. This addendum applies that distinction to access routes; it does not infer that every route realizes the same phenomenal content.

### 6.3 A bounded interpretation of "less conscious of"

The motivating formulation, *less conscious in this way*, is interpreted here as reduced or unavailable access to a specified distinction, not a quantity of consciousness possessed by the whole person. The qualifier carries the scientific content.

A capability profile is also not the same as a current experiential state. A participant may be capable of noticing a distinction while not attending to it now. Conversely, a recorded response does not establish that the participant can reproduce the distinction reliably across conditions.

ACO's rejection of a single global scalar is relevant, but its six regime axes are not sensory-access measurements [C2, Section 5]. Equation (7) is a new local research descriptor, not a redefinition of those axes. No global personhood, welfare, or phenomenal ranking follows from it.

## 7. What Does "Your Red Is My Red" Ask?

### 7.1 A family of comparisons

The phrase admits several noninterchangeable questions:

- **Comparison:** Same reference  
  **Possible evidence:** Coordinated identification of a sample or event  
  **What it does not settle alone:** Same category boundaries or experienced character

- **Comparison:** Same category commitment  
  **Possible evidence:** Consistent application across positive and contrast cases  
  **What it does not settle alone:** Identical discrimination capacities

- **Comparison:** Corresponding discrimination  
  **Possible evidence:** Calibrated performance across a declared stimulus family  
  **What it does not settle alone:** Identical phenomenal character

- **Comparison:** Corresponding practical or inferential role  
  **Possible evidence:** Preserved consequences under selected transformations  
  **What it does not settle alone:** Complete semantic identity outside that scope

- **Comparison:** Similar reported experience  
  **Possible evidence:** Independently elicited and compared descriptions  
  **What it does not settle alone:** Direct equality of private experiences

- **Comparison:** Phenomenal equivalence  
  **Possible evidence:** A separately justified theory and evidential bridge  
  **What it does not settle alone:** Not established by the preceding rows by definition

Jointly pointing to a sample can ground reference. It does not determine which property is being picked out or guarantee stable generalization. Two observers could agree on a familiar sample while using different rules on less familiar cases. The relevant comparison therefore includes contrasts and consequences, not only one coinciding word.

Nevertheless, uncertainty about the final row need not invalidate every earlier row. A shared semantic practice can be well grounded without first proving that all its realizations have identical felt character. This is the practical force of separating semantic correspondence from phenomenal equivalence [L2, Section 5.2; Q1, Sections 8.2 and 11.5].

### 7.2 Comparison contracts and partial projections

Let a comparison contract be:

$$
K=\langle J,I_K,r_K,\mathcal T_K,\mathcal O_K,\epsilon_K\rangle,
\tag{8}
$$

where $J$ specifies the domain and conditions, $I_K$ the required semantic commitments, $r_K$ the descriptive resolution, $\mathcal T_K$ the proposed transformations, $\mathcal O_K$ the observation procedures, and $\epsilon_K$ any declared tolerance rule. The contract states what kind of sameness is being investigated; it does not choose the result.

For each participant, define a proposed partial semantic projection:

$$
\pi_{a,K}:\mathcal E_{a,J}\dashrightarrow\mathcal M_K.
\tag{9}
$$

$\mathcal E_{a,J}$ denotes the relevant episode domain; $\mathcal M_K$ the common comparison vocabulary or structure. The dashed arrow allows non-resolution. An episode need not fit the current representation, and missing evidence can block a warranted assignment.

Where both maps are defined, a proposed exact correspondence is:

$$
\pi_{a,K}(e_a)=\pi_{b,K}(e_b).
\tag{10}
$$

This is equality in the chosen comparison structure. It is not equality of episode tokens, physical states, whole meanings, or phenomenal character. In practice, researchers estimate projections from evidence records, $\widehat\pi_{a,K}(D_{e_a})$, rather than accessing an episode in its entirety. A measured match must carry those estimation limits.

Equation (10) is a local construction implementing the Locus invariant principle, not a claim that the source's informal invariant predicates alone automatically provide every mathematical equivalence axiom. Exact equality of defined projections gives an equivalence relation on the common tagged domain. Approximate matching under a tolerance need not be transitive [Q1, Section 11.5].

### 7.3 Resolution-relative sameness

Suppose a finer contract $K_f$ and a coarser contract $K_c$ are related by a declared forgetting map $g$:

$$
\pi_{a,K_c}=g\circ\pi_{a,K_f}.
\tag{11}
$$

Then exact fine correspondence implies exact coarse correspondence, on their common domain. The converse need not hold. Two records can agree on `red category` while differing in a separately described saturation contrast, association, or confidence. No contradiction arises if the contracts genuinely ask different questions.

The relation in (11) must be established rather than inferred from the adjectives *fine* and *coarse*. A newly introduced schema can change the question instead of merely refine it. A chart that drops a distinction cannot later claim it was preserved because the remaining labels still agree.

One may call the preimage $\pi_K^{-1}(m)$ a realization fiber for the comparison. Here *fiber* means only a set-theoretic preimage. No smooth bundle, unique reconstruction, or physical sameness is implied. The terminology locates which differences a projection does not resolve; it does not declare those differences irrelevant to every purpose.

### 7.4 The residual phenomenal question

The inverted-spectrum question is not eliminated by equation (10). It is narrowed: after the declared semantic and functional correspondences are established, what further organization would have to correspond for the experienced character to be the same?

The companion H-P hypothesis proposes that sufficiently relevant realized binding organization constitutes qualitative specificity [Q1, Section 4.1]. This addendum neither proves that thesis nor rejects it. It distinguishes the mapping that establishes a shared semantic target from the additional realization account required to settle phenomenal equivalence. "We both say red" is not such an account. "We can compare nothing until phenomenal identity is proved" is equally unnecessary for the bounded tasks already specified.

## 8. Objectivity Across Standpoints, Subjectivity in Situated Occurrence

### 8.1 An explanatory distinction, not two substances

The phrase *objectivity in semantics and subjectivity in realization* expresses a useful initial intuition, but becomes misleading if read as a rigid partition. Standpoint-specific associations are themselves semantic bindings. Realizations have properties that can be investigated publicly. The distinction concerns explanatory roles rather than separate substances or impermeable regions.

The proposed formulation is:

> Objectivity concerns relations whose validity remains warranted across specified changes of standpoint; subjectivity concerns the situated occurrence through which relations and qualities are encountered.

A person can participate in a shared distinction through an individual realization. The reality of that perspective does not make every judgment true, and the existence of public constraints does not make the individual episode interchangeable with another.

This position follows the Locus framework's constraint realism: interpretation participates in meaning while remaining answerable to worldly conditions, other agents, and consequences [L1, Sections 2 and 12; L2, Section 15]. It is a proposed extension of that account to intersubjective comparison, not a derivation of all scientific objectivity from agreement.

### 8.2 Invariance is insufficient without adequacy

A constant classifier is invariant under every change of input. It does not thereby track the relevant distinction. Likewise, two observers can apply the same mistaken rule and agree reliably. Agreement and invariance must therefore be supplemented by differential sensitivity, independent grounding, appropriate consequence tests, and explicitly bounded claims.

The semantic threshold already requires that some counterfactual changes make a difference [L2, Section 4.4]. The truth distinction separately requires that a claim answer to what it describes [L2, Sections 15.1-15.2]. Neither condition can be replaced by a count of agreeing observers.

A research comparison should therefore record at least two assessments: whether the participants' commitments correspond under $K$, and whether those commitments are supported by the relevant independent evidence. The second assessment may be unavailable in an autobiographical domain or incomplete in an empirical one. Correspondence must then be reported as correspondence, not silently upgraded to truth.

### 8.3 Objectivity can include a true account of difference

If one participant reports a homecoming association and another reports a farewell association, the statements can be jointly represented without contradiction when their subjects, episodes, and evidential status remain explicit. The common account need not assert that the music intrinsically possesses both meanings for everyone.

The shared claims might be:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Reports}(a,\operatorname{Association}(e_a,\mathrm{homecoming})),\\
\operatorname{Reports}(b,\operatorname{Association}(e_b,\mathrm{farewell})).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$

These are initially report-level claims. Further evidence or participant correction may strengthen, qualify, or revise the associated interpretation. Agreement that the reports occurred is not proof of a uniquely identified private state.

The significant point is that the common semantic structure can represent asymmetry. Objectivity need not mean deleting every index until only an impersonal statement remains. Sometimes the accurate common statement is precisely that two situated relations differ.

## 9. Perspective Bridges That Preserve Differences

### 9.1 Beyond the common intersection

A simple overlap model asks what both participants already possess. A perspective bridge asks more: which commitments match, which belong to only one standpoint, which can be translated, how they differ, and what remains unresolved.

Define a proposed bridge record:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P_{ab,K}= \langle &C_{ab}, L_a, L_b, \Delta_{ab}, T_{ab}, T_{ba}, U_{ab}, \\
&\Pi_{ab}\rangle
\end{aligned}\tag{13}.
$$

$C_{ab}$ records warranted common commitments; $L_a,L_b$ standpoint-specific claims; $\Delta_{ab}$ represented differences; $T_{ab},T_{ba}$ directional translation procedures; $U_{ab}$ unresolved or unavailable content; and $\Pi_{ab}$ provenance, scope, and revision history. A component can remain empty or unknown without forcing total bridge failure.

Equation (13) applies the source's co-projection requirements [L2, Section 8.5]. It is not a meet or join unless an appropriate order and the relevant existence conditions have been supplied. A shared binding core also need not be the largest possible one; the evidence may support only a bounded subset.

### 9.2 Differences as first-class relations

A difference record should identify its objects, aspect, direction, and evidence. "We differ" is underdetermined. It could mean different input access, different discrimination, different category use, different appraisal, or different desired continuation. Those are not substitutes.

A minimal typed difference is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{ab}= \langle &a, b, e_a, e_b, \mathrm{aspect}, v_a, v_b, K, W\rangle
\end{aligned}\tag{14}.
$$

The values may be categories, judgments, distributions, or unresolved alternatives rather than numerical coordinates. The record should not infer an internal causal explanation merely because the difference is expressible. A participant's account of why an association arose remains distinguishable from an independently tested explanation.

Source indexing is essential. Exchanging the owner of an association changes the meaning. Replacing both associations with a compromise label can destroy the difference the bridge exists to preserve. A representation may be coherent precisely because it refuses that collapse.

### 9.3 Directionality and imperfect reversibility

Understanding another person's description can require information not needed for them to understand ours. Consequently, $T_{ab}$ need not have an inverse, and the two directions need not have equal loss or coverage. A concise translation may preserve a practical instruction while losing autobiographical significance.

A round trip can be tested for named invariants without demanding full recovery:

$$
\operatorname{Inv}_K\bigl(T_{ba}(T_{ab}(d_a))\bigr)=\operatorname{Inv}_K(d_a),
\tag{15}
$$

where the maps are defined and $\operatorname{Inv}_K$ extracts the properties that the contract requires to survive. The full source object can still differ after the round trip.

Across more than two participants, pairwise bridges do not guarantee one compatible global account. A translation can preserve different properties on different paths. The source framework already permits failed joins [L2, Sections 8.3-8.4]; the companion paper requires compatible complete realization paths rather than merely separate edge witnesses [Q1, Section 9]. A collective perspective registry should retain those limits instead of using transitive closure to manufacture consensus.

### 9.4 Non-resolution is part of the account

`WITHHELD`, `NOT_MEASURED`, `UNMAPPED_BY_SCHEMA`, `UNCERTAIN`,  
and `DISPUTED` record different relations to evidence. None means that no experience occurred. A person may also decline to make a comparison that the researcher finds interesting.

The current technical brief already preserves original descriptions and typed missingness [Q2, Sections 5.3 and 5.7]. The present extension is interpersonal: a bridge can faithfully preserve a boundary rather than treating every remaining private aspect as a defect to eliminate. The conversation worked example likewise distinguishes a refused path from empty data [W1, "Admissibility and Refusal"].

## 10. Shared Encounters as Higher-Order Binding

### 10.1 Several forms of sharing

The expression *shared experience* should not be forced into a single threshold. The following forms are useful to distinguish:

- **Form:** Common exposure  
  **Minimal object of analysis:** Participants encounter a common event or related presentation  
  **Additional question:** Did either know about the other's encounter?

- **Form:** Corresponding interpretation  
  **Minimal object of analysis:** Some commitments match under a contract  
  **Additional question:** Were the correspondences communicated or recognized?

- **Form:** Coordinated participation  
  **Minimal object of analysis:** Their actions enter a jointly organized activity  
  **Additional question:** What causal contribution does the interaction make?

- **Form:** Mutual acknowledgment  
  **Minimal object of analysis:** Each has evidence that the other participates  
  **Additional question:** Which level and content of recognition are supported?

- **Form:** Transformative sharing  
  **Minimal object of analysis:** Participation changes later attention, interpretation, or continuation  
  **Additional question:** Does the change exceed common exposure or one-way information alone?

These are not a developmental ladder or an ordering of consciousness. An encounter can combine some forms and not others. A later conversation can establish sharing across initially independent episodes. A one-way narrative can create an important relation without becoming reciprocal interaction with its author.

### 10.2 The shared binding

For a bounded encounter, propose:

$$
\mathcal S_{ab}=\operatorname{Bind}_J
(a,b,e_a,e_b,o,H_{ab},\Gamma_{ab}),
\tag{16}
$$

where $o$ is a common referent or event where applicable, $H_{ab}$ the interaction history, and $\Gamma_{ab}$ the relevant relational constraints. The expression identifies a candidate higher-order organization: already constituted episodes and their participants enter further relations. It applies recursive Meta-Binding rather than inventing a new fundamental operation [L2, Section 6].

Two boundaries are indispensable. First, equation (16) is not evidence that the binding occurred. A researcher can write it for two unrelated records. Evidence of actual exposure, communication, or uptake must justify the particular sharing claim. Second, the expression does not identify a third phenomenal subject. A coordinated system can be a useful unit of explanation without a demonstrated group experience.

An interaction can also be partial, conflicted, unpleasant, or strategically misleading. The existence of a relation does not establish its fidelity, benevolence, or legitimacy. The source distinction between binding presence and binding quality applies here, but SBQ must not be converted into a measure of moral worth or phenomenal intensity [B1, Sections 2-3].

### 10.3 The second-person turn

The transition from recognizing matching content to recognizing another standpoint can be stated as follows:

> The other is not merely a second instance of my answer. The other is a participant whose response can change what I am warranted in saying about this encounter.

The source account of *you* supplies the conceptual basis: address binds recognition, distinction, possible response, expectation, concern, and relation [L2, Section 13.5]. The new application is to experiential comparison. A partner can confirm a proposed correspondence, qualify it, correct it, introduce an unanticipated dimension, or decline further disclosure.

This does not make a report infallible. It makes the person's response part of the evidence and interaction, rather than treating the investigator's reconstruction as complete by default. The addendum separates two questions: how accurate an account is, and whether the procedure permits the affected participant to contest or limit that account.

Addressability itself does not require a present reply. The source expressly permits standing addressive relationships to absent or nonresponsive participants [L2, Section 13.5]. Accordingly, the lack of a current response must not be coded as absence of personhood or incapacity for any shared relationship. A stronger claim of presently reciprocal uptake simply needs evidence that the standing relation alone cannot supply.

### 10.4 Recognition without infinite recursion

A proposed *mutually acknowledged encounter* may include evidence that $a$ recognizes $b$'s participation and $b$ recognizes $a$'s. This need not be formalized as an infinite hierarchy in which each knows that the other knows at every level. A bounded operational contract can specify the acknowledgments needed for a task.

Nor must recognition be verbal. In a particular study, coordinated action, correction, or an agreed response procedure could supply evidence. Such evidence remains fallible and task-dependent. The analysis should not infer a rich model of another mind from one matching gesture.

## 11. Interaction Changes the Continuation Problem

### 11.1 Common causes and contingent exchange

A schematic interactive model is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^a_{t+1}&=F_a(x^a_t,u_t^a,c_t,m_t^{b\to a}),\\
x^b_{t+1}&=F_b(x^b_t,u_t^b,c_t,m_t^{a\to b}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$

The shared input $c_t$ can generate correlated responses even if the message terms have no effect. Contingent interaction requires more than observing temporal alignment: the other participant's actual activity must enter the relevant causal organization, under a declared model and measurement procedure.

Conversely, a useful exchange can be asymmetric or delayed. Turn-taking may require complementary rather than matching actions. A study should therefore avoid treating synchronous output as either necessary or sufficient for the higher-order sharing claim.

For H-I, the research target is an independently predicted change attributable to contingent exchange. A live-versus-replay comparison is one candidate design, but replay can alter expectations, information, timing, or perceived social presence. Those differences must be measured or acknowledged; the contrast is not automatically a pure intervention on reciprocity.

### 11.2 Joint feasibility is not marginal feasibility

Dynatology defines coordination through jointly admissible continuation rather than mere overlap of futures [D1, Section 17]. For this application, a candidate joint state can be:

$$
z_t=(x_t^a,x_t^b,h_t,r_t),
\tag{18}
$$

where $h_t$ contains needed interaction history and $r_t$ shared resource or environmental state. A joint transition must satisfy the participants' relevant dynamics and coupling constraints:

$$
A_{ab}(z_t,z_{t+1})=
A_a\land A_b\land C_{ab},
\tag{19}
$$

with the arguments of each predicate specified in an implementation. Equation (19) is a proposed bounded model, not a claim that arbitrary physical interaction factorizes cleanly into independent agents plus one coupling term.

Two people may each be able to attend to an event, yet lack a jointly feasible route to a particular coordinated performance under the same timing or resource constraints. Two report sequences may each be individually possible without being jointly compatible with the actual interaction history. The parent complete-path principle therefore extends naturally to intersubjective studies: preserve a compatible witness through the whole coupled sequence [Q1, Sections 9.7-9.9 and 11.6].

Physical feasibility, informational availability, and authorization remain separate questions. Their conjunction can govern execution, but a failure of authorization must not be reported as a discovery of physical impossibility [D1, Section 8; Q2, Sections 4 and 9].

### 11.3 What interaction can add without merging experiencers

In the melody example, one participant's explanation can make an association available to the other. The latter may subsequently attend to the passage differently. That is a candidate effect on a continuing trajectory, not evidence that the original episodes have retrospectively become identical.

The additional shared content can include a relationship between differences: which feature is common, which histories supply divergent significance, and how each participant now understands the contrast. Interaction can thus modify the future accessibility of meanings without requiring the participants to exchange or merge their first-person occurrences.

The converse also matters. A conversation can misunderstand, distort, or impose an interpretation. An interactional model must retain those possibilities. The proposal predicts conditional benefits of particular difference-preserving procedures, not that social exchange automatically improves understanding.

## 12. Understanding Without Convergence

### 12.1 Correspondence is a different objective

A listener can initially assume that a partner's experience resembles their own. After discussion, the listener may discover that the resemblance was limited. Apparent agreement can decrease while the listener's account becomes more adequate.

The target of understanding is therefore not simply the distance between the participants' own positions. It includes how well each represents the other's distinctions, commitments, uncertainty, and boundaries. Agreement is one possible relation between positions. Accuracy about that relation is another.

This suggests separating:

$$
\operatorname{Agree}_K(a,b),\qquad
\operatorname{Correspond}_K(\widehat B_{a\to b},B_b),
\tag{20}
$$

where $B_b$ is the relevant aspect of $b$'s organization or commitments and $\widehat B_{a\to b}$ is $a$'s model of it. In empirical work, $B_b$ is not directly available in full; the comparison must use independently elicited responses, consequences, correction opportunities, and other appropriate evidence. The notation must not disguise that limitation.

H-U proposes that a difference-preserving representation can improve bounded correspondence. It does not define understanding as total reconstruction of another person. Nor does it require agreement, identification, endorsement, or access to everything the partner prefers not to disclose.

### 12.2 Prediction is one witness, not the whole concept

Predicting a partner's responses on held-out questions can be a useful test. It should be complemented by structural fidelity: can the account preserve which distinctions belong to the partner, which are inferred, which were corrected, and where the evidence runs out?

A predictor can succeed through irrelevant regularities. A partner can also change their mind during interaction. The target and prediction horizon must therefore be explicit. A post-conversation prediction of future behavior is not a retrospective measurement of what the partner experienced before the conversation.

A reliable model can furthermore be used without care or reciprocity. The ability to predict another does not establish concern for them, permission to act on them, or the ethical quality of the relationship. Those are separately typed questions. The second-person emphasis adds responsiveness to correction and boundary, not a claim that every accurate predictor is empathic.

### 12.3 A limited operational measure

For a bounded binary response, let $p$ be the partner's stipulated response probability and $q$ the observer's prediction. The expected squared probability error is:

$$
L(q;p)=p(1-q)^2+(1-p)q^2
=p(1-p)+(q-p)^2.
\tag{21}
$$

The second term measures error beyond the partner's intrinsic response variability in this stipulated model. Its minimum occurs at $q=p$. This is an illustrative proper prediction criterion, not a measure of consciousness, empathy, or complete interpersonal understanding.

Agreement between two independently sampled responses with probabilities $p_a$ and $p_b$ is instead:

$$
G(p_a,p_b)=p_ap_b+(1-p_a)(1-p_b).
\tag{22}
$$

The independence assumption belongs to this toy calculation and is not assumed for real interacting participants. Equations (21) and (22) nevertheless make the logical distinction inspectable: prediction accuracy about the partner and similarity of the participants' own responses are different quantities.

### 12.4 Corrections are events, not overwritten errors

Suppose the listener attributes a farewell association to the wrong participant. A correction should create a new interpretation record linked to the original, not silently rewrite the history so that the mistake disappears. The correction may reveal a local attribution error while preserving the common musical recognition.

This extends the source framework's provenance and amendment discipline to interpersonal comparison. The original report, the interpretation, the partner's correction, and the revised bridge are different objects [L2, Sections 15-16; Q2, Sections 5.6-5.7]. A disagreement about the revised bridge should remain representable. Recognition does not require granting the analyst, the listener, or the reporting participant infallibility over every question.

## 13. Two Worked Extensions: Appraisal and Continuity

### 13.1 Disliking an event while valuing its discovery

Consider a hypothetical participant who finds a novel flavor unpleasant but values learning how it affects them. There is no contradiction if the evaluations have different targets:

$$
\operatorname{Valence}(a,e,\mathrm{sensory})<0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Appraisal}(a,\operatorname{LearnFrom}(e),\mathrm{discovery})>0.
\tag{23}
$$

The signs indicate stipulated opposing evaluations, not measurements. The example shows why a single undifferentiated valence field can lose structure. An experiential episode can enter a higher-order evaluation, and that evaluation can influence whether the participant continues.

The resulting account should not require every sensation to include reflective self-assessment. Recursive and reflexive binding are optional additional organizations, not definitional prerequisites for all phenomenal occurrence [L2, Sections 6 and 10; Q1, Section 5.4]. Nor does the example establish that sensory unpleasantness and reflective value are independently controllable. They may interact.

The connection to shared experience is direct. Two people may match on sensory appraisal while differing on the value of the encounter, or differ on sensory appraisal while valuing the joint discovery. A bridge must identify the target and level of each comparison rather than forcing one verdict about whether they "liked the same experience."

### 13.2 Preserving facts versus preserving their operative role

Consider a hypothetical transfer of an artificial participant's memory records. A list of facts can be preserved while the relationships that make those facts available, salient, or consequential change. Conversely, a changed encoding can preserve a declared functional role. The relevant claim therefore requires a preservation contract rather than a visual comparison of files.

This follows the existing distinction among standing structures, actual realization, and constitutional invariants [L2, Section 5.4]. The companion path analysis adds that future continuity can depend on state not visible in a current coarse description [Q1, Sections 9.7-9.9].

Nothing here proves that experiential organization is inherently uncopyable or that one substrate uniquely supports a person. It states an obligation: identify what must be preserved and test the corresponding continuations. A copied record is neither automatically a copied experience nor automatically incapable of contributing to one.

The same issue arises in accounts of shared events. A transcript preserves selected utterances. It does not by itself preserve all timing, context, attention, or relational effects of the encounter. A later reader can enter a meaningful relationship with the record without thereby reproducing the original participation.

## 14. Limited Formal Consequences

The following propositions concern stipulated models and their stated assumptions. They are elementary consequences offered to discipline the research architecture, not new mathematical results about consciousness.

### 14.1 Proposition 1: Coarse correspondence does not entail fine correspondence

Assume that a common fine projection $\pi_f$ and coarse projection $\pi_c$ are defined on a tagged episode domain and satisfy $\pi_c=g\circ\pi_f$. Then:

$$
\pi_f(e_1)=\pi_f(e_2)\Longrightarrow\pi_c(e_1)=\pi_c(e_2).
\tag{24}
$$

The converse fails when $g$ is noninjective on the relevant image.

**Proof.** Apply $g$ to equal fine outputs to obtain equal coarse outputs. For the converse, choose distinct $x,y$ in the image with $g(x)=g(y)$ and episodes realizing those outputs. They are coarsely equal but finely distinct. $\square$

The result locates the scope of a sameness claim. It does not determine whether either projection captures a phenomenal difference.

### 14.2 Proposition 2: Invariance and agreement do not entail truth

There exists a classifier that is invariant across a selected transformation family and agreed upon by every modeled observer, yet fails to discriminate the task's independently stipulated categories.

**Proof.** Let the task contain at least two distinct target categories, and let every observer use the constant function returning only the first. The classifier is invariant and observers agree, but it is incorrect on every instance of the other category. $\square$

A nonconstant shared classifier can also be wrong, for example by consistently reversing a fixed category assignment. Differential sensitivity is necessary to avoid the trivial constant case but remains insufficient for truth. Independent adequacy checks are still required.

### 14.3 Proposition 3: A shared description can preserve opposed local appraisals

Let $v_a\neq v_b$ be appraisals assigned to distinct subject-indexed terms $A(a,e_a)$ and $A(b,e_b)$. The conjunction

$$
A(a,e_a)=v_a\ \land\ A(b,e_b)=v_b
\tag{25}
$$

is satisfiable unless additional constraints require those terms to be equal.

**Proof.** Interpret the two terms as distinct function applications and assign their stated values. Both clauses hold. No contradiction follows merely from the difference in values. $\square$

Thus an integrated account can contain a difference without collapsing it. The proposition does not establish that the assignments are true of actual participants; that requires evidence. Subject indexing also does not dissolve genuine disagreements about one common proposition. "The same sample meets the declared threshold" and its negation cannot both be asserted as true under the same conditions merely by attaching different speaker labels. What can be jointly true is that the speakers hold opposed positions.

### 14.4 Proposition 4: Perfect alignment does not imply reciprocal causation

Let a shared input $c_t$ determine both outputs:

$$
y^a_t=c_t,\qquad y^b_t=c_t,
\tag{26}
$$

with neither output entering the other's update. The outputs coincide at every time, yet an intervention on $y^a_t$ alone leaves $y^b_t$ unchanged under the model, and conversely.

**Proof.** Both output equations refer only to $c_t$. Replacing either equation does not replace the other or alter $c_t$. $\square$

The result rules out inference from synchrony alone to reciprocal influence. It does not rule out common exposure as a meaningful relation, nor make reciprocal causation sufficient for phenomenal sharing.

### 14.5 Proposition 5: Better partner prediction can coexist with lower agreement

Under equations (21)-(22), fix $p_b=0.1$. Let an observer's prediction improve from $q_0=0.9$ to $q_1=0.1$. Its expected error falls from $0.73$ to $0.09$. Independently, let its own response probability change from $p_a=0.5$ to $p'_a=0.9$. Expected response agreement with the partner falls from $0.50$ to $0.18$.

**Proof.** Substitute the stipulated values into (21) and (22). The changes satisfy both inequalities. $\square$

This is an existence example, not a prediction that learning about another normally increases disagreement. Holding $p_a$ fixed would also permit improved prediction without any change in agreement. Neither case identifies full understanding with a binary prediction score.

### 14.6 Corollary: No scalar agreement measure can recover all bridge content

A number counting matching judgments cannot, by itself, determine who holds each nonmatching judgment, which differences are understood, whether a correction was incorporated, or what was withheld. Distinct perspective bridges can have the same agreement count while differing on all of those properties.

A scalar can summarize a declared task, but it cannot replace the typed record. This is a representational limitation, not an argument against quantitative research.

## 15. Prospective Research Program

The following studies are proposals. All empirical outcomes remain **NOT_RUN**. They extend the parent program without creating a new route from behavioral success to certified consciousness.

### 15.1 Study F: Shared formative operations

Select two or more tasks hypothesized to reuse a particular operation, alongside matched tasks without that proposed dependency. Independently operationalize the candidate constituent before confirmatory testing. Compare targeted training or an appropriate authorized perturbation with control interventions.

The primary prediction is a specified transfer or interference pattern, not a general performance improvement. Competing models should include separate-task mechanisms, a generic resource factor, and a shared-operation model with comparable information and parameter budgets. A composition model earns support through held-out task variants and role-sensitive effects.

A positive result would support the registered reuse claim within scope. It would not establish a universal cognitive basis or the evolutionary history of any detector. A failure should lead to a recorded revision or rejection of the proposed dependency rather than an unbounded search for hidden components.

### 15.2 Study A: Target capability versus observer attribution

Use independently evaluated target behavior and vary presentation, observer information, or familiarization. The open-loop track presents fixed traces through different interfaces. The interactive track permits observer behavior to affect the target and analyzes that coupled process explicitly.

Measure actual task performance, attributed competence, attributed experience, confidence, and unease separately. The study must not assume that its experience-attribution labels are correct. Accuracy can be evaluated for independently known task capacities; phenomenal attribution may have no validated ground truth in the design.

A presentation effect on attributed experience would establish an attribution dependency, not a change in target experience. In the interactive track, effects on subsequent target behavior should be recorded rather than hidden under the phrase *same controller*. Demand characteristics, anthropomorphic wording, and prior familiarity are competing explanations to measure or control.

### 15.3 Study X: Alternative access and semantic participation

Use a bounded, nonclinical task in which one route to a distinction is available, degraded, or replaced by another informational route. Keep source information, category rules, task instructions, and reporting requirements explicit. The purpose is not to diagnose a sensory condition but to test which forms of participation survive a change in access route.

Outcomes include discrimination, application of category consequences, reportability, confidence, and generalization to new cases. Semantic success through an alternate channel need not imply restored performance on the original sensory task. Reports of experienced change remain a separate outcome.

A null result is possible: the alternative route may fail to supply the information or training the task requires. A positive result supports route-specific access and transfer for that task, not identical phenomenology across routes.

### 15.4 Study I: Common exposure versus contingent sharing

Compare independent common exposure, one-way exchange, matched replay, and live contingent interaction where feasible. Define the target property before data collection: for example, incorporation of a partner's correction into later judgments, recognition of a partner-specific distinction, or adaptation to that partner's actual response.

A live-versus-replay contrast alone is insufficiently specific if the conditions differ in timing, content, expectations, or task difficulty. Designs should preserve or measure those differences and distinguish the total effect of live participation from a narrowly isolated contingent component.

Similarity of time series is not the primary criterion. A preregistered model should predict how altering a particular communicative contribution changes later behavior while common input is accounted for. Dyads and sessions, rather than every utterance as an independent observation, should determine the appropriate dependence structure in evaluation.

A supported interaction effect would concern the tested behavioral or representational organization. It would not establish identical qualia or a collective phenomenal subject.

### 15.5 Study U: Difference-preserving understanding

Compare three modeling procedures: an agreement-focused summary, a similarity-focused summary, and a perspective bridge that records common commitments, local claims, explicit differences, corrections, and unresolved content. Control for information access, time, prompting, and representational budget. More fields alone must not give the preferred model an unacknowledged advantage.

Before interaction, elicit each participant's own bounded judgments and predictions about the partner. After interaction, repeat those measures on held-out cases and collect structured corrections where appropriate. Distinguish prediction accuracy, actual agreement, believed agreement, attribution fidelity, uncertainty calibration, and participant assessment of whether the account represents them.

No one measure is decisive. A participant's approval can reflect agreement-seeking; a predictive model can exploit irrelevant cues; a detailed bridge can become burdensome or intrusive. The hypothesis gains support if its specified structure improves held-out correspondence and reduces particular attribution errors beyond information-matched alternatives, without requiring convergence of the participants' own positions.

### 15.6 Cross-cutting controls and failure conditions

All studies require frozen comparison contracts, explicit input availability, independent calibration, group-aware holdouts, and uncertainty propagation. Model selection must not use the final test cases. Changes to a bridge or dimension after a failure create a successor hypothesis rather than retroactively repairing the original prediction [Q2, Sections 11-12].

Important negative controls include common-input synchronization without interaction, constant agreement without discrimination, highly predictive opaque identifiers, rich descriptions with reversed participant attribution, and a model that refuses every comparison. The last control matters because abstention can prevent false claims while making the system useless. Both coverage and error within covered cases must be reported.

Results that would challenge specific addendum hypotheses include no selective cross-task transfer, attribution effects disappearing under presentation controls, alternate-route gains reducible to leakage, live-interaction gains explained entirely by unmatched information, and no advantage for difference-preserving representations over simpler contextual models. These are meaningful negative findings, not failures to appreciate the framework.

## 16. Objections, Boundaries, and Interpretive Risks

### 16.1 Is this ordinary perspective-taking in new vocabulary?

It can become that if the representation merely renames familiar observations. The proposed incremental value lies in explicitly typed comparisons, partial directional bridges, registered invariants, and tests that distinguish accurate correspondence from agreement. The novelty claim is the integration and its contracts, not discovery that people have perspectives.

The source corpus already acknowledges interactional and relational precedents [L1, Section 3; L2, Section 3]. A serious empirical or philosophical submission must engage those traditions directly. This addendum supplies a research articulation, not a completed priority assessment.

### 16.2 Does shared semantics make experience public without remainder?

No. A common comparison can leave many differences unresolved. The model does not require that every aspect of an experience be expressible in the available vocabulary, much less exhaustively measurable. It does require that any asserted correspondence state what evidence supports it.

Conversely, an unmeasured remainder is not automatically ineffable in principle.   
`UNMAPPED_BY_CURRENT_SCHEMA` must remain different from a justified claim of permanent inaccessibility. The latter would require an argument not supplied by a missing field.

### 16.3 Does subject indexing permit every disagreement to be reconciled?

No. It permits a shared account of who makes which claim. It does not make incompatible claims about one common state jointly true. A bridge can preserve an unresolved contradiction or conclude that one attributed claim is not supported. Difference preservation is not blanket relativism.

A jointly accepted account can also be false. World-constrained adequacy remains separate from the social fact that participants accept a record [L2, Section 15].

### 16.4 Does recognizing someone constitute their consciousness?

No such implication is assumed. Recognition is an event in the observer and, in interaction, a possible contributor to a relationship. It does not create or abolish the target's phenomenal status by fiat. Likewise, failure to recognize a capability is not evidence that the capability cannot exist.

For ordinary human examples, separate persons are treated as experiential participants. For artificial systems, the platform may establish task competence, responsiveness, or attribution patterns while leaving phenomenal status unresolved. The same evidential ceiling applies whether an interface looks human or plainly mechanical.

### 16.5 Does the framework require explicit concepts for basic experience?

No. Semantic access in this research vocabulary is broader than possession of a word, and higher-order reflection is not made a prerequisite for every sensation. The distinction between discrimination, semantic uptake, and phenomenal constitution remains intact. A richly labeled model is not necessarily closer to the actual mechanism than a well-specified nonlinguistic one.

The formative-capability account must similarly avoid turning each analytically convenient label into a brain module. Dependencies have to survive independent tests and comparisons with alternative decompositions.

### 16.6 Does the shared binding imply a group mind?

Not by itself. A relational event can have properties absent from either participant considered in isolation, such as a jointly completed correction or coordinated action. That does not establish a unified phenomenal subject. The addendum supplies no threshold for assigning group consciousness.

Neither does it prohibit such hypotheses in principle. It requires them to specify additional claims and evidence rather than infer subject unity from a graph containing several agents.

### 16.7 Can understanding become extractive?

Yes as a design possibility: a system can model another accurately while disregarding their preferences or disclosure boundaries. Therefore fidelity, care, consent, authority, and performance must not be merged. The technical design should preserve access restrictions and participant corrections rather than treat maximum disclosure as the definition of success.

A record of withheld content should normally store only the authorized fact of non-disclosure and its scope, not an inferred replacement value. The conversational worked example already treats a declined path as meaningful [W1, "Admissibility and Refusal"]. Preserving that boundary is a proposed ethical and engineering requirement here, not a claim that refusal reveals a particular hidden emotion.

### 16.8 What remains unresolved?

The addendum does not specify the complete causal organization of any human experience, establish a universal color metric, identify a minimal neural basis for formative capacities, settle inverted spectra, or certify artificial consciousness. These limits do not make the proposed questions empty. They identify what the present definitions and experiments can discriminate, and which stronger conclusions require further work.

The constructive aim is narrower and substantive: make shared meaning, distinct access, attributed mind, interactive effects, and interpersonal understanding separately articulable and testable without treating their relations as accidental.

## Difference-Preserving Intersubjective Bridges

The completed P1 corpus positions The Locus of Shared Experience as a theory of **difference-preserving intersubjective bridges and shared encounters across distinct realizations**. It explains how coordination, comparison, recognition, correction, and joint continuation can become real without requiring identical inner states, a merged subject, or complete phenomenal access.

- **Adjacent work:** *The Locus of Qualia*  
  **Supplies:** A contextual experiential-binding hypothesis for local qualitative targets.  
  **Shared-experience boundary:** Shared experience relates distinct local targets; it does not dissolve their locality.

- **Adjacent work:** *Qualia Binding and Realization Mapping*  
  **Supplies:** Measurement and realization-bridge contracts.  
  **Shared-experience boundary:** Those contracts test local and cross-realization claims; this paper supplies the intersubjective relation model.

- **Adjacent work:** *The Locus of Meaning*  
  **Supplies:** Semantic binding, jurisdiction, uptake, and consequence.  
  **Shared-experience boundary:** Shared meaning can support an encounter, but semantic agreement is not phenomenal identity.

- **Adjacent work:** *A Formal Theory of Work Custody*  
  **Supplies:** Responsibility, transfer, return, and terminal disposition for joint work.  
  **Shared-experience boundary:** Joint custody can be part of a shared encounter; coordination responsibility is not shared experience by itself.

- **Adjacent work:** *The Well-Governed Mind*  
  **Supplies:** Bounded cognitive governance and self-revision.  
  **Shared-experience boundary:** Each participant can revise locally while the bridge records mutual correction and remaining difference.

A shared-encounter record is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S= (&participants, target, local\_profiles, bridge, \\
&interaction, recognitions, corrections, \\
&joint\_constraints, residual\_differences, evidence, \\
&permissions, standing)
\end{aligned}.
$$

Several relations must remain distinct: common cause, simultaneous exposure, semantic agreement, mutual prediction, reciprocal influence, coordination, recognition, and phenomenal sameness. A strong shared encounter may establish some of these while leaving phenomenal comparison unresolved. Better understanding may increase the precision of represented disagreement rather than convergence.

The bridge is directional and permission-sensitive. What participant A can infer, render, or act upon regarding B need not be symmetric. A high-fidelity model can still be extractive, nonconsensual, or authority-laundering. Refusal, selective disclosure, correction, contest, and withdrawal are therefore part of the bridge contract rather than noise to optimize away.

The universal GSM can type   
`PARTICIPATES_IN_ENCOUNTER`, `HAS_LOCAL_PROFILE`,

Seven retained downstream uses place shared experience beside governed binding, qualia research, semantic attractors, recursive orientation, accountable notice, local experiential hypotheses, and interface-frame analysis. Common cause, simultaneous exposure, semantic agreement, prediction, reciprocal influence, recognition, coordination, and phenomenal sameness remain separate relations. A bridge is directional: what A may infer, retain, render, or act upon regarding B need not be symmetric. Withheld information should be represented by the authorized fact and scope of non-disclosure, not by an inferred substitute.

No aggregate map, mutual acknowledgment flag, high prediction accuracy, or stable shared vocabulary creates a group subject. Better understanding may increase the precision of disagreement. ## Shared Does Not Mean Identical

### Public orientation

This paper asks how people can genuinely share an encounter while retaining different bodies, histories, access routes, appraisals, and experiences. Its answer is a permissioned, directional relation that can preserve correspondence and difference. It does not claim direct access to another person's phenomenality, identical qualia, or a merged subject.

### Adversarial tests

1. **Prediction-as-understanding attack.** Accurate behavioral prediction is treated as full understanding. Prediction is one witness and may coexist with misrecognition, coercion, or explanatory error.
2. **Agreement-as-truth attack.** Mutual convergence is treated as objective correctness. Agreement can be coordinated, mistaken, or produced by shared bias.
3. **Difference-erasure attack.** A common representation discards local appraisal, uncertainty, disability, language, culture, or refusal. Bridge profiles must state preserved differences and projection loss.
4. **Permission-bypass attack.** One participant's data or interpretation is made available to another without consent, purpose limitation, or withdrawal. Accessibility and authorization are independent.
5. **Power-asymmetry attack.** An institution defines the shared account while a participant cannot correct it. Attribution records need source, standing, contest, correction, and retained dissent.
6. **Group-mind attack.** Higher-order coordination is described as a new experiencing subject. The paper refuses that inference absent independent evidence and criteria.
7. **Recognition-recursion attack.** Mutual recognition is required without a stopping rule. Protocols need bounded sufficient evidence and lawful non-resolution.
8. **Privacy-through-inference attack.** A model infers intimate states from interaction and labels the result shared understanding. Inference, disclosure, and use each require separate authority.

### Claim and evidence ceiling

The paper provides a conceptual and technical grammar for difference-preserving intersubjective records. Its propositions demonstrate non-entailments inside the declared models; they do not prove claims about phenomenology, psychology, neuroscience, cross-species cognition, or social outcomes.

The executable illustration tests data structures and validation rules only. Publication claims about real participants require current external literature, ethics and affected-party review, consent procedures, empirical methods, uncertainty, and evidence that correction and withdrawal actually work. No external research was added in this pass.

### Reader-facing consequence

The strongest public result is that understanding need not mean convergence: a system can represent what is shared, what differs, who may see it, how each party may correct it, and what remains unresolved without forcing one account to absorb the other.

## 18. Conclusion: Commonality Without Erasure

The contextual-binding proposal becomes more informative when extended from individual episodes to relations among participants. A capability depends on an operating organization and its support. An observer's recognition depends on a different organization, with its own access and expectations. A shared semantic distinction can be realized through different routes, while agreement at one resolution leaves finer differences open.

Objectivity and subjectivity therefore need not function as mutually exclusive answers to where meaning belongs. Publicly warranted relations can connect situated encounters without replacing them. The shared account can include not only what matches, but also what differs, who reports the difference, which explanation is supported, and where comparison remains unavailable.

Interaction adds another possibility. By responding to one another, participants can create consequential relations among their encounters. These relations can change what becomes salient, intelligible, or reachable next. They do not have to collapse the participants into one subject or make their experiences identical.

The deepest practical consequence is that understanding need not be measured by convergence alone. Learning that another person differs from oneself can correct a false assumption of sameness. A more faithful bridge can preserve disagreement while improving the common account of it.

The original qualia program asks what organization could constitute a particular experience. This addendum asks how distinct experiential lives can become mutually intelligible. Its answer is provisional but precise:

> A shared experience need not be an identical experience held twice. It can be a consequential relation in which commonality and difference become answerable to one another, while each participant remains distinct.

### A.7 Validation and refusal conditions

The proposed validator should reject or qualify at least the following errors:

- **Condition:** Episode merged with category because labels match  
  **Disposition:** `IDENTITY_KIND_COLLISION`  
  **Interpretation:** Identity targets differ

- **Condition:** Local claim loses its participant owner  
  **Disposition:** `PERSPECTIVE_ATTRIBUTION_LOST`  
  **Interpretation:** Difference record no longer means the same thing

- **Condition:** Coarse match treated as fine match  
  **Disposition:** `COMPARISON_RESOLUTION_EXCEEDED`  
  **Interpretation:** Missing refinement evidence

- **Condition:** Alternative access treated as original modality  
  **Disposition:** `ACCESS_ROUTE_SUBSTITUTED`  
  **Interpretation:** The evidence concerns a different route

- **Condition:** Common exposure treated as reciprocal uptake  
  **Disposition:** `INTERACTION_CLAIM_UNSUPPORTED`  
  **Interpretation:** The claimed relation needs additional evidence

- **Condition:** Same controller treated as same live trajectory  
  **Disposition:** `TRAJECTORY_CONSTANCY_UNVERIFIED`  
  **Interpretation:** Feedback may have changed inputs

- **Condition:** Agreement treated as truth or phenomenal identity  
  **Disposition:** `CLAIM_CEILING_EXCEEDED`  
  **Interpretation:** Different questions were conflated

- **Condition:** Withheld or unmapped content converted to zero  
  **Disposition:** `MISSINGNESS_COLLAPSED`  
  **Interpretation:** Absence of data was mistaken for a measured absence

- **Condition:** Corrected claim silently replaces original  
  **Disposition:** `REVISION_LINEAGE_LOST`  
  **Interpretation:** Interaction history was overwritten

- **Condition:** Pairwise bridges closed into global agreement  
  **Disposition:** `GLOBAL_BRIDGE_NOT_ESTABLISHED`  
  **Interpretation:** Composition requires independent support

A typed refusal is a finding about the proposed research operation, not a judgment that the participant lacks experience or understanding. The tool should preserve the original description when its schema is insufficient, following the parent brief and Meaning Crystals' account of representational gaps [Q2, Section 5.7; M1, Section 5].

### A.8 Evaluation contracts

A minimal implementation should report separate outcomes for correspondence coverage, errors within covered cases, correct participant attribution, preservation of declared differences, correction incorporation, partner-prediction calibration, and actual versus believed agreement. Any numerical aggregation must state its purpose, denominator, scope, and exclusions. There is no default composite understanding or consciousness score.

Counterexamples are first-class tests. A bridge that always says "we agree" should fail a difference-preservation case. A bridge that always refuses should fail useful coverage. A system that predicts a partner accurately through an accidentally leaked identifier should fail the information-availability contract. A model of a shared event should fail a reciprocity claim when only a common cause is supplied.

Before fitting models, freeze the source records, data splits, question, and comparison resolution. Freeze learned projections and thresholds before final evaluation. After a correction, retain both the earlier and later bridge with an explicit revision edge. The original trial or record must not be silently relabeled as though the correction were available at the time.

### A.9 Minimal implementation work order

The first work order is limited to record validation and synthetic adversarial cases. It should not require a language model, live participant, or external service. A second work order can implement comparison and partner-prediction models on appropriately authorized data. Empirical protocols require independent consent, privacy, and methodological review.

Acceptance requires successful distinction of positive, negative, and unresolved examples. A successful record validator demonstrates enforcement of declared representation rules. It does not demonstrate H-F, H-A, H-I, H-U, or H-P. No existing semantic primitive, version, or governance configuration is amended by passing these tests.
