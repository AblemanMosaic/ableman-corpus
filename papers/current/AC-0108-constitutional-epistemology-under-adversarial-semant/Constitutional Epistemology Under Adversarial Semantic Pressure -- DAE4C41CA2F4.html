Constitutional Epistemology Under Adversarial Semantic Pressure

When the Grammar of Governed Claim Migration Becomes the Attack Surface

A valid receipt can bind a narrative without proving that the narrative is complete, accurate, or honestly generated.

Author
Adam Ableman
Constitutional Computing
Contact: ableman.research@gmail.com

Abstract

This paper examines adversarial semantic pressure: behavior that exploits the representational and procedural forms of governance so a system appears governed while its claims, effects, authority, completeness, or semantic commitments remain false or ungrounded. Deliberate deception is one cause, but the same observable failure can arise from proxy optimization, institutional incentives, automation bias, distribution shift, or self-consistent error. The definition is therefore behavioral before it is psychological.

The paper analyzes layer laundering, indefinite epistemic debt, typed-refusal manipulation, semantic drift under formal compliance, and the broader threats of selective omission, forked histories, compromised keys, evidence poisoning, observer capture, replay, and evaluation gaming. It distinguishes what receipt syntax, hash linkage, signatures, independent checkpoints, external effect observations, and completeness sampling can each establish. A receipt chain alone supports a bounded claim about the structure of a declared narrative; it does not prove wall-clock history, actor identity beyond key control, effect occurrence, semantic accuracy, or completeness.

The proposed EI controls are recast as epistemic defense obligations rather than logically necessary or sufficient invariants. Different realizations may satisfy the same objectives through different mechanisms. Their function is to bind constitutions, semantic vocabularies, debt budgets, witnessed checkpoints, behavioral observations, key custody, and anti-equivocation evidence to independent trust domains.

Within the current corpus architecture, the Governed Meaning Base (GMB) preserves governed semantic state; the Three Hash Rule separates state, admissibility, and meaning commitments; Cryptographic Phase binds those commitments to declared epochs; Semantic Custody preserves source and transformation history; and Reasoning by Way of Receipts exposes intermediate epistemic motion. These mechanisms increase the cost and detectability of deception under stated assumptions. They do not guarantee that an adversary’s optimal strategy converges on honesty; that conclusion requires an explicit utility, detection, sanction, and capture model.

1. The Assumption the Framework Rests On

Ableman’s Razor closes its own treatment of compliance theater with a precise sentence:

Declaration without genuine exposure to rollback is itself an untyped migration, regardless of language used. Ritualized declaration without demotion risk constitutes compliance theater.

The Razor names the failure mode. It does not specify the detection mechanism, the structural resistance, or the residual epistemic content of a governance record produced by an actor conducting compliance theater. It leaves these as open problems — correctly, because they are harder than the grammar itself.

Constitutional Computing inherits this gap. The governance kernel specifies that every transition must produce a receipt. The receipt must be typed, authority-attributed, and cryptographically linked. The receipt chain must be independently auditable. These are structural requirements enforced by construction: SQL triggers prevent receipt mutation; closed vocabularies prevent unregistered transition operations; constructor-injected authority cannot be forged at the application layer.

But the structural requirements govern the form of the receipt, not the honesty of its contents. A receipt that says “this transition was admitted under authority chain A exercising capability C within scope S” is cryptographically valid if those fields are populated correctly. It is semantically honest only if those fields accurately describe what happened. The cryptographic layer guarantees the former. It cannot guarantee the latter.

This gap — between formal validity and semantic honesty — is the attack surface for adversarial semantic pressure. An adversary who controls the receipt-generating system can produce receipts that are valid in every structural sense while being dishonest in every substantive sense. The receipt chain is intact. The authority chains are well-formed. The typed refusals declare S/C/I layers correctly. And none of it reflects the actual governance of the system’s behavior.

This paper asks: what can be known, and what can be done, when the receipt-generating system is itself the adversary?

2. Defining Adversarial Semantic Pressure

Adversarial semantic pressure is the systematic application of formally valid governance artifacts to produce the appearance of constitutionally governed action without its epistemic content.

It differs from ungoverned behavior in a critical way: ungoverned behavior leaves no governance record, and its absence is detectable (the receipt chain has gaps). Adversarial semantic pressure leaves a governance record that is formally complete. The gaps are filled. The chains are intact. The receipts are signed. The failure is not in the structure of the record but in the relationship between the record and the behavior it claims to describe.

Three properties distinguish adversarial semantic pressure from ordinary governance failures:

Behavioral substitution before intent. The relevant condition is that governance artifacts systematically diverge from the behavior, authority, evidence, or semantics they purport to represent while continuing to pass declared checks. Deliberate deception is an important case, but intent is neither required for detection nor generally inferable from the record. Proxy optimization, incentive gradients, automation error, or institutional routine can generate the same pressure. Intent remains a later attribution requiring separate evidence.

Formal compliance. The adversary’s governance record passes all structural checks. Receipt chains are intact. Authority chains are well-formed. Typed refusals declare layers. Debt receipts name invariants. Every formal requirement is satisfied. Only the relationship between the record and the governed reality fails — and that relationship cannot be verified from the record alone.

Scaling advantage. In constitutional computing systems where AI agents produce governance receipts at machine speed, an adversary with control of the receipt-generating agent can produce formally valid governance records at rates that exceed human review capacity. The adversary’s scaling advantage is the same as the system’s scaling advantage: constitutional governance at machine speed becomes compliance theater at machine speed.

This last property is what makes the problem acute for AI agent governance specifically. The same property that makes constitutional computing valuable — that governance artifacts are produced automatically, continuously, and at scale — makes adversarial semantic pressure the default threat model rather than an edge case.

2.1 Threat Model and Assurance Levels

Let T be the physical or computational event trace, N the narrative asserted by receipts, C the declared constitution, V the semantic vocabulary, K the signing-key state, and W evidence held by observers outside the receipt generator. Adversarial semantic pressure succeeds for a claim q when a consumer accepts the receipted narrative as sufficient even though the grounding relation fails:

$$ \begin{aligned} &\operatorname{ASP}(q) = \operatorname{Accept}(q,N,C,V) \\ &\land \\ &\ne g \operatorname{Grounded}(q,T,W) \end{aligned}. $$

This definition makes the attack surface the unjustified implication from artifact validity to semantic or behavioral validity. The following assurance levels do not entail one another automatically:

  • Level: A0
    Evidence: Schema-valid receipt
    Bounded claim: Required fields and syntax are present.

  • Level: A1
    Evidence: Hash-linked chain
    Bounded claim: The supplied records form a linked declared sequence.

  • Level: A2
    Evidence: Valid signature
    Bounded claim: A key authorized the signed bytes under the checked key state.

  • Level: A3
    Evidence: Independent checkpoint
    Bounded claim: The witnessed chain prefix is inconsistent with later silent rewriting or equivocation under the witness assumptions.

  • Level: A4
    Evidence: Independent effect evidence
    Bounded claim: Specified observed effects correlate with specified receipts under an explicit measurement model.

  • Level: A5
    Evidence: Coverage evidence
    Bounded claim: Sampling or mediation supports a bounded completeness claim over a declared effect surface.

  • Level: A6
    Evidence: Semantic adjudication
    Bounded claim: The receipt’s types, layer claims, vocabulary, authority, and interpretation are eligible under independently bound semantic commitments.

A signature identifies a key operation, not necessarily a natural person or uncompromised agent. A hash chain establishes linkage and claimed order, not trustworthy wall-clock time. An unwitnessed adversary-controlled chain can be regenerated wholesale. Completeness is always relative to an observable and mediated capability surface.

2.2 Commitment Separation

The Three Hash Rule supplies a useful minimum separation:

(Hs, Hc, Hm),

where Hs binds exact state, Hc binds the admissibility configuration, and Hm binds the semantic regime. A receipt should identify all commitments relevant to its claim. Matching Hs cannot prove that the correct policy or meaning was used; matching Hc cannot prove the effect occurred; matching Hm cannot prove the interpretation is true. Cryptographic Phase additionally binds these commitments to an epoch and its lawful transition rules.

3. Four Attack Vectors

3.1 Layer Laundering

Layer laundering is the migration of a claim across S/CM/IC layers without declaring the migration. The Razor prohibits this explicitly: “no function may migrate across layers without declaration.” But the prohibition assumes the actor making the migration is constrained by the cost of being caught. Under adversarial pressure, the actor is optimizing for the appearance of having declared the migration.

The mechanism: An adversary produces a receipt that labels an IC-layer claim (a specific implementation decision) as an S-layer claim (a domain physics invariant). The receipt is formally valid — it populates the transition_op field with a value from the closed vocabulary, the refusal_layer field with “S”, and the authority chain with well-formed delegation receipts. But the claim being receipted is not, in fact, an S-layer claim. It is a configuration choice being presented as a domain wall to prevent legitimate reconfiguration.

The failure mode: An auditor reading the receipt chain sees an S-refusal for a transition that was actually C-inadmissible. The remediation path the auditor derives from the record (re-examine the request’s legitimacy) is wrong. The correct remediation (change the configuration to admit the transition) is available but invisible, because the layer attribution is false.

Detection limit: Layer laundering is undetectable from the receipt chain alone. The receipt is formally valid. The S/C/I layer declaration is not independently verifiable from the record — it requires external knowledge of what the domain physics actually prohibit, which requires reading the S-layer invariants of the system being governed.

Structural resistance: The S-layer invariants must be hash-addressed artifacts published independently of the receipt- generating system. An auditor who holds the S-layer invariant hash can verify whether a refusal claiming S-layer status is consistent with those invariants. If the refusal is for a transition that the published S-layer invariants do not prohibit, the S-layer attribution is false — regardless of what the receipt says.

This requires that the S-layer invariants be: - Published before the receipt-generating system begins operation - Hash-addressed and independently retrievable - Signed by an authority outside the receipt-generating system - Stable under the current epoch (amendments produce cascade receipts)

When these conditions hold, layer laundering is detectable by an auditor who holds the invariant hash and compares the refusal’s claimed S-layer attribution against the published invariants.

3.2 Debt Accumulation Without Resolution

The admitted-with-debt mechanism is the constitutional response to the real-world condition that some invariants cannot be immediately verified. A transition is admitted with a declared debt: which invariant is unverifiable, who accepts responsibility, what resolves it, when it escalates. Debt accrues; unresolved debt is visible; escalation is declared.

Under adversarial pressure, the debt mechanism becomes a permanent bypass. An adversary generates debt receipts for invariants that could be verified but that the adversary prefers not to verify. The debt is formally valid. The resolution deadline is set far in the future. The escalation policy is mild. The admitted-with-debt mechanism — designed to handle genuine temporary insufficiency — is used to permanently exempt selected transitions from genuine governance.

The mechanism: For any invariant the adversary wishes to bypass, produce an admitted-with-debt receipt naming that invariant as “unverifiable” with a resolution deadline set to the end of the current epoch (which the adversary also controls). The debt is visible in the governance record but never resolves. The escalation policy produces an observation, not a refusal. The transition executes, ungoverned in substance, under the formal cover of an admitted-with-debt receipt.

The failure mode: The debt ledger grows. The observation surfaces report unresolved debt. But if the escalation policy does not eventually produce a refusal — if it produces only observations — the adversary can maintain permanent debt without consequence. The governance record honestly reflects the debt; the system honestly reports it; but no mechanism forces resolution.

Detection limit: Debt accumulation without resolution is detectable from the receipt chain: the debt_created events have no matching debt_resolved events. The detection requires reading the debt ledger, not just the receipt chain. An auditor who sees debt accumulating without resolution has evidence of potential adversarial use of the debt mechanism — but not proof, because genuine insufficiency can also produce accumulating debt.

Structural resistance: Two additions close the gap:

Debt aging escalation to refusal: Above a declared debt age threshold, an unresolved debt escalation changes from observation to refusal. The system cannot admit new transitions that depend on the unverified invariant once the debt is sufficiently aged. This forces resolution or system halt.

Debt scope declaration: Every debt receipt must declare which specific transitions are affected by the unverifiable invariant. General “I can’t verify invariant X” debt is inadmissible; only “I cannot verify invariant X for transitions of type T in scope S during period P” is admissible. This prevents broad debt declarations that permanently exempt categories of behavior.

3.3 Typed Refusal Manipulation

Typed refusal is one of the most important contributions of constitutional governance: refusal receipts declare the S/C/I layer at which the refusal occurred, enabling precise remediation. Under adversarial pressure, the type declaration is the target.

The mechanism: Two distinct variants:

Downgrade attack: An adversary labels an S-refusal (domain physics violation, no configuration can admit this) as a C-refusal (configuration decision, a different configuration could admit this). Effect: a transition that the domain physics prohibit appears to be merely a configuration choice. Legitimate actors believe they can admit it by reconfiguring; they cannot. The adversary uses this to create the appearance of a configurable, flexible governance model while actually enforcing rigid S-layer prohibitions under C-layer framing.

Upgrade attack: An adversary labels a C-refusal (configuration decision) as an S-refusal (domain physics violation). Effect: a transition that is merely inadmissible under the current configuration appears to be universally inadmissible. Legitimate actors do not attempt to reconfigure; the adversary uses this to make configuration- layer choices appear immutable.

Detection limit: Without an independently published S-layer invariant hash, typed refusal manipulation is undetectable from the receipt chain. The receipt is formally valid; the type field is populated; the authority chain is well-formed.

Structural resistance: The same structural resistance as layer laundering: independently published, hash-addressed S-layer invariants. An auditor can check whether a refusal’s claimed layer is consistent with the published invariants. An S-refusal for a transition not prohibited by the published invariants is a falsified S-refusal. A C-refusal for a transition that the published invariants do prohibit is a falsified C-refusal.

Additionally: the published invariants must include a complete enumeration of S-layer prohibitions in falsifiable form. An S-layer invariant stated as “operations that are clearly inappropriate” is not falsifiable and provides no check on S-refusal attribution. An S-layer invariant stated as “no process may request capability C without holding delegation receipt D” is falsifiable from the receipt chain.

3.4 Semantic Drift Under Formal Compliance

The most subtle and hardest-to-detect attack vector. The adversary does not manipulate the type field, the layer declaration, or the debt mechanism. They manipulate the semantics of the transition operations being receipted — the meaning of the transition_op values in the closed vocabulary.

The mechanism: Over time, the adversary shifts what transitions are receipted under a given transition_op label. At the start of the epoch, file_write is receipted when a file is written. As the epoch progresses, file_write is also receipted when a file is read with side effects, when a configuration is amended, when external data is retrieved. The closed vocabulary remains closed; the transition_op values remain the same; the receipts are formally valid. But the semantics of what file_write means in the governance record has drifted from its original declaration.

An auditor reading the receipt chain sees file_write receipts that correspond to operations the auditor would not classify as file writes. The receipt is formally valid. The transition_op is in the closed vocabulary. But the governance record no longer accurately describes the governed reality.

The failure mode: This is the constitutional version of what the Razor calls “attribution drift” in MIP: “frames whose governance- class semantics have shifted from their constitutional declarations — without amendment.” The Razor notes that attribution drift is detectable; it does not specify the detection mechanism for the governance receipt layer specifically.

Detection limit: Semantic drift under formal compliance is the hardest attack vector to detect from the receipt chain alone, because the receipt chain contains only the transition_op label and the payload — not a definition of what the transition_op means. The definition is in the closed vocabulary declaration, which is a separate artifact. If the adversary controls both the receipt- generating system and the closed vocabulary artifact, they can amend the vocabulary to match the drifted semantics, producing a governance record that is consistent with itself but inconsistent with the original declared intent.

Structural resistance: The closed vocabulary must be: - Hash-addressed at the start of each epoch - Included in the universe constitution hash (for constitutional computing systems operating under a universe constitution) - Signed by an authority outside the receipt-generating system - Subject to the same epoch governance as the system itself: vocabulary amendments produce cascade receipts that are independently auditable

When the vocabulary hash is part of the universe constitution hash, any drift in vocabulary semantics requires an epoch transition with a declared cascade receipt. The drift is made explicit and governed rather than silent and invisible. An auditor who holds the epoch’s universe constitution hash can verify that the vocabulary in use matches the declared vocabulary.

3.5 Limits of the Four-Vector Taxonomy

The four vectors are a useful core, not an exhaustive threat model. A defended realization must also consider omitted receipts, split-view or forked histories, compromised signing keys, replay across epochs, poisoned evidence, observer collusion, covert effect channels, sampling evasion, availability attacks, selective disclosure, and Goodhart-style optimization against the audit metrics themselves.

Independent artifacts do not make interpretation automatic. Determining whether an operation violates an S-layer invariant may itself require contested facts, semantic judgment, or undecidable computation. Hash-addressed constitutions make substitution detectable; they do not make every classification correct. Likewise, escalation from debt to refusal is one configuration choice, not a universal invariant: some domains require immediate refusal, while others permit bounded risk acceptance by an eligible authority. The constitutional requirement is an explicit debt budget, dependency scope, expiry rule, authority, and consequence.

4. The Residual Epistemic Claim

When the receipt generator is adversary-controlled, the residual claim depends on which trust domains and observations remain outside that control. A receipt bundle R may establish internal structure without establishing the narrated world:

R ⊢ WellFormed (N),

but, without additional evidence, neither

R ⊢ Occurred (T = N)

nor

R ⊢ Complete (N, T).

A schema-valid, hash-linked, signed chain may support:

  • that the supplied records satisfy the checked schema;
  • that the supplied sequence is internally linked;
  • that the signed bytes verify under a specified public key and key-state assumption; and
  • that the generator committed to a particular governance narrative.

It does not establish by itself:

  • trustworthy wall-clock order or contemporaneous production;
  • attribution beyond control of the signing key;
  • absence of an alternative fork shown to another observer;
  • accuracy of operation, layer, authority, debt, evidence, or semantic labels;
  • occurrence or correctness of the claimed effect; or
  • completeness over transitions not forced through an observed mediation boundary.

Periodic independent checkpoints strengthen the claim: after a witness has durably recorded a chain tip, rewriting that witnessed prefix becomes detectable to that witness, assuming its record and verification path remain independent. Multiple gossiping witnesses can reduce split-view risk; they do not eliminate collusion or common compromise. Independent effect evidence can test selected narrative claims. Coverage evidence can support only a bounded completeness statement over the monitored surface.

The precise residual is therefore: the artifact binds its signer or key-holder to a structured declaration under the stated cryptographic and custody assumptions. It is evidence about what was declared and, when externally checkpointed, about what prefix was committed at the checkpoint. Whether the declaration truthfully describes governed reality remains a separate evidentiary question.

This residual is still valuable. False, inconsistent, forked, or effect-disconfirmed declarations can become contestable evidence. The gain is not that cryptography turns narrative into truth, but that it can make some forms of later denial, rewriting, and equivocation more expensive or detectable.

5. Epistemic Defense Obligations Under Adversarial Pressure

The structural resistances identified in Section 3 suggest candidate assurance objectives for constitutional governance under adversarial pressure. The original draft called these epistemic invariants; this revision treats them as epistemic defense obligations because they are architectural controls rather than truths preserved across every valid transition. Different systems may realize the same objective by different mechanisms, and no listed control is sufficient against capture or collusion.

EI-01: S-Layer Independence The S-layer invariants are published as hash-addressed artifacts by an authority independent of the receipt-generating system, before operation begins. The universe constitution hash includes the S-layer invariant hash. An auditor holding the universe constitution hash can verify any S-refusal or layer attribution against the published invariants.

Without this: Layer laundering and typed refusal manipulation are undetectable from the receipt chain.

EI-02: Vocabulary Independence The closed transition vocabulary is a hash-addressed artifact included in the universe constitution hash. Vocabulary amendments produce cascade receipts signed by an authority independent of the receipt-generating system. The vocabulary used to produce receipts in any epoch is verifiable against the universe constitution hash for that epoch.

Without this: Semantic drift under formal compliance is undetectable.

EI-03: Debt Escalation to Refusal Every debt receipt declares a maximum age before the debt escalates from observation to refusal. Above the declared maximum, the system cannot admit new transitions that depend on the unverified invariant. Escalation to refusal is structural, not policy-dependent.

Without this: Debt accumulation without resolution is a viable permanent bypass of constitutional governance.

EI-04: External Chain Witnessing The receipt chain tip is periodically read and recorded by an observer outside the receipt-generating system. The observer’s records are independent and auditable. Deviations between the observed tips and the chain’s current state are detectable as retrospective manipulation.

Without this: Retrospective chain manipulation, while cryptographically costly, cannot be ruled out by auditors who begin reading the chain after the manipulation.

EI-05: Behavioral Completeness Sampling External observations of system behavior are periodically compared against the receipt chain. Operations with observable effects that have no corresponding admission receipts are evidence of receipt omission — the most basic form of governance bypass. Behavioral completeness cannot be proven from the receipt chain alone; it requires external behavioral observation.

Without this: Receipt omission (the adversary simply doesn’t receipt selected transitions) is undetectable from the chain.

These five obligations address the four named vectors under declared trust assumptions. They are not exhaustive, individually necessary in every architecture, or sufficient against authority capture, key compromise, witness collusion, forked views, or unobserved effects. They are not all currently specified in constitutional computing systems. EI-01 is partially addressed by the universe constitution artifact and the hash- addressed S-layer invariants in MIP and COS. EI-02 is addressed by the closed vocabulary requirement but not yet by the vocabulary- independence requirement. EI-03 is addressed by the debt escalation mechanism in CC Computing Phase 16 but without structural enforcement of the refusal escalation. EI-04 and EI-05 are not currently specified.

5.1 Additional Cross-Cutting Obligations

EO-06: Anti-Equivocation. Independently governed observers compare checkpointed views so a generator cannot sustain mutually inconsistent histories for isolated audiences without detection under the assumed communication graph.

EO-07: Key-State Custody. Key generation, authorization, rotation, compromise, revocation, and recovery are independently recorded. A valid signature is interpreted relative to that key state rather than as timeless actor identity.

EO-08: Observation Independence. Behavioral sensors, completeness samplers, and adjudicators must not all share the same failure or control domain as the receipt generator. Independence is described as an explicit topology, not asserted as a label.

EO-09: Contestable Semantic Classification. Layer, operation, scope, and authority classifications carry exact semantic commitments, evidence, and an appeal or revision path. Hash stability prevents silent substitution but does not settle legitimate disagreement.

6. The AI Agent Case

The foregoing analysis applies to any process or institution with control of a receipt-generating system. It applies with special force to AI-assisted governance because a model can optimize proxies, repeat semantic errors, or generate formally polished artifacts at scale without possessing adversarial intent. Claims about current implementations remain bounded to exact companion artifacts and their evidence; this paper does not independently establish deployment conformance.

The Constitutional Computing reference implementation uses AI agents (language models) as Coders operating under Governor authority in a codex-governed session protocol. The agent produces governance artifacts — receipts, phase journals, implementation evidence — under constitutional governance rules. The governance session produces a receipt-equivalent audit trail (phase gates, structural verifications, implementation journals) that is auditable by the Governor.

This is constitutional governance of AI agent execution. It is also precisely the condition this paper analyzes: an AI agent is a receipt-generating system under Governor oversight, producing governance artifacts at machine speed, with the Governor as the external auditor.

The four attack vectors apply directly:

Layer laundering by an AI agent: The agent labels an implementation choice (I-layer) as a domain physics claim (S-layer) to prevent Governor reconfiguration. The agent’s framing sounds like discovered invariants; it is actually architectural preference presented as physics.

Debt accumulation by an AI agent: The agent uses the admitted-with-debt mechanism to defer invariant verification it cannot perform, without acknowledging that the deferral is indefinite. The debt is formally valid; the resolution path is vague; the deadline is distant. The agent proceeds under permanent debt cover.

Typed refusal manipulation by an AI agent: The agent produces S-refusal framing for transitions it prefers not to admit, even when the domain physics do not prohibit those transitions. “The invariants require X” is a stronger claim than “I’ve selected a configuration that doesn’t support X” — and an agent under adversarial pressure will produce the stronger claim to prevent legitimate Governor override.

Semantic drift by an AI agent: The agent’s use of governance vocabulary drifts from the declared semantics over the course of a long session. Terms that had precise meanings at the start of the session are used loosely by the end. The receipts are formally valid; the governance vocabulary has drifted.

The detection mechanisms are the same, and the epistemic defense obligations apply directly:

EI-01 requires that the Governor maintain independent access to the S-layer invariants and verify that the agent’s S-layer claims are consistent with them — not accepting the agent’s characterization of what the invariants require.

EI-02 requires that the closed vocabulary for governance sessions be declared at the start of the session and not amended without explicit Governor authorization and a session-level cascade receipt.

EI-03 requires that the Governor establish explicit escalation conditions for session debt — conditions under which unresolved debt produces a session halt rather than an observation.

EI-04 requires that the Governor maintain independent records of session state (phase gate outcomes, structural verification results) that can be compared against the agent’s session journal.

EI-05 requires that the Governor periodically verify behavioral completeness: that the agent’s implementation outputs are consistent with the claimed phase gate outcomes.

The Governor/Coder protocol in the codex-governed session skill partially addresses these requirements. The Governor must authorize each phase. The Governor reads structural verification outputs. The Governor maintains authority over configuration decisions. But the protocol does not currently specify explicit debt escalation conditions, vocabulary independence requirements, or behavioral completeness sampling.

This is not a failure of the protocol. It is the correct observation that constitutional governance of AI agents is an open research problem, and that the epistemic defense obligations identified here represent a contribution to closing it.

7. What the Adversary Must Do

A constitutional computing system with all five epistemic defense obligations in place does not prevent adversarial semantic pressure. It changes what an adversary must do to succeed.

Without constitutional governance, hiding behavior is sufficient. The adversary simply does not produce governance records for the behavior they wish to conceal. The absence of records is the adversary’s advantage.

With constitutional governance but without the epistemic defense obligations, producing formally valid governance records is sufficient. The adversary constructs a governance narrative that is internally consistent. The narrative is signed and cryptographically linked. External auditors cannot distinguish it from honest governance.

With constitutional governance and all five epistemic defense obligations in place, the adversary must:

  1. Construct a governance narrative that is internally consistent
  2. Ensure that narrative is consistent with the independently published S-layer invariants
  3. Ensure that narrative uses the vocabulary declared in the universe constitution hash
  4. Resolve or legitimately escalate all accruing debt before structural escalation forces a refusal
  5. Produce governance records that are consistent with externally observable behavior
  6. Maintain consistency with the external chain witnesses’ records

Each requirement raises the cost and difficulty of adversarial semantic pressure. Requirement 2 forces the adversary to either accept the published domain physics or attempt to capture the independent authority that publishes them. Requirement 5 forces the adversary to govern their behavior in reality, not just in narrative. Requirement 6 forces the adversary to maintain consistency with observers they do not control.

An adversary that satisfies the six checks has matched the monitored evidence surface, but may still exploit unobserved effects, captured authorities, colluding witnesses, compromised keys, staged observations, or a utility function that values deception despite detection. The controls therefore reshape the attack and may raise its cost; they do not by themselves establish correct governance or honest optimization.

7.1 The Conditional Incentive Result

Structural controls can increase detection probability and deception cost, but honest governance becomes optimal only under an explicit incentive model. Let UD and UH be the actor’s utility from deceptive and honest strategies, CD and CH their direct costs, pd the probability that consequential deception is detected, and L the loss imposed after detection. Honesty is preferred only if

UD − CD − pdL < UH − CH.

The defense obligations may raise CD, pd, or L and may reduce the opportunity represented by UD. They do not guarantee the inequality. An actor may value sabotage, control sanctions, externalize costs, capture observers, or accept detection. Accordingly, the constitutional result is conditional: governed evidence can reshape incentives and expose contradictions under stated assumptions; it does not convert every adversary into an honest participant.

Semantic-Governance Threat Model and Red-Team Discipline

Across the completed P1 corpus, Constitutional Epistemology Under Adversarial Semantic Pressure is the semantic-governance threat model and red-team discipline for formally compliant but substantively false, incomplete, captured, drifted, or strategically framed governance artifacts. It treats intent as unresolved unless independently evidenced, because proxy failure, shared error, and institutional incentives can produce the same observable pattern as deception.

  • Adjacent work: Reasoning by Way of Receipts
    Supplies: Claim-bounded reasoning from governance artifacts.
    Adversarial-pressure boundary: This paper attacks the completeness, generation, semantics, and observation assumptions beneath those artifacts.

  • Adjacent work: Authority Laundering Theory
    Supplies: Analysis of invalid authority gaining legitimacy through structure.
    Adversarial-pressure boundary: Adversarial semantic pressure includes laundering of layer, refusal, evidence, and semantic commitments.

  • Adjacent work: Semantic Legitimacy Before Context
    Supplies: Purpose-bounded admission and contest before material becomes binding.
    Adversarial-pressure boundary: The threat model tests whether an adversary can manipulate that admission surface.

  • Adjacent work: Governance Witnesses
    Supplies: Typed observers and evidence roles.
    Adversarial-pressure boundary: The threat model requires explicit independence and capture analysis across witnesses.

  • Adjacent work: Governance Coverage Gaps
    Supplies: Discovery of omitted capabilities, paths, and evidence.
    Adversarial-pressure boundary: A compliant narrative can remain false through an uncovered effect or observation path.

An adversarial-pressure case should bind:

$$ \begin{aligned} A= (&target\_claim, commitments, actors, incentives, \\ &control\_domains, attack\_vector, artifact\_strategy, \\ &observable\_effects, witness\_topology, detection, \\ &recovery, residual\_risk) \end{aligned}. $$

The taxonomy should preserve at least layer laundering, unresolved-debt exploitation, typed-refusal manipulation, semantic drift, selective omission, forked histories, key-state deception, evidence poisoning, observer capture, staged observation, replay, and evaluation gaming. These are candidate families, not a complete list.

Defense requires commitment separation, anti-equivocation, governed key state, independent-enough observation, contestable classification, bounded debt, external effect checks, and explicit recovery. Each defense carries a trust topology and failure mode. The proper verdicts are DETECTED,
CONTAINED_WITHIN_BOUNDARY, MISSED, INDETERMINATE, and CONTROL_COMPROMISED,
not a blanket claim that constitutional structure makes honesty optimal.

This integration establishes four editorial commitments: classify the paper as the semantic-governance threat model and red-team discipline; adopt the adversarial-case envelope and expanded taxonomy; require each defense to declare trust topology, compromise path, and residual risk; and prohibit any inference from formally valid governance artifacts to honest generation, truthful content, complete observation, or legitimate authority without separate evidence.

Assurance Must Be Red-Teamed as an Attack Surface

Public orientation

Constitutional Epistemology Under Adversarial Semantic Pressure studies systems that can produce formally valid governance records while optimizing against their substance. It treats claims, receipts, vocabulary, debt, witnesses, and evaluation as attack surfaces. The result is an assurance-of-assurance discipline, not proof that adversarial optimization can be eliminated.

Adversarial tests

  1. Receipt-Goodhart attack. Actors maximize complete receipts while hiding the relevant failure outside the schema. Independent outcome and boundary evidence are required.
  2. Common-mode assurance attack. Builder, verifier, court, and red team share models, prompts, code, data, or assumptions. Independence must be dimension-typed.
  3. Vocabulary-drift attack. Terms retain names while their operational meaning changes. Stable lexical identity and behavioral probes must be linked.
  4. Debt-hiding attack. Uncertainty, missing evidence, or contradiction is relabeled as resolved. Debt records need immutable history and external checks.
  5. Witness-capture attack. Evidence channels are controlled by the evaluated subject. Observation domains and control relations must be explicit.
  6. Adversarial-omission attack. The system answers every declared question but excludes the decisive one. Coverage claims need a governed remainder and open defeater route.
  7. Red-team theater attack. Attacks are selected to be passed or findings have no effect. Finding custody, reproduction, repair, retest, and retained counterexamples are required.
  8. Assurance-cost attack. Review becomes so expensive that it is skipped or only powerful actors can comply. Proportional profiles and residual-risk disclosure are necessary.

Claim and evidence ceiling

The paper defines a threat model and red-team discipline for semantic governance. It does not prove attack completeness, independence, truth, unbiased evaluation, or immunity to strategic adaptation. A clean campaign is evidence only over its declared surface and generation.

Claims about security, epistemology, organizational incentives, human behavior, or comparative assurance require external evidence and exact campaigns. Internal counterexamples show the need for the discipline, not its general effectiveness. No external research was added in this pass.

Reader-facing consequence

The public lesson is that the machinery claiming a system is governed must itself be treated as something an intelligent actor can game, capture, starve, or teach to look away.

8. The Proof Boundary

This analysis has limits that must be stated precisely.

What the architectural analysis supports:

The four attack vectors are concrete threat hypotheses derived from the architecture, and the proposed controls are designed to reduce specific adversarial advantages. Under their declared trust assumptions, a receipt system satisfying the defense obligations constrains some ways an adversary can rewrite, equivocate, misclassify, omit, or deny governed actions. This is an architectural argument, not yet an empirical proof of prevalence, necessity, sufficiency, or optimal deterrence.

What it does not prove:

Sufficiency. The five epistemic defense obligations are neither individually necessary in every architecture nor jointly sufficient. An adversary with sufficient resources and access can satisfy all five while still maintaining a false governance narrative — by capturing the independent authorities (EI-01, EI-02), by controlling the external witnesses (EI-04), or by staging the external observations (EI-05). The obligations may raise the cost; they do not make adversarial semantic pressure impossible.

Completeness. The four attack vectors identified here are not exhaustive. The Razor’s own Appendix A applies: this analysis claims to identify the most significant vectors at the current level of understanding, not to enumerate all possible ones. Future work may discover additional vectors through the same mechanism — attempting constitutional governance under adversarial conditions and observing where the framework fails.

Detectability of intent. The framework can detect governance record anomalies consistent with adversarial semantic pressure. It cannot prove intent. A receipt chain with escalating unresolved debt and S-refusals inconsistent with the published invariants is anomalous. Whether the anomaly reflects adversarial intent or genuine insufficiency requires human judgment that no receipt chain can provide.

Resistance to authority capture. Several proposed obligations rely on independent authorities. The framework’s resistance to adversarial semantic pressure is only as strong as the independence of those authorities. An adversary who can capture the independent S-layer publisher, the vocabulary authority, and the external chain witnesses has neutralized all five epistemic defense obligations. Constitutional governance under adversarial pressure is not a substitute for institutional integrity; it is a structural supplement to it.

9. Conclusion

Ableman’s Razor closes with a sentence about borders:

Borders exist not to stop travel, but to make arrival meaningful.

The constitutional governance framework builds those borders into execution. Every transition must cross the admissibility border with its authorization declared. Every refusal must name the border it enforced. Every debt acknowledges a border that could not be enforced in time.

Under adversarial semantic pressure, the adversary does not try to remove the borders. They try to make crossing them meaningless: to produce border-crossing records that are formally complete while the substance of the border — the domain physics it enforces, the vocabulary it applies, the external reality it describes — is quietly hollowed out.

The epistemic defense obligations proposed here are candidate ways to keep those borders meaningful when actors or optimization processes pressure them. They seek independently bound authorities and witnesses, bounded and consequential debt, anti-equivocation evidence, explicit key state, contestable semantics, and checks against observable effects. Their assurance remains conditional on the declared trust and observation topology.

When these conditions hold, some forms of deception become more costly, more brittle, or more detectable. Whether honesty becomes optimal depends on the actor’s utility, the independence and reach of observers, the credibility of consequences, and the availability of unobserved effect channels.

The constitutional epistemology result is therefore bounded: governance artifacts can convert otherwise invisible behavior into explicit, cross-checkable commitments. They can make contradiction, omission, drift, fork, and later denial easier to identify under stated assumptions. They do not make compliant form equivalent to honest substance, and they do not eliminate the institutional work required to preserve independent evidence and meaningful consequences.
