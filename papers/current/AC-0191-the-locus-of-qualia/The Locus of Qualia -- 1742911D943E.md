## Abstract

What would it mean to specify the taste of mint mathematically? This paper proposes a contextual binding event as the explanatory unit for qualitative specificity: an organized occurrence in which sensory differentiation, bodily state, temporal history, attention, and other relevant constraints jointly determine a particular experiential character. The proposal extends the Semantic Binding Principle and its distinctions among identity, support, constitution, and realization. It does not follow deductively from that semantic framework. The qualitative event, its hypothesized constitutive organization, its physical realization, and the observations used to investigate it remain separately typed.

Mint provides a worked domain: weak and strong mint, peppermint and spearmint, fresh and dried preparations, decay-associated qualities, and evolving aftertaste. These form candidate regions and trajectories in a context-indexed experiential atlas, not predetermined coordinates in a universal Euclidean space. Typed dimensional distinction, statistical independence, geometric orthogonality, and causal selectivity are distinguished. Experiential-content coordinates are also separated from the Ableman Consciousness Ontology's binding-regime axes, Semantic Manifold Competence's operational capacities, and the epistemic properties of a research account.

A source-integrated architecture assigns different responsibilities to Locus, Global Term Key, Dynatology, Semantic Manifold Topology, Semantic Binding Quality, and the GMB/crystallization lineage as governance of the research account. Dynatology contributes explicit reachability; SMT contributes declared continuity auditing rather than an assumed geometry of experience; SBQ supplies candidate criteria for model integrity rather than a consciousness score. A new path-realization requirement shows why individually realizable projected transitions may form an unrealizable sequence when a chart identifies states with different continuations. A finite-path lifting criterion and a worked counterexample make this issue testable.

The contribution is a falsifiable research architecture, not an empirical demonstration of phenomenal constitution. Proposed studies compare typed, contextual, history-sensitive binding models with strong alternatives on held-out observations, interventions, and sequences. Limited mathematical results and two executable synthetic fixtures establish only properties of stipulated models. The strongest hypothesis remains that qualitative specificity is constituted by a particular realized binding organization; prediction, operational competence, governance admission, and causal correspondence do not individually establish that hypothesis.

**Keywords:** qualia; semantic binding; phenomenal structure; contextual realization; architectonics; experiential atlas; causal abstraction; psychophysics; representational geometry; mint; consciousness; identifiability.

## 1. Introduction: From the Object for Mint to the Constitution of an Experience

The expression *the taste of mint* appears to identify one familiar quality. On examination, it covers many distinguishable encounters. A faint aftertaste differs from an intense initial sensation. Peppermint and spearmint may be discriminated without being classified as entirely unrelated. A fresh leaf, a dried preparation, and a decay-associated odor can preserve some recognizably mint-related character while differing on other dimensions. An apparently identical stimulus can also be encountered under different histories, expectations, and bodily conditions.

These examples motivate the present hypothesis; they are not a dataset. Their significance is that the explanatory target may be a structured family of contextual realizations rather than an isolated, context-free primitive called `MINT`.

The immediate point of departure is an interview transcript supplied by the author. Donald Hoffman challenges computational accounts to specify a mathematical structure for a particular conscious quality and explain why that structure is mint rather than chocolate. He distinguishes the absence of an explanatory mapping from the computational expense of evaluating an already specified theory [T1, approximately 19:59-24:30]. The distinction is useful, but the question admits several interpretations. A context-free matrix is one possible target; a contextually embedded causal organization is another. A computational theory need not restrict itself to the former.

The present proposal therefore does not declare Hoffman's challenge refuted. It asks whether a better unit of analysis can make part of the challenge experimentally tractable:

$$
\text{Which realized binding organization specifies this experience,}
$$

$$
\text{and which transformations of its physical support predict its changes?}
$$

The proposal has a deliberately strong conceptual ambition. Qualitative character may be constitutively relational: not merely influenced by context after a complete quale has already formed, but individuated through the organization of a situated event. Its empirical ambition is narrower and more immediate. A binding-sensitive model should explain and predict discriminable changes better than competing models under controlled conditions.

This paper develops those ambitions separately. It does not equate having a useful semantic model with having a theory of phenomenal existence.

### 1.1 Contributions

The paper makes six proposed contributions. It identifies a contextual binding event as a candidate explanatory unit for qualitative specificity; separates identity, support, constitution, realization, and evidence; develops local, potentially mixed discrete-continuous descriptions without presupposing independent sensory axes; distinguishes content from system-regime and competence descriptions; defines both intervention-correspondence and complete-path realization tests; and provides an experimental program in which the resulting structure must earn its role against strong alternatives. The source integration assigns responsibilities without treating related author manuscripts as independent empirical corroboration.

The novelty claimed is this particular integration and its explicit research contracts. The paper does not claim to originate relational accounts of experience, multidimensional psychophysics, contextual perception, representational geometry, or causal abstraction.

### 1.2 Source discipline

Four kinds of material are kept distinct. The supplied author manuscripts provide the conceptual lineage and explicitly bounded source commitments [M1-M2, M4-M15]. The earlier draft also cited a Semantic Field Mechanics brief [M3]; that bibliographic lineage is retained, but that separate brief was not newly supplied or re-examined for this revision. The interview provides Hoffman's stated challenge, not proof of his metaphysics or a verified account of the entire consciousness literature [T1]. Published sources provide the prior draft's selected empirical and methodological context [R1-R12]. The literature register is retained rather than presented as a new systematic literature verification. It was not externally revalidated during this closed-corpus pass. New definitions, applications, counterexamples, and tests are identified as constructions of this manuscript. Source discrepancies are recorded in Appendix E rather than silently amended.

The literature discussion is selective rather than systematic. No priority claim or exhaustive novelty search is asserted.

## 2. Four Questions That Must Not Be Collapsed

The phrase *define a quale* can refer to at least four tasks.

**Descriptive specification** identifies the differences that characterize an experience: cooling, aromatic character, intensity, temporal profile, and so on. **Predictive specification** maps independently measured conditions onto reports or other experience-related observations. **Constitutive specification** identifies what organization makes an event the particular experience it is. **Existential explanation** explains why such organization is experienced at all.

These tasks constrain one another but are not interchangeable. A good predictor can leave constitution underdetermined. A detailed description can omit physical realization. A proposed identity between organization and experience can remain empirically ungrounded. The distinction between explaining functions and explaining phenomenal experience is central to Chalmers's formulation of the explanatory problem [R1].

This paper primarily develops descriptive and predictive specification, and formulates a constitutive hypothesis. It does not assume that success at the first two levels settles the fourth.

### 2.1 Semantic, qualitative, and lexical distinctions

A lexical category is a conventional label such as *peppermint*. A semantic binding is a structured, consequential relation within a specified context. A qualitative event is the occurrence of experience whose character is at issue. None is definitionally identical to the others.

The term *semantic* is used here in the framework's broader sense of differential, role-sensitive, constraint-governed organization, not merely as linguistic meaning. Nevertheless, broadening the term cannot do the empirical work. A circuit that discriminates inputs is not thereby shown to feel them. A language model's elaborate description of mint is not itself evidence that it tastes mint. Conversely, lacking the word *mint* does not disqualify a subject from having a mint-related experience.

The research program must therefore remain open to prelinguistic experience, unfamiliar sensations, and experience without explicit conceptual recognition. It must also distinguish a change in what an experience is called from a change in how it feels.

### 2.2 Artifact does not mean fabrication

The initiating intuition describes a quale as a *contextual artifact at its fundamental representation*. Here, *artifact* means a particular constituted product or event, not something unreal, deceptive, or necessarily manufactured by a human.

Three objects must be separated:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{specification of an experience-related structure} \\
&\neq \text{a realization of that structure} \\
&\neq \text{a record describing the realization}
\end{aligned}.
$$

The strong hypothesis concerns the organization of the experiential event. The research implementation manipulates records and models of that organization. Saving a JSON object with a `cooling` field does not instantiate cooling experience.

## 3. Conceptual Lineage and Related Research

### 3.1 Locus, binding, and architectonics

The author's *The Locus of Meaning* treats meaning as constituted through structured binding rather than located exclusively in an object or an interpreter. It distinguishes current realization support, standing structures that preserve possibilities for reactivation, and constitutional invariants across admissible realizations. It also cautions that a semantic domain is not automatically a mathematical manifold [M1, Sections 6-7].

The manuscript stored as *What Locus of Meaning Adds.md* explicitly distinguishes semantic binding, recursive meta-binding, and reflexive meta-binding. Bindings have typed roles, constraints, provenance, context or jurisdiction, and consequences; a binding may itself become an element in a higher-order binding [M2, Abstract]. These are conceptual commitments, not evidence that any particular phenomenal event has been explained.

The later *Semantic Field Mechanics* brief emphasizes transformation laws and invariants, treats semantic architectonics as organization built from bindings, and rejects an additional stored identity-locus primitive unless ablation establishes its necessity. An address is not meaning, and a realization is not its constitution [M3, "Transformation-First Hypothesis" and "Semantic Locus Constraint"].

These distinctions shape the present proposal. They also constrain it. The prior Locus paper explicitly states that semantic binding does not by itself establish consciousness [M1, Section 14.5]. An experiential-binding thesis is consequently an extension requiring independent justification, not a result already contained in the earlier framework.

### 3.2 Geometry and structure are established research directions

Gardenfors's conceptual-space framework represents conceptual information through quality dimensions and geometrical relations [R2]. Representational similarity analysis compares patterns of dissimilarity across neural data, behavior, and computational models [R3]. Neurophenomenal structuralism investigates constraints arising from relations between phenomenal and neural structures [R4]. These precedents already undermine any assertion that consciousness-related science considers only isolated, context-free neural objects.

The present proposal adds a particular emphasis on typed relational roles, contextual constitutions, transformation contracts, and provenance-sensitive comparison. Whether those additions are scientifically useful must be decided by ablation and predictive testing, not by the extensiveness of their vocabulary.

### 3.3 Olfactory and cooling research provide bounded footholds

Howard and colleagues reported correspondence between perceptual odor-quality similarity and distributed fMRI patterns in human posterior piriform cortex [R5]. McKemy and colleagues characterized a receptor activated by menthol and cool-to-cold thermal stimuli, providing a concrete peripheral mechanism relevant to a cooling component of mint-related sensation [R6]. De Araujo and colleagues investigated cognitive modulation of olfactory processing using different verbal descriptions of a test odor [R7]. Lee and colleagues developed a learned odor representation that supports prediction of odor qualities for previously uncharacterized molecules [R8].

These results concern particular mechanisms, perceptual judgments, or predictive representations. They do not establish that mint phenomenology is exhausted by one receptor, one region, one label, or one learned embedding. They do establish that the proposed program should build on existing empirical decompositions rather than portray the field as having no relevant structure to investigate.

### 3.4 Computation and contextual binding are not opposites

Integrated Information Theory 4.0 proposes an account connecting phenomenal properties to intrinsic cause-effect structure, not simply to an arbitrary matrix interpreted by an external observer [R9]. Hoffman and Prakash instead develop a consciousness-first mathematical framework with experiences among its primitives [R10]. The present proposal does not require either approach, and it does not establish their equivalence.

A contextual binding model can be computational, dynamical, mechanistic, or interpreted within another ontology. Computation can represent relations, history, boundaries, embodiment, and feedback. The claim that a context-free description is insufficient therefore does not establish that computational explanation is impossible.

Moreover, bare matrix indices are mathematically unlabeled in any theory. Their operational interpretation, state definition, and causal role matter. It would be unfair to demand explanatory power from an uninterpreted numerical array and treat its silence as a refutation of every functional theory.

### 3.5 Identifiability and causal abstraction

Unsupervised learning does not in general identify uniquely meaningful disentangled factors without additional assumptions; Locatello and colleagues make this limitation explicit in a formal and empirical analysis [R11]. Geiger and colleagues use causal abstraction and interchange interventions to test whether internal neural-network representations realize specified higher-level causal roles [R12].

These two results motivate complementary disciplines here: do not infer natural experiential axes from an arbitrary latent basis, and do not infer causal realization from successful decoding alone.

### 3.7 ACO: complementary regime questions and a substantive boundary

ACO defines meaning-binding through constraint relevance and its persistence under transformation. It expressly distinguishes this usage from semantic, linguistic, or intentional meaning [M6, Section 2]. The expanded Locus manuscript instead imposes a semantic threshold involving typed coherence, differential sensitivity, constraint, uptake, reidentifiability, jurisdiction, and consequence [M2, Section 4.4]. The two usages should therefore be represented as related but nonidentical predicates. A relation can modify continuation possibilities without thereby satisfying every condition of the Locus semantic threshold.

ACO's six local axes are Binding Density, Binding Integration, Binding Stability, Renegotiation Capacity, Meta-Binding, and Epistemic Autonomy. These characterize a system's binding regime, not the aromatic or cooling character of one episode [M6, Section 5]. The expanded Locus paper also distinguishes representation of a system's constraints from authority to revise them and from phenomenal consciousness [M2, Sections 10.4-10.6]. A detailed self-model is not, by that description alone, a source of either amendment authority or felt mint.

There is a genuine difference of target. ACO rejects qualia as a definitional boundary and characterizes them as private, unmeasurable claims, permitting them only as possible correlates of binding regimes [M6, Section 10]. The present paper does not silently replace that statement with a weaker claim. It adopts a different research commitment: public reports, comparisons, and interventions can constrain hypotheses about qualitative specificity, even though they do not provide direct, complete access to another experience. The proposed H-P hypothesis is an additional bridge, not a theorem of ACO.

The older S-to-C-to-I grammar in ACO also differs from the four-stage S/CM/IC/IR grammar in the expanded Locus account. In particular, ACO's Specification language already invokes admissibility, whereas the expanded Locus Specification includes constructible candidates that may be inadmissible [M6, Section 3; M2, Section 12]. This paper uses the latter grammar for research artifacts. It does not claim a lossless conversion of every historical use of the former.

### 3.8 Competence and continuation are different from qualitative content

Semantic Manifold Competence proposes intelligence as the capacity to construct, maintain, navigate, transform, and govern structured meaning while preserving coherence under novelty. Its scope statement explicitly leaves realizing mechanisms in place rather than claiming to replace them [M8, Section 2]. For the present program, remembering a distinction, comparing two reports, applying a category, or planning a transformation are competence tasks. Their success need not establish the phenomenal character of the event they concern.

Dynatology supplies a different object: a state space and relations distinguishing conceivable from admissible transitions [M7, Sections 3 and 8]. Its relevance is not that it has already found mint's physical dynamics. It permits a precise distinction between a describable target and an attainable continuation. Sections 9.6-9.10 use this distinction to formulate bounded realization queries.

The conversation worked example describes a thick origin-binding involving context, history, identity, and future conversation. It also distinguishes a continuation being available from a specific assertion being warranted [M10, "Confidence Is Not Admissibility"]. This is an instructive template: the word *mint* can open a family of candidate distinctions without establishing that any one of them was experienced on a particular trial.

### 3.9 Continuity auditing is not the phenomenal territory

The main SMT manuscript defines its task narrowly: it checks whether declared semantic commitments survive authorized transformations. It expressly disclaims a theory of meaning and an unconditional metric geometry [M9, Abstract; Sections 3 and 21]. Its role here is consequently methodological. A chart conversion can be audited for preservation of recorded distinctions; that audit does not decide which distinctions a subject actually experiences.

There is a difference of scope between this definition and the worked example's statement that SMT describes structured reachability while GMB adds governance [M10, "Advanced Thesis"]. This revision retains the main manuscript's diagnostic definition when using the name SMT. The worked example supplies exposition, not an unnoticed replacement definition. Similarly, a failure of an authorized continuity claim does not by itself show that the underlying experience disappeared. Empirical absence and lack of audit standing have different types.

### 3.10 Binding quality and crystallization concern the research account

SBQ distinguishes binding presence from binding integrity and proposes five interacting dimensions: structural coherence, generativity, stability under perturbation, constraint alignment, and minimal distortion [M11, Sections 2-3]. These can motivate a research-model evaluation profile, but their operationalization for qualia studies is new work. The revision notes are editorial instructions, not independent confirmation of the SBQ theory; their terminology changes and unresolved overlay terminology are recorded as such [M12].

CBT contributes a loop from ambiguity through anchor resolution and realization to evidence and amendment, while warning against premature collapse and excessive fragmentation [M13, Sections 1 and 9]. CSC distinguishes a canonical artifact from one admitted to binding status and explicitly says admission does not guarantee correctness [M14, Sections 4-5]. Meaning Crystals examines representational constraints and "oracle surfaces," where an apparently explicit account still depends on undeclared interpretation [M15, Section 5].

Their proposed research use is to stabilize a testable account without erasing its uncertainty. A formal model can be frozen for evaluation while remaining a hypothesis. A raw report can be preserved when a coordinate schema cannot express it. Neither freezing nor admission turns a model into a conscious event. The source's crystallization language is not a thermodynamic mechanism [M14, Section 4.4]; the oscillatory language in Meaning as Binding and the semantic-energy analogy in SBQ likewise supply no measured neural bridge [M4, Section 8; M11, Section 4].

### 3.11 GMB and the universal governed semantic model

The **Governed Meaning Base (GMB)** is the current name in the author-confirmed historical lineage `CCF -> CMB -> GMB`. In this research program, GMB belongs to the architecture of the **research account**. It is not proposed as a neural substrate, a phenomenal field, or a container that stores qualia. A GMB could retain competing hypotheses, exact sources, observations, models, evidence, standing, counterevidence, and revision relations without making any retained claim true.

The universal governed semantic model can represent this paper through separately typed records:

- **Record:** experiential episode  
  **What it represents:** the participant- and context-indexed target event  
  **What it must not be collapsed into:** a report, category label, or database row

- **Record:** observation  
  **What it represents:** report, comparison, behavior, physiological measure, or neural measure  
  **What it must not be collapsed into:** transparent access to the episode

- **Record:** binding hypothesis  
  **What it represents:** a proposed organization of roles, relations, context, and history  
  **What it must not be collapsed into:** established phenomenal constitution

- **Record:** physical state  
  **What it represents:** an independently characterized substrate state or trajectory  
  **What it must not be collapsed into:** its measured projection or experiential descriptor

- **Record:** realization relation  
  **What it represents:** a scoped mapping or intervention-sensitive bridge  
  **What it must not be collapsed into:** identity merely because decoding succeeds

- **Record:** evidence  
  **What it represents:** a result with method, scope, uncertainty, and alternatives  
  **What it must not be collapsed into:** universal corroboration by the surrounding corpus

- **Record:** source  
  **What it represents:** exact artifact and custody metadata  
  **What it must not be collapsed into:** logical-work identity, truth, or authority

- **Record:** standing  
  **What it represents:** proposed, tested, supported, weakened, refused, superseded, or unresolved status  
  **What it must not be collapsed into:** the underlying experience itself

This separation lets Hyperpedia or another pedagogical projection explain the program while retaining links back to exact evidence and unresolved alternatives. The projection may speak **about** a hypothesized experiential organization; it does not speak **from within** another subject's experience.

## 4. The Contextual Experiential-Binding Hypothesis

Let an experiential episode be indexed by a subject or system $a$, an interval $I$, and an experimental jurisdiction $J$. A jurisdiction specifies the conditions under which a claim is intended to hold, including population, task, measurement procedure, and relevant environmental range.

Let $\mathcal{B}_e$ denote the hypothesized actually realized binding organization of episode $e$. Let $\mathcal{A}_e$ denote its architectonic description: the organized arrangement of components, relational roles, constraints, and transformation possibilities. Let $Q_e$ denote the qualitative event, without assuming that it is directly measurable as a vector.

The central constitutive hypothesis is:

$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
\text{The qualitative specificity of }Q_e\text{ is constituted by}\\
\mathcal{B}_e\text{ within its operative context and history.}
\end{gathered}}
\tag{1}
$$

Equation (1) is a hypothesis in words, not an identity proved by notation. A mathematical symbol for binding does not provide a mechanism simply by being placed beside a symbol for experience.

### 4.1 Three nested commitments

**H-S: structural adequacy.** A context-indexed binding description captures reproducible distinctions in experience-related observations better than a flat vocabulary alone.

**H-R: realization relevance.** Some independently characterized substrate transformations systematically realize the changes predicted by a specified binding model, including under interventions and held-out contexts.

**H-P: phenomenal constitution.** The relevant realized binding organization constitutes qualitative specificity itself, rather than merely a correlated capacity to discriminate, categorize, remember, or report it.

H-S can hold while H-R fails. H-R can receive substantial support while H-P remains underdetermined. Even H-P, if established for qualitative specificity, would not automatically imply that any instance of semantic binding is sufficient for phenomenal existence.

The deliberately stronger universal thesis,

$$
\text{semantic binding exists}\Longrightarrow\text{phenomenal experience exists},
\tag{2}
$$

is not assumed. The present experiment does not test universal consciousness, panpsychism, or a global metaphysics of matter.

### 4.2 What the hypothesis excludes

For a specified implementation to be substantive, it must exclude possible outcomes. A model may, for example, predict that a relation-sensitive perturbation changes a particular quality profile even when component marginals remain approximately unchanged. It may predict that one context changes the effect of an intensity manipulation, while a matched irrelevant context does not. It may predict that a particular order of interventions matters.

If the same architecture can accommodate every result merely by adding an unmeasured contextual variable, it has ceased to function as an empirical explanation. Contextual boundaries, parameter counts, and revision rules must therefore be fixed before confirmatory testing.

## 5. Locus Is Not a Container: A Typed Ontology

### 5.1 Identity, support, and content

An identity address $\iota_e$ allows an episode, concept, or record to be referred to. It does not contain a quality. The support locus concerns the components and relations relevant to sustaining an event or a description of that event. The constitutional locus concerns invariants that define a proposed family of realizations. The realized event is neither its address nor its database entry.

A useful research record is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_e= \langle &\iota_e, \widehat{\mathcal{A}}_e, c_e, h_e, J_e, \\
&\mathcal{R}_e, \mathcal{E}_e\rangle
\end{aligned}\tag{3},
$$

where $\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_e$ is an estimated architectonic description, $c_e$ and $h_e$ are declared contextual and historical variables, $\mathcal{R}_e$ specifies the proposed realization bridge, and $\mathcal{E}_e$ records evidence and uncertainty. Hats distinguish estimates from hypothesized underlying organization.

Equation (3) defines a descriptive artifact. It is not a recipe for manufacturing a conscious subject.

### 5.2 Typed binding hypergraphs

Represent a candidate architectonic by a typed attributed hypergraph:

$$
G_e=(V_e,E_e,\tau_e,\rho_e,\alpha_e).
\tag{4}
$$

Here $V_e$ contains candidate components; $E_e$ contains relations that may involve several components; $\tau_e$ assigns types; $\rho_e$ assigns participant roles within each relation; and $\alpha_e$ contains attributes such as values, units, time intervals, and uncertainty.

A binding involving sensory input, expected source, and perceived location is not equivalent to an unordered bag containing those three items. Role assignments are part of the hypothesis. A component serving as a current sensory input is not interchangeable with the same component represented as a memory or an instruction.

Each modeled binding also carries an epistemic descriptor:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat b= \langle &\text{relata}, \text{roles}, \text{context}, \\
&\text{interval}, \text{constraint}, \text{provenance}, \\
&\text{standing}\rangle
\end{aligned}\tag{5}.
$$

Provenance and standing belong to the research account, not necessarily to the organism's mechanism. It would be a category mistake to claim that a neuron needs an administrative authorization record before a sensation can occur.

### 5.3 Bounded support rather than universal context

A proposed support set should be no larger than its explanatory role warrants. Relevant history may be encoded in present adaptation or memory states; it need not be a complete chronological archive. A cultural label may influence a task through expectation without being a necessary constituent of every sensory event. An external mint leaf may be a stimulus source without being part of every current internal realization after the leaf is removed.

For a declared model class and intervention family $\mathcal{I}_J$, define an operational support as a set of variables sufficient for a specified predictive or interventional task. Removing a variable that changes neither task performance nor the claimed intervention structure supplies evidence against its necessity *within that task*. It does not prove universal dispensability.

This formulation avoids converting distributed support into the assertion that everything in the universe participates equally in every quale.

### 5.4 Recursive and reflexive binding

A subject may experience a cooling sensation and subsequently recognize it as mint, associate it with toothpaste, or evaluate the recognition itself. These can be modeled as successive or interacting bindings:

$$
\mathcal B_0
\longrightarrow
\mathcal B_1(\mathcal B_0,\text{category})
\longrightarrow
\mathcal B_2(\mathcal B_1,\text{self-assessment}).
\tag{6}
$$

The notation specifies higher-order organization, not a compulsory ladder that every experience must ascend. Elementary experience need not include a reflective concept of itself. Experimental reporting may introduce higher-order bindings that were not present in the original event; this is a measurement issue, not an inconvenience to ignore.

### 5.5 Identity has several noninterchangeable targets

GTK treats the resolved semantic binding as the lexical unit, accompanied by aliases and competing bindings; its invariant floor attaches to that binding, not to the raw word [M5, Sections 1-3]. Applying that structure to the research registry requires a further distinction between a rich lexical entry and the opaque address used to retrieve it. The entry can contain semantic commitments; an address alone cannot supply them.

A proposed identity discipline distinguishes at least five object kinds: a category such as mint-related character; a preparation or source; a delivered-stimulus event; an experiential episode; and a measurement or documentary record. A sixth kind, the binding hypothesis, describes relations among these without being identical to any of them. Thus two trials can share a preparation and category while differing in delivered stimulus, episode identity, or reported character.

The principal relations are also typed:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{SameCategory}_J(e_1,e_2)&\not\Rightarrow\operatorname{SameEpisode}(e_1,e_2),\\
\operatorname{SameSource}(e_1,e_2)&\not\Rightarrow\operatorname{SameProfile}_J(e_1,e_2),\\
\operatorname{MatchedObservations}_J(e_1,e_2)&\not\Rightarrow\operatorname{PhenomenallyIdentical}(e_1,e_2).
\end{aligned}
$$

These are restrictions on inference, not claims that the pairs can never coincide. An aftertaste continuing through time can retain one episode identifier while its measured profile changes. A repeated mint exposure can generate a different episode with a similar profile. The study must declare its temporal individuation rule rather than letting database row boundaries define experience.

A content digest identifies particular serialized bytes or a declared canonical form. A stable reference identifies the record lineage or entity to which revisions refer. A new digest need not mean a new subject or a new experience. Conversely, reusing a reference does not prove that the underlying binding constitution was preserved. This separation is compatible with GTK's content-addressed entry revisions [M5, Section 4], but must be implemented explicitly rather than treating every kind of identity as one hash.

### 5.6 Three loci in the mint example

The source distinction among constitutional, standing, and realization loci [M1, Section 6; M2, Section 5.4] supports a useful experiential application. A proposed constitutional locus specifies the invariants of a bounded family of episodes. A proposed standing locus comprises relevant dispositions or preserved conditions for later reactivation. A proposed realization locus identifies the actual support of a particular ongoing episode. These are hypotheses about different explanatory roles, not three necessarily disjoint physical regions.

A memory of mint, the ability to recognize mint, and a present mint-related sensation can consequently share structures without being treated as one event. Nor does semantic recurrence prove that the same phenomenal quality has recurred. The research task is to identify which relations remain stable, which change, and which distinctions the available observations can support.

Provenance, an investigator's approval, and the validity of a measurement belong to the account of these loci. They should not be inserted into the proposed biological mechanism unless a separately specified causal pathway makes them relevant, for example through an instruction that changes a participant's expectation. The mere existence of an approval record is not such a pathway.

## 6. Mint as a Family of Contextual Realizations

### 6.1 A multidimensional target without invented coordinates

The everyday phrase *taste of mint* is retained for continuity with the motivating question. Operationally, the research target should distinguish possible gustatory, olfactory, cooling or irritation-related, oral-somatosensory, and temporal contributions. The menthol-sensitive cold-receptor finding supplies one particular physiological foothold, not a reduction of the entire experience to cooling [R6].

Candidate descriptive dimensions include perceived overall intensity, cooling, aromatic character, sweetness, bitterness, perceived freshness, oral localization, texture, temporal persistence, familiarity, and pleasantness. Attention, expectation, recent exposure, and bodily state may be modeled as context, as components of wider experiential organization, or both under explicitly different analyses. They should not silently change roles between training and testing.

These candidate dimensions are not established orthogonal primitives. Some may be redundant, composite, culturally variable, inaccessible to a particular measurement, or irrelevant in a given jurisdiction. A sparse descriptor is preferable to an elaborate inventory that cannot be independently assessed.

### 6.2 Contrasts and their interpretation

- **Contrast:** Weak versus strong mint  
  **Candidate change:** Intensity trajectory, potentially with quality and irritation changes  
  **What must not be assumed:** A concentration increase affects only one experiential dimension

- **Contrast:** Peppermint versus spearmint  
  **Candidate change:** Discriminable aromatic and cooling profiles under a specified preparation  
  **What must not be assumed:** A universal numerical distance or fixed sweetness ranking

- **Contrast:** Fresh versus dried mint  
  **Candidate change:** A combination of preparation, release, texture, and contextual differences  
  **What must not be assumed:** Freshness is a single independent physical variable

- **Contrast:** Fresh versus decay-associated mint  
  **Candidate change:** Preservation of some identity cues with additional aversive or unfamiliar qualities  
  **What must not be assumed:** Rotting material is safe to taste or a one-axis stimulus manipulation

- **Contrast:** Onset versus aftertaste  
  **Candidate change:** Time-dependent change, persistence, and adaptation  
  **What must not be assumed:** A static average adequately describes the event

- **Contrast:** Same preparation under different expectations  
  **Candidate change:** A possible change in categorization, report, or experience  
  **What must not be assumed:** Any report change proves a phenomenal change

The rows are experimental contrasts, not claims that their mechanisms or outcomes are already known. Botanical identity, preparation, chemical composition, delivered stimulus, category label, and phenomenal character should be recorded separately.

For decay-associated cases, the study should use only institutionally approved safe representations or substitutes. The conceptual contrast does not authorize tasting spoiled material or exposing participants to uncontrolled biological hazards.

### 6.3 Mint as a region, not necessarily an atom

Let $\mathcal Q_{a,J}$ be a provisional space of within-subject experiential distinctions under jurisdiction $J$. A lexical mint category can be modeled as a region:

$$
\mathcal M_{a,J}\subseteq\mathcal Q_{a,J}.
\tag{7}
$$

The boundary may be uncertain, context-sensitive, or graded. It is not assumed to be convex, connected, or identical across people. An unfamiliar sensation may be representable without a stable lexical category. Conversely, two discriminably different events may receive the same label.

A particular episode is represented, where supported, by a trajectory:

$$
q_e:I\rightarrow\mathcal Q_{a,J}.
\tag{8}
$$

This is a coordinate-level representation of the event, not the event itself. Point estimates should be replaced by uncertainty distributions when the data warrant no unique placement.

## 7. Orthogonal Dimensions: Four Different Claims

The expression *orthogonal dimensions* can be valuable only if its meaning is controlled.

### 7.1 Typed dimensional distinction

Two properties can be separately named and assigned distinct measurement procedures. Aromatic identity and cooling intensity are conceptually distinguishable. This is a type distinction, not proof that either can vary independently of the other.

### 7.2 Statistical independence and decorrelation

For measured descriptors $q_i$ and $q_j$, zero covariance is not generally equivalent to independence:

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(q_i,q_j)=0
\ \not\Longrightarrow\
p(q_i,q_j)=p(q_i)p(q_j).
\tag{9}
$$

A rotated or whitened coordinate system can have uncorrelated components without revealing independent sensory mechanisms. The learned basis also depends on sampling, task, and model assumptions [R11].

### 7.3 Geometric orthogonality

Where a differentiable local chart and a justified metric $g$ exist, tangent directions $u$ and $v$ are orthogonal when

$$
u^\top g(q)v=0.
\tag{10}
$$

Neither the metric nor its interpretation is supplied by writing a feature list. It might be derived from a specified discrimination model, with uncertainty and a local domain of validity. Euclidean geometry is an optional model, not a default fact about experience.

### 7.4 Causal selectivity

Let $u_k$ denote a controlled intervention parameter and $\widehat q_i$ an estimated coordinate. A local intervention-response matrix is

$$
J_{ik}=\frac{\partial\mathbb E[\widehat q_i\mid do(u)]}{\partial u_k}.
\tag{11}
$$

A manipulation aimed at dimension $k$ is selectively effective only relative to declared on-target and off-target criteria. A possible diagnostic is

$$
L_k=
\frac{\sum_{i\notin T_k}w_i|\Delta\widehat q_i|}
{\epsilon+\sum_{i\in T_k}w_i|\Delta\widehat q_i|},
\tag{12}
$$

where $T_k$ is the intended target set and scales, weights, and $\epsilon$ are fixed in advance. A low value does not prove perfect independence; a high value can reveal coupled dimensions, a poor intervention, or an incomplete model.

Selectivity also requires a nontrivial on-target effect. A manipulation that changes nothing should not receive a high success score simply because it has no off-target effects.

### 7.5 Dependency is a finding, not a failure of the whole program

Increasing a delivered mint stimulus might alter intensity, cooling, quality, or pleasantness together. Such coupling does not automatically refute a binding framework. It does refute a particular claim of selective control or separability when that claim was preregistered.

Conversely, the framework must not become immune to falsification by treating every coupling as a victory. The empirical commitments concern a specific graph, dimension set, and transformation family. Revisions must be recorded as revisions and evaluated on new data.

### 7.6 Content, regime, competence, and evidence are different coordinate families

The source integration introduces four descriptive families:

$$
\mathcal D_e=\langle q_e,r_e,k_e,\varepsilon_e\rangle.
$$

Here $q_e$ describes the episode's candidate qualitative profile; $r_e$ describes the system's candidate binding regime; $k_e$ describes competence on declared operations; and $\varepsilon_e$ describes the evidence available to the investigator. This tuple is bookkeeping, not an assertion of a product geometry, causal independence, or full observability.

For $r_e$, the source vocabulary is ACO's six axes. For $k_e$, it is the construction, maintenance, navigation, transformation, and governance capacities of Semantic Manifold Competence [M6, Section 5; M8, Section 2.1]. Values must remain unassigned until independently operationalized. A number of self-referential sentences is not automatically a measurement of Meta-Binding; a persistent category label is not automatically a measurement of Binding Stability.

The four families can interact. A modeled capacity to retain distinctions might affect a delayed report, and an expectation might affect a content judgment. These are candidate pathways to test, not reasons to collapse the variables into one scale. A richer description of a system's regime does not entail stronger mint, and a more complete evidence record does not entail a more intense or more conscious episode.

A useful comparative design therefore asks whether independently measured regime or competence variables explain additional variance or transfer beyond sensory conditions and history-aware baselines. It separately tests whether any apparent gain is confined to reporting. No six-axis threshold is proposed as necessary or sufficient for mint experience.

## 8. Experiential Atlases, Not an Assumed Universal Manifold

### 8.1 Local charts

A chart is a bounded representational procedure:

$$
\phi_{a,J}:\mathcal E_{a,J}\rightarrow\mathcal Z_{a,J},
\tag{13}
$$

where $\mathcal E_{a,J}$ is a class of episodes and $\mathcal Z_{a,J}$ can contain continuous coordinates, ordinal categories, discrete relations, and missing values. A conventional smooth manifold is a special case, requiring additional mathematical and empirical justification.

For example, intensity ratings might be modeled ordinally while a stimulus-source relation is categorical and timing is continuous. It would be inappropriate to force all three into an unqualified Euclidean distance just because software accepts a numeric array.

### 8.2 Cross-context and cross-person maps

When two charts overlap, a transport map may be estimated:

$$
T_{J\rightarrow K}:\mathcal Z_{a,J}\dashrightarrow\mathcal Z_{a,K}.
\tag{14}
$$

The dashed arrow denotes a partial map: some states may not transport, and uncertainty can make the image a distribution rather than a point. A cross-person map adds an independently calibrated subject index. It should not be inferred from identical verbal labels alone.

Transport is evaluated by preservation of declared relations, rankings, or intervention responses on held-out episodes. Failure to establish a map is an informative result. It may reflect measurement incompatibility, inadequate data, individual differences, or a genuine limit of the shared model.

### 8.3 Compatibility without forced gluing

The Locus framework's atlas and presheaf vocabulary suggests comparing local descriptions on their overlaps [M1, Section 7]. A disciplined use here is simply to require that two transported descriptions agree on shared measured quantities within declared tolerances before treating them as one coherent account.

A full sheaf formulation would additionally require a specified context structure, restriction maps, and a justified gluing condition. Those are not established merely by drawing overlapping circles. Nor does failure to glue constitute evidence of quantum contextuality, nonlocal consciousness, or a nonphysical substrate.

### 8.4 Historical dependence and path order

Let $T_1$ and $T_2$ be context-changing or stimulus-changing operations. A dynamic binding model may predict

$$
T_2\circ T_1(q)\neq T_1\circ T_2(q).
\tag{15}
$$

An order effect can motivate a history-sensitive model, but does not uniquely support the present framework. Adaptation, memory, response strategies, and other dynamical explanations must remain active competitors. The binding account earns support only when its specified organization predicts the order effect more accurately or parsimoniously than those alternatives.

A trajectory need not be Markovian in the chosen descriptors. If omitted history matters, one can augment the state or use an explicit history-dependent model. State augmentation is a modeling operation, not a proof that the resulting state is phenomenally complete.

## 9. The Realization Problem

### 9.1 Physical state, measured state, and experiential descriptor

Let $z_t$ denote the relevant physical state at a declared resolution. Let $x_t$ denote a measurement of it:

$$
x_t=M(z_t,\eta_t),
\tag{16}
$$

where $M$ is an instrument model and $\eta_t$ includes measurement noise and nuisance factors. Let $y_t$ denote experience-related observations such as a rating, a comparison judgment, a choice, or a timed report.

The proposed realization relation is

$$
\mathcal R_J\subseteq
\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{physical},J}\times
\mathcal B_J\times
\mathcal E_J.
\tag{17}
$$

It relates physical support, binding organization, and an experiential episode. It is written as a relation rather than a function because many-to-one realization, incomplete specification, and uncertainty are all possible. A function becomes appropriate only after the necessary sufficiency assumptions are stated.

The operationally estimable object is more modest:

$$
\widehat p(y_{t:t+\Delta}\mid x_{\leq t},c_{\leq t},h_{\leq t},u_{\leq t}).
\tag{18}
$$

This predicts observations under specified conditions. It is not automatically an estimator of an independently accessible phenomenal state.

### 9.2 What it means to plot electronics or neurons

The initiating proposal asks for structures that permit plotting electrical or neural changes corresponding to qualitative realization. The appropriate physical variables depend on scale: a computational model's state variables, circuit voltages, ion-channel dynamics, population activity, or an instrument's indirect measurement. The proposal does not require tracking every electron, and changes in neuronal activity should not be described as whole neurons moving through a semantic space.

Instrument variables are not interchangeable with microscopic mechanisms. A bridge from fMRI patterns to reported odor similarity, for example, is a bridge at the measurement level unless further mechanistic evidence is supplied. The spatial and temporal transformations involved in neural measurement are explicitly relevant to representational analysis [R3].

A useful model may operate at a coarse level. Its usefulness does not license claims about finer-scale physical causes it did not measure.

### 9.3 No requirement of global distance preservation

It is tempting to demand

$$
d_{\mathrm{physical}}(z_i,z_j)\approx d_{\mathrm{experience}}(q_i,q_j).
\tag{19}
$$

But even a correct realization relation need not preserve raw distances. Redundant microstates can realize the same higher-level condition. Measurement can distort distances. The relation can be nonlinear, history-dependent, and subject-specific.

The stronger scientific target is therefore not global geometric resemblance but *specified transformation correspondence*. A physical manipulation should have the high-level consequences predicted by the bridge, with known bounds and exceptions.

### 9.4 Intervention-commutation

Let $M_\mu$ be a low-level causal model and $M_B$ a higher-level binding model. Let $\rho$ map low-level states into higher-level variables, and let $\omega$ map an allowed low-level intervention $i$ into its hypothesized high-level counterpart. A realization test compares

$$
\rho_*P_{M_\mu}^{do(i)}
\quad\text{with}\quad
P_{M_B}^{do(\omega(i))},
\tag{20}
$$

where $\rho_*$ denotes the induced distribution after mapping. The disagreement can be quantified as

$$
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{comm}}(i)=
D\left(\rho_*P_{M_\mu}^{do(i)},
P_{M_B}^{do(\omega(i))}\right),
\tag{21}
$$

using a divergence or distance appropriate to the variables and sampling design. Causal-abstraction methods provide a precedent for testing such higher- and lower-level alignments [R12].

A small commutation error supports the tested correspondence. It does not by itself establish that the high-level variables exhaust experience. Interventions, mappings, and targets must be specified independently of the held-out outcomes; otherwise an exact fit can be engineered by construction.

### 9.5 No guaranteed reverse compiler

A forward prediction model does not imply that any desired quality profile can be induced. A proposed inverse problem is

$$
u^*=\arg\min_{u\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{allowed}}}
\mathbb E\left[d(\widehat q_{t+\Delta},q^*)\mid do(u)\right]
+\lambda\operatorname{Cost}(u).
\tag{22}
$$

This formulation requires a causal model, a feasible target, and an independently authorized intervention set. The solution may be nonunique or nonexistent. A structurally incompatible target must be refused rather than approximated while reporting success.

The word *compiler* is thus a useful analogy for a validated translation between levels, not evidence that experiences can already be programmed.

### 9.6 Dynatological reachability under a declared physical model

Following Dynatology's transition-system core, let

$$
\mathcal D_{\mu,J}=(Z,\Omega_\mu,A_\mu)
$$

be a bounded model of physical or simulated states under jurisdiction $J$. Here $\Omega_\mu$ records model-conceivable transitions and $A_\mu\subseteq\Omega_\mu$ the transitions admitted by the specified dynamical model. Where actions matter, write $A_\mu^u$ for the transition relation under action $u$. None of these symbols asserts that all relevant physical states or actions have been discovered [M7, Sections 3, 8, and 25].

Physical feasibility, evidentiary availability, and execution permission remain separate. A researcher may lack evidence for a feasible transition. A well-supported feasible transition may be outside the authorized protocol. A permitted model query may return no path. The graph used for a particular execution request can intersect these constraints, but the record must retain the result of each check rather than call every excluded edge physically impossible.

For finite nonnegative edge costs, define a directed path cost by minimizing their sum over admitted paths. No path gives infinite cost within the declared model and horizon. This is not asserted to be a symmetric phenomenal distance. A cheaply reachable descriptor can be very dissimilar; a similar descriptor can be unreachable by the available controls. A permitted zero-step identity path also does not establish indefinite viability.

### 9.7 A projected path can be a false promise

Let $\rho:Z\rightarrow\widehat Q$ be a fixed map into a space of modeled experiential descriptors. An existentially projected transition is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(q,u,q')\in A_Q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists z, \\
&z'\in Z:\rho(z)=q, \ \rho(z')=q', \ (z,z')\in A_\mu^u
\end{aligned}.
$$

This is a useful coarse representation, but it can join witnesses that do not join in the underlying dynamics. Consider four states with exactly these relevant transitions:

$$
a\xrightarrow{u}b,\qquad c\xrightarrow{v}d,
$$

and projections

$$
\rho(a)=q_0,\quad\rho(b)=\rho(c)=q_1,\quad\rho(d)=q_2.
$$

The projected graph contains $q_0\xrightarrow{u}q_1\xrightarrow{v}q_2$. Nevertheless, no corresponding two-action path starts at $a$: the first step ends at $b$, whereas the second edge has witness $c$. Equality of a descriptor has been mistaken for interchangeability of underlying states.

This counterexample is a new application of the supplied transition and path-governance ideas, not an empirical finding about mint. In a future experiment, the concealed difference might be an independently measured adaptation state or other history variable. The example itself does not establish which variable matters.

An extra transition $b\rightarrow c$ would not retroactively make the original two-step sequence valid. It would be an additional action or dynamical interval requiring its own timing, costs, and evidence. Likewise, an experimental reset between separately validated transitions must appear in the complete path. It cannot be silently inserted by the analysis.

### 9.8 Complete-path realization and its quantifiers

For a descriptor/action sequence

$$
\pi_Q=(q_0,u_0,q_1,\ldots,u_{n-1},q_n)
$$

and a declared initial-state set $Z_0$, define compatible endpoint sets recursively:

$$
L_0=Z_0\cap\rho^{-1}(q_0),
$$

$$
L_{j+1}=\left\{z'\in\rho^{-1}(q_{j+1}):
\exists z\in L_j,\ (z,z')\in A_\mu^{u_j}\right\}.
$$

A nonempty $L_n$ witnesses at least one complete model path consistent with the specified sequence and initial set. The qualifier *at least one* matters. The actual starting state may not be the successful member of $Z_0$. Nondeterministic dynamics may also admit failing outcomes alongside the witness. A robust-control or probabilistic-success claim requires a different quantified test and, for probabilities, a calibrated stochastic model.

The recurrence addresses fixed finite paths with endpoint-state sufficiency. A cumulative resource limit or history-dependent obligation requires an augmented state or retained path monitor. If two individually affordable transitions cost two units each but the whole-path budget is three, the sequence fails the budget even when its physical endpoint sequence exists. SMT's independent path-level contract is directly relevant to documenting this distinction [M9, Section 11].

A complete bounded model with no witness warrants *no lift in this model*. An incomplete observed graph with no witness warrants an unresolved realization query, not physical impossibility. A model witness is also not an observed physical execution. These are separate evidence levels.

### 9.9 Continuation-sensitive refinement

A descriptor map intended for sequential prediction must retain enough state to preserve the continuations relevant to that prediction. One strong sufficient condition is the local lifting condition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\forall z,u,q':\ (\rho(z),u,q')\in A_Q \ \\
&\Longrightarrow \ \exists z':\ (z,z')\in A_\mu^u\ \\
&\land \ \rho(z')=q'
\end{aligned}.
$$

Under that condition, every finite projected path can be lifted from every compatible initial state, by successive selection of witnesses. The condition is stronger than existential projection and need not hold in realistic coarse charts. It is a sufficient modeling contract, not a universal requirement for useful descriptive coordinates.

When it fails, the research program has several options: restrict the chart's claim to description; retain a distribution over hidden states; augment the state with independently justified history; or refine a merged descriptor into continuation-distinct classes. These options must compete on frozen sequential holdouts. Adding a hidden variable after every failure would make the account nonpredictive.

A crucial consequence is that *phenomenologically matched according to current measurements* need not mean *causally interchangeable for the next intervention*. A content chart can be adequate for similarity judgments while inadequate for intervention planning. Rejecting its use for planning does not establish that its descriptive distinctions are false.

### 9.10 Viability is an optional dynamical property, not a phenomenal verdict

Dynatology defines viability through the existence of an infinite admitted continuation [M7, Section 12]. For a finite model, this is equivalent to reaching a directed cycle, including a self-loop. The source's fixed-point deletion rule supports this definition; its separate closed-component algorithm wording requires the qualification recorded in Appendix E.

This criterion is existential. A state with a self-loop and an exit to a terminal state is viable in that sense because continuation can remain on the loop. It is not necessarily safe under every controller or almost surely persistent under stochastic selection. Nor is a terminal state conscious or unconscious because of its graph position. The optional computation is useful only for explicitly declared questions about sustained model continuation.

The finite fixture in the companion brief checks this distinction and the path counterexample. It does not analyze an actual neural viability kernel or provide a biological survival prediction.

## 10. Measurement: Experience Is Not Its Report

### 10.1 A measurement model

Suppose $q_e$ is a latent coordinate representation of an episode. A report model may be written

$$
y_e\sim p_\theta(y\mid q_e,\ell_e,d_e,k_e),
\tag{23}
$$

where $\ell_e$ is the response vocabulary, $d_e$ the decision setting, and $k_e$ the reporting task. A context manipulation can affect these variables without necessarily changing the underlying experience. Conversely, experience can change without a particular response instrument detecting it.

This is why label effects are relevant but not decisive. Research on cognitive modulation of olfactory processing motivates controlling verbal context [R7]; it does not authorize treating every verbal difference as a direct readout of phenomenology.

### 10.2 Triangulation without fictitious access

The proposed battery combines several imperfect measures: repeated intensity and quality ratings, pairwise or triadic similarity judgments, discrimination choices, temporal descriptions, confidence assessments, and selected physiological or neural observations. Their convergence can strengthen an interpretation. Their disagreement must be retained.

No channel is automatically a phenomenal ground truth. A classifier output is not an independent witness when it was trained on the same labels used to define the target. Multiple questions answered in one response session are not independent observers merely because they occupy different columns in a file.

Within-subject calibration is the initial priority. Cross-subject alignment is an additional hypothesis with its own validation burden. A shared word, shared stimulus, or shared coordinate value is insufficient evidence of identical experiences across people.

### 10.3 Representational residuals and oracle surfaces

Meaning Crystals identifies schema insufficiency, unrepresentable concepts, and undeclared interpretive assumptions as possible oracle surfaces [M15, Section 5]. Applied to measurement, the practical requirement is to preserve a participant's original description, its elicitation procedure, and the normalization that produced a coordinate record. A phrase that does not fit the current atlas is a representational residual, not automatically noise or zero intensity.

The study should distinguish a participant declining to report, being unable to use the requested vocabulary, having an inapplicable dimension, and supplying a description that the schema cannot encode. These conditions have different evidentiary implications. Original descriptions are not themselves infallible phenomenal ground truth; retaining them makes interpretation inspectable rather than resolving the problem by decree.

Alternative schemas can be compared for held-out reconstruction of declared distinctions, discriminatory usefulness, and uncertainty calibration. A schema that silently deletes residuals may appear more stable precisely because it cannot represent its own failures.

### 10.4 Model identification and sensitivity

The analysis should compare alternative measurement models, including models in which contextual effects act predominantly through response criteria. A phenomenal interpretation gains credibility only to the extent that it survives plausible alternatives and predicts evidence not used to construct it.

A useful sensitivity analysis changes the assumed reliability of reports, permits subject-specific scale usage, and tests how much unmeasured confounding would be required to explain the apparent bridge. Where several latent models fit equally well, the appropriate output is a set of admissible explanations, not an artificially precise latent coordinate.

## 11. Limited Formal Results

The following propositions are mathematical consequences of their assumptions. They are not empirical results about mint and are not asserted to be novel theorems.

### 11.1 Proposition 1: Address-renaming invariance

Let $D$ be a well-formed descriptive artifact. Suppose an injective renaming $\sigma$ changes identity addresses and all corresponding references while preserving typed roles, values, and relationships. If the prediction and validation procedures depend only on that preserved structure, then

$$
F(\sigma D)=F(D).
\tag{24}
$$

**Proof.** By assumption, every argument inspected by $F$ is preserved, and addresses have no independent semantic payload. Thus the renaming does not change any relevant input. The outputs are equal. If the artifact is serialized differently, its ordinary file hash can nevertheless change. $\square$

This proposition operationalizes the distinction between identity addressing and content. A model that uses accidental record identifiers to improve prediction violates the intended invariance and may be exploiting leakage.

### 11.2 Proposition 2: Observational coordinates are not uniquely identified

Suppose a latent model has state $q$, transition law $K$, and observation law $p(y\mid q)$. For any invertible transformation $g$, define $q'=g(q)$, transform the transition law by $g$, and set

$$
p'(y\mid q')=p(y\mid g^{-1}(q')).
\tag{25}
$$

The transformed model induces the same observational distribution.

**Proof.** Substitute $q=g^{-1}(q')$ in the latent-state construction and transport the initial and transition distributions accordingly. The observed outputs retain their original law. $\square$

Therefore, an observationally successful embedding does not uniquely identify intrinsic mint coordinates. Calibration, constraints, and interventions can restrict this freedom but do not remove every interpretive question. This elementary argument is consistent with broader identifiability concerns in representation learning [R11].

### 11.3 Proposition 3: Local controllability is conditional

Assume a differentiable, locally valid map $f:u\mapsto\mathbb E[\widehat q\mid do(u)]$ with Jacobian $J$ at $u_0$. A small desired change $\delta q$ is achievable to first order by an unconstrained local perturbation only if

$$
\delta q\in\operatorname{im}(J).
\tag{26}
$$

**Proof.** First-order changes have the form $J\delta u$. Their set is the image of $J$. $\square$

Even membership in this image is not sufficient for real-world feasibility: safety bounds, parameter limits, nonlinear effects, and uncertainty may preclude the intervention. The proposition supports refusal of unsupported inverse claims, not a promise of controllable qualia.

### 11.4 Proposition 4: Matched marginals need not determine binding-sensitive outputs

Let two variables $a$ and $b$ occupy different roles and define

$$
F(a,b)=(a,b,ab).
\tag{27}
$$

Then $F(a,b)\neq F(b,a)$ when $a\neq b$, although the unordered multiset of component values is unchanged.

This elementary counterexample shows why an unordered component inventory need not determine a role-sensitive output. It does not show that actual mint experience has this form. The empirical question is whether a similarly specific relational distinction improves prediction under controlled conditions.

### 11.5 Operational equivalence is not phenomenal identity

For an exactly specified intervention family, two implementations may be called operationally equivalent when they yield the same distributions over all declared observables under corresponding interventions. That is a genuine equivalence relation under suitable fixed definitions.

Approximate indistinguishability within a tolerance is different. It need not be transitive: $A$ can be close to $B$ and $B$ close to $C$ while $A$ is not close to $C$. Therefore, approximate pairwise similarity must not automatically trigger identity merging.

Neither exact operational equivalence on a restricted test family nor approximate similarity establishes phenomenal identity. The claim remains bounded by the observation model and the interventions actually considered.

### 11.6 Proposition 5: Finite-path lifting criterion

Fix $Z$, $A_\mu^u$, $\rho$, $Z_0$, and a finite descriptor/action sequence as in Section 9.8. Assume all constraints relevant to continuation are represented in the state. There exists a low-level path beginning in $Z_0$ that projects to the entire sequence if and only if $L_n\neq\varnothing$.

**Proof.** At step zero, $L_0$ is exactly the compatible initial states. Inductively, $z'\in L_{j+1}$ precisely when there is a state in $L_j$ with an allowed $u_j$ transition to $z'$ and the required next descriptor. By the induction hypothesis, that predecessor has a witnessing prefix. Extending the prefix supplies a witness for $z'$. Conversely, the endpoint of every witnessing prefix is inserted by the recurrence. The claim follows at step $n$. $\square$

The proof establishes a decision rule for a stipulated finite model. It does not establish the completeness of a measured graph. With cumulative nonnegative cost as the only additional path variable, retaining the least-cost witness per endpoint suffices for a fixed budget. More general history-dependent obligations require an appropriate monitor state or multiple nondominated histories.

### 11.7 Proposition 6: Edgewise realization is insufficient

Existence of a witness for each projected edge does not imply a compatible witness for the concatenated path.

**Proof.** In Section 9.7, the first edge is witnessed by $(a,b)$ and the second by $(c,d)$. The only permitted first-step endpoint from $a$ is $b$, which has no $v$ successor. Thus $L_1=\{b\}$ and $L_2=\varnothing$, although both projected edges exist. $\square$

This is a standard kind of coarse-state ambiguity expressed here as a realization contract. No priority claim is made for the elementary graph result. Its role is to expose an inference that an experiential "compiler" must not make.

### 11.8 Corollary: A strong local lifting contract suffices

Under the condition of Section 9.9, every finite projected path lifts from each compatible initial state.

**Proof.** Start from that state. At each step the condition provides a successor in the required next fiber. Finite induction constructs the full path. $\square$

This is not a robustness theorem: other successors may violate the requested sequence. Existential reachability, universal nondeterministic safety, and probability of successful execution require distinct contracts.

## 12. Empirical Predictions and Competing Explanations

### 12.1 Prediction family P1: Context-sensitive generalization

A preregistered binding model predicts how specified contextual variables modify responses to the same delivered stimulus. It must outperform not only a context-free stimulus model but also a strong contextual baseline with comparable information and modeling capacity.

**Support:** improved held-out predictive performance and calibration, particularly in contexts or combinations omitted from training. **Challenge:** no practically relevant improvement, or gains eliminated by a simpler contextual model. A label effect by itself supports neither a uniquely binding-based account nor H-P.

### 12.2 Prediction family P2: Compositional transformation transfer

A model fitted to selected aroma, intensity, and context combinations predicts new combinations using shared rules rather than a separate entry for every condition.

**Support:** successful held-out composition with interpretable limits. **Challenge:** performance collapses outside familiar combinations, or a flat matched-capacity model transfers just as well. The claim is about explanatory economy and transfer, not an assertion that ordinary neural networks cannot learn interactions.

### 12.3 Prediction family P3: Binding-sensitive relational contrasts

Construct conditions that preserve relevant component distributions as closely as feasible while changing their source, temporal, spatial, or expectation-related binding. The model predicts a specific change in reported organization or discriminability.

**Support:** predicted relational effects after controls for stimulus delivery, attention, timing, and reporting. **Challenge:** the effect disappears under controls or is fully accounted for by unmodeled low-level differences. Perfect physical matching is difficult; residual differences must not be hidden beneath the phrase *same components*.

### 12.4 Prediction family P4: Intervention correspondence

An independently selected intervention has a prespecified effect on a binding variable and its downstream measured consequences. The bridge is tested on interventions not used to fit its correspondence.

**Support:** low held-out commutation error with acceptable on-target effect and bounded off-target changes. **Challenge:** predictive decoding remains high while intervention correspondence fails. This dissociation would show that a correlational representation was mistaken for a realization map.

### 12.5 Prediction family P5: Temporal and historical organization

A dynamical model predicts onset, persistence, adaptation, and order effects better than static summaries and competitive history-aware baselines.

**Support:** transfer to new temporal schedules without adding a parameter for each schedule. **Challenge:** gains disappear after accounting for simple decay or adaptation, or the supposedly necessary history terms have no measurable contribution.

### 12.6 Prediction family P6: Restricted cross-realization correspondence

A transformation defined at the high level can have distinct lower-level implementations while preserving the tested causal profile. A computational fixture, a learned neural model, and human measurements can be studied as separate tracks.

**Support:** bounded correspondence under independently defined tasks and interventions. **Challenge:** a shared semantic label is the only commonality, or alignment works only after outcome-dependent remapping. Even successful correspondence would not establish that all implementations share phenomenal character.

### 12.7 Prediction family P7: Sequential realization and hidden-state discrimination

A preregistered model predicts complete sequences rather than only isolated changes. It must distinguish conditions with matched current descriptors but independently characterized differences in future response. Calibration of each elementary intervention is separated from evaluation of their compositions.

**Support:** accurate sequence prediction with compatible low-level witnesses or calibrated outcome distributions, including correct rejection of unsupported projected paths. **Challenge:** isolated-step accuracy coexists with sequence failure; apparent success requires an undeclared reset; or a matched history-aware baseline performs equally well with less structure.

A failure can show that a chart is insufficient for planning while leaving its descriptive usefulness intact. The hypothesis under test must name which function the chart claims to support.

### 12.8 Prediction family P8: Separation and interaction of descriptive families

If ACO-derived regime descriptors or SMC-derived competence measures are included, they are independently operationalized and compared with sensory, contextual, and reporting baselines. The model predicts which variables affect immediate judgments, delayed descriptions, or transfer, without declaring every competence change a qualitative change.

**Support:** a prespecified incremental effect or dissociation that survives information-matched controls and independent replication. **Challenge:** the measures only restate the target reports, fail reliability, or supply no incremental prediction. This family tests the proposed application of those source frameworks; it does not validate their entire ontologies.

### 12.9 Why no single positive result is decisive

These prediction families overlap with established contextual, relational, or dynamical research programs. This is intentional: the proposal should enter a comparative scientific landscape rather than claim exclusive ownership of contextuality. Its incremental value must arise from a jointly constrained architecture that predicts more with fewer unsupported additions.

A model that is merely renamed after successful fitting has not tested the theory. The binding graph, target contrasts, permissible model updates, and decisive holdouts must be fixed before confirmatory analysis.

## 13. A Prospective Mint Research Program

### 13.1 Stage A: Formal and synthetic preparation

Build the record types, dimension registry, graph validation, train-test separation, uncertainty handling, and claim restrictions. Test them on synthetic data where the generating process is fully known. Synthetic trials verify implementation behavior and reveal failure modes. They supply no empirical evidence for human phenomenology.

The companion technical brief contains a small executable algebraic fixture. It demonstrates role sensitivity, contextual dependence, a commutation check, and rejection of an infeasible target. Its outputs are consequences of an explicitly chosen mathematical construction, not discovered properties of mint. A second finite-transition fixture tests full-path witnesses, cumulative budgets, incomplete-model uncertainty, and existential viability. Both are formal preparation, not psychophysical experiments.

### 13.2 Stage B: Within-subject descriptive calibration

Establish whether participants can reliably make the distinctions the proposed atlas requires. Use repeated comparisons and bounded rating tasks rather than assuming that a long adjective list is a valid coordinate system. Record vocabulary comprehension, response uncertainty, fatigue, and missingness.

A participant's inability to rate an investigator-selected axis should not be converted to zero intensity. It can indicate an unsuitable instrument, absent concept, uncertain report, or inapplicable dimension.

Sample size is to be determined by a preregistered precision or power analysis informed by pilot reliability. This paper does not supply an empirical effect size or assert that an arbitrary participant count is sufficient.

### 13.3 Stage C: Bounded factorial and compositional study

An illustrative design space comprises two aroma preparations, three independently documented delivery-strength levels, two approved temperature conditions, and two framing conditions. This yields $2\times3\times2\times2=24$ cells before repetitions and controls. It is a design example, not an exposure protocol or a claim that the factors are perceptually independent.

The delivered stimulus must be characterized independently of the report. Use neutral or balanced framing; any deception requires appropriate review and debriefing. Randomize or counterbalance order within a design that accounts for carryover. A predefined subset of combinations remains unavailable during model fitting and selection.

Fresh-versus-dried preparations should enter a separate, explicitly matched block because they change more than one physical property. Decay-associated qualities should initially be represented only through safe, approved substitutes or nonexposure tasks; spoiled material is outside the proposed tasting protocol.

### 13.4 Stage D: Neural correspondence

Use an appropriate institutional collaboration or suitable existing data. A recording modality is chosen for a specific question, not because it offers the most visually compelling activity map. Define time alignment, preprocessing, nuisance controls, measurement resolution, and independent regions or feature-selection rules in advance.

Test whether binding-sensitive predictors explain held-out neural or behavioral relations beyond stimulus identity, intensity, task, motor response, and history-aware alternatives. Evidence at this stage is primarily correlational unless the design supports causal inference.

### 13.5 Stage E: Intervention and replication

Begin causal investigation with authorized sensory and contextual manipulations. Any direct neural intervention belongs only within independently approved professional research or clinical protocols; no stimulation settings or do-it-yourself procedure are specified here.

Distinguish the total effect of a stimulus manipulation from a hypothesized neural mediation pathway. Conditioning on a measured neural mediator can change the estimand and introduce bias; mediation claims require additional assumptions beyond randomized stimulus assignment.

An independent replication should freeze the confirmatory model and test family. Failure should be analyzed before adding dimensions or special cases. A changed ontology belongs to a subsequent study, with a new holdout.

### 13.6 Stage F: Sequential contrasts and continuation-sensitive charts

After elementary interventions have been independently characterized, evaluate a frozen set of action sequences. Preserve the starting-state uncertainty, actual intertrial intervals, any resets, and the full recorded intervention order. Compare the content-only chart, a history-augmented chart, and a strong dynamical baseline with equivalent information access.

A useful diagnostic is whether two conditions matched on the current response instrument nevertheless require different continuation models. A positive finding would support state refinement for that task, not prove that the initial experiences were phenomenally identical or different. The study should also include sequences deliberately outside the model's identified domain, for which calibrated nonprediction is the appropriate response.

No extra exposure or neural intervention is authorized by this stage description. It changes the analysis question and the protocol design obligations, not the independently approved intervention boundary.

## 14. Analysis, Baselines, and Falsification

### 14.1 Required baseline family

The comparison set should include a stimulus-only model; a contextual additive model; a contextual model with selected interactions; a history-aware dynamical model; a neural predictor when such measurements are available; an untyped or role-scrambled counterpart of the binding architecture; and a matched-capacity flexible model.

The role-scrambling ablation must preserve as much nonrelational information as possible. Otherwise its failure can be explained by information destruction rather than the necessity of binding roles. Context and history ablations similarly require careful interpretation: withholding predictive inputs is not by itself evidence of a uniquely semantic mechanism.

The most convincing result would be improved transfer under comparable information access, parameter budget, and model-selection effort. A modest binding model that only defeats a deliberately impoverished baseline is insufficient.

### 14.2 Evaluation levels

Primary outcomes should be preregistered proper predictive scores or other metrics matched to the response scale. Secondary outcomes can include calibration, rank preservation, trajectory error, relation-sensitive contrast accuracy, and intervention-commutation error. They should not be compressed into a single *consciousness score*.

Repeated observations from one participant are not independent participants. Evaluation should distinguish within-person held-out sessions, cross-context generalization, and cross-person generalization. Confidence intervals or resampling must respect participant and session dependence.

Feature extraction, normalization, dimensionality selection, graph selection, hyperparameter tuning, and chart alignment must all be learned from training data or separate calibration data. Holding out only the final regression stage does not prevent leakage.

### 14.3 Practical significance and equivalence

A statistically detectable improvement can be scientifically negligible. Specify a smallest relevant gain and reliability-aware tolerances before confirmatory testing. Failure to reject a difference is not proof of equivalence. A selective-control claim requires evidence of a meaningful target effect and sufficiently small off-target effects, not merely nonsignificant secondary comparisons.

Uncertainty should propagate from trial measurement through latent-coordinate estimation to intervention and transfer claims. Missing, censored, and inapplicable observations require different handling.

### 14.4 Falsification and revision

The following outcomes would count against particular versions of the program: no reproducible descriptive structure; binding roles providing no benefit over matched models; context effects confined to response labeling; poor held-out composition; failure of hypothesized intervention correspondence; unstable coordinates across repeat measurement; or a need for unbounded special cases.

Not every negative result falsifies every relational view of experience. Conversely, that logical fact must not be used to immunize the current implementation. The correct disposition can be *this model failed in this jurisdiction*, *this bridge is unsupported*, or *the available measurement cannot discriminate the alternatives*.

### 14.5 Operationalizing SBQ without turning it into a consciousness scale

The following profile is a proposed application of SBQ's five interacting dimensions [M11, Section 3], not an inherited validated instrument.

- **SBQ dimension:** Structural coherence  
  **Candidate research-model diagnostic:** Rate of incompatible typed claims and violated declared relationships  
  **Required limitation:** Internal consistency does not establish empirical truth

- **SBQ dimension:** Generativity  
  **Candidate research-model diagnostic:** Correct held-out combinations or sequences per declared modeling budget  
  **Required limitation:** Count valid predictions, not merely generated hypotheses

- **SBQ dimension:** Perturbation stability  
  **Candidate research-model diagnostic:** Preservation of prespecified performance and calibration under bounded nuisance changes  
  **Required limitation:** Correct detection of a real change must not be penalized as instability

- **SBQ dimension:** Constraint alignment  
  **Candidate research-model diagnostic:** Compliance with identity, data-role, intervention, and claim-scope contracts  
  **Required limitation:** Administrative compliance does not establish the mechanism

- **SBQ dimension:** Minimal distortion  
  **Candidate research-model diagnostic:** Information loss in report normalization and reliance on post hoc exceptions  
  **Required limitation:** A simpler model is not better when it erases genuine distinctions

The profile belongs to an account or transformation, not directly to the pleasantness or intensity of an experience. A well-grounded model of an aversive event can have high representational integrity. A polished but poorly supported model of a pleasant event can have low integrity. Likewise, stability of a mistaken category is not automatically a scientific virtue.

These diagnostics should remain a profile with declared denominators, uncertainty, and tradeoffs. An aggregate score, if used for a bounded engineering comparison, must not be renamed a degree of consciousness. SBQ's energy landscape is an analogy in its source, not a physical potential governing neurons [M11, Section 4].

### 14.6 Separate empirical findings from continuity and admission status

SMT can audit a declared transformation of research records, including a probabilistic preservation condition with explicit observation rules [M9, Sections 8-11]. The empirical evaluation must independently report whether the data support, contradict, or fail to resolve the relevant hypothesis. A required invariant that is unobserved may block an audit claim while leaving the empirical proposition unresolved.

For example, a chart migration can lack authorized lineage and therefore fail continuity admission even when its numerical predictions match. Conversely, it can preserve every declared record field while propagating an empirically wrong model. The workbench records both results. It does not use the word *collapse* to imply phenomenal loss when only the research artifact's continuity failed.

CSC adds a separate candidate-to-admitted governance event [M14, Sections 4-5]. A precisely serialized model, an admitted research artifact, an empirically supported predictor, and a warranted phenomenal-constitution claim are four different statuses. The approval of a protocol permits its bounded execution; it cannot determine its outcome.

## 15. Worked Formal Illustrations

### 15.1 Context, roles, and coupled outputs

Consider a deliberately artificial high-level state $(a,b,h)$, where $a$ and $b$ are role-distinguished components and $h$ is a contextual or historical variable. Define

$$
q=(a,b,ab+0.25h).
\tag{28}
$$

This $q$ is a synthetic descriptor, not measured phenomenology. Encode the same state at a lower level as

$$
z=(a+b,a-b,h).
\tag{29}
$$

The inverse map recovers $a=(z_1+z_2)/2$, $b=(z_1-z_2)/2$, and $h=z_3$. Increasing $a$ by $\delta$ corresponds to adding $(\delta,\delta,0)$ to $z$. Applying that lower-level change and decoding produces the same result as applying the high-level transformation and recomputing (28). This is an exact commutation property by construction.

At $(a,b,h)=(0.2,0.8,0)$, the descriptor is $(0.2,0.8,0.16)$. Increasing $a$ by $0.1$ gives $(0.3,0.8,0.24)$. The interaction coordinate changes by $0.08$ even though $b$ remains fixed. Separate inputs do not imply orthogonal output changes.

Indeed, with $h$ fixed, the pullback of an output Euclidean metric has the form

$$
J^\top J=
\begin{bmatrix}
1+b^2 & ab\\
ab & 1+a^2
\end{bmatrix}.
\tag{30}
$$

The cross term is nonzero when $ab\neq0$. A target $(0.3,0.8,0.16)$ is not reachable while keeping $h=0$ and obeying (28). It should be identified as incompatible with the construction rather than presented as a feasible independently controlled profile.

This example is intentionally simple enough that every result can be inspected. It shows what an implementation must distinguish: roles, coupling, context, transformation correspondence, and feasible targets. The relation between such a construction and actual experience remains an empirical question.

### 15.2 Same displayed state, different available future

The second illustration is the four-state construction in Section 9.7. Its displayed middle descriptor $q_1$ hides the distinction between $b$ and $c$. This example makes a further kind of binding relevant: the binding between a current descriptor, the concrete state that realizes it, and the next permitted action. It is not enough to bind the descriptor to *some* state on each occasion; the selected witnesses must belong to one compatible history.

The companion fixture returns no complete lift for the two-step sequence, returns a witness after adding a compatible second transition, and requires an explicit additional step when a bridge between the two middle states is introduced. It also distinguishes a complete toy graph's negative result from an unresolved query in a partial graph. These are properties of the stated model.

Together the two illustrations show why an experiential research architecture needs more than feature arithmetic. The first checks roles and contextual coupling within a state; the second checks compatibility across a trajectory. Neither asserts that a conscious episode has already been realized.

The fields support a claim ladder rather than a single verdict. ## 16. Objections and Limits

### 16.1 Is this merely a more elaborate description?

It could be. A graph with many contextual annotations can redescribe known results without predicting anything. The decisive difference would be transferable transformation rules, reduced out-of-sample error, and independently tested intervention structure. Without those, the contribution remains a representational proposal.

### 16.2 Does semantic binding presuppose a conscious interpreter?

Not by the definition used here. Typed differentiation and consequence can be modeled without assuming a linguistic interpreter. However, avoiding that assumption does not establish consciousness in the modeled system. The explanatory challenge cannot be solved by defining every useful relation as already experiential.

### 16.3 Does context merely relocate the mystery?

A contextual constitution may explain why one experience differs from another while leaving open why any such event is experienced. That would be genuine but limited progress. The proposal is valuable only if it clarifies and constrains what needs explanation; adding the word *binding* to the unexplained phenomenon would not suffice.

### 16.4 Can a rich enough matrix already represent the proposal?

Potentially. A matrix or dynamical model with appropriately defined states can encode role structure, history, and context. The present hypothesis is not an argument against mathematics or computation. Its challenge is to a particular explanatory decomposition, not to numerical representation as such.

If a competitor's matrix captures the same independently tested causal organization more parsimoniously, the binding account should acknowledge that equivalence or advantage. An architectonic representation need not be the unique correct notation.

### 16.5 Are qualitative dimensions truly compositional?

That is an empirical question. Some qualities may combine nonlinearly, require joint description, or appear only in particular contexts. The framework permits such interactions but pays an explanatory cost for each additional dependency. Noncompositional residuals must not be hidden by assigning a new primitive to every observation.

### 16.6 Can structural correspondence distinguish inverted or absent experience?

Not necessarily. Observational and interventional agreement on the chosen test set may leave several phenomenal interpretations open. The identifiability limitation is not a reason to abandon experiments; it is a reason to state what their results actually discriminate.

Evidence that a binding variable plays a causal role in reporting does not alone prove that it is the felt quality. Conversely, the absence of a universally accepted phenomenal test does not negate the reality or importance of ordinary first-person reports.

### 16.7 Does nonfundamental spacetime follow?

No. The formalism does not derive spacetime, abolish physical objects, or establish that consciousness is ontologically prior to physics. Hoffman's observer-first research program motivates one route into the problem, but its stronger metaphysical claims are not premises of this paper. The supplied interview itself acknowledges an outstanding burden to recover relevant physical structures [T1, approximately 1:22:25-1:23:37].

The manuscript makes no claims about telepathy, anomalous aerial phenomena, altered-state entities, or access to a hidden reality. None follows from local charts, binding graphs, or Markov descriptions.

### 16.8 Does the source integration settle the philosophical question?

No. ACO provides one explicitly non-phenomenal boundary for its use of *consciousness*, Locus supplies a theory of semantic constitution, and H-P asks an additional question about qualitative specificity. Their joint inclusion does not constitute three independent arguments establishing the same conclusion. The disagreement over the role and measurability of qualia must remain visible [M1, Section 14.5; M6, Section 10].

Nor does the ability to describe an episode with an elaborate binding record establish that the record identifies its sufficient physical support. The proposed integration is valuable only if its distinctions lead to better measurements, predictions, or explanatory exclusions.

### 16.9 Does a substrate-neutral specification imply substrate-neutral experience?

No. A specification can be implemented in different media while preserving selected operations. Whether phenomenal character also transfers is a further hypothesis. Biological features, dynamical properties, intrinsic causal organization, or other constraints could matter in ways the chosen specification misses.

A deterministic simulation can validate an analysis pipeline without possessing the properties its variables describe. A simulated cooling variable is not thereby a cold sensation.

## 17. Ethical and Engineering Boundaries

Research with human participants requires independent ethical oversight, informed consent, appropriate sensory-exposure review, accessibility, and an explicit plan for privacy and withdrawal. No potentially spoiled stimulus is justified by the conceptual usefulness of a decay contrast. No invasive intervention is justified by a desired coordinate change.

Experience-related data can contain sensitive bodily, autobiographical, and affective information. The default technical design should minimize collection, separate identities from trial data, preserve consent restrictions, and prevent secondary use outside the declared scope.

For artificial systems, the architecture must keep semantic competence, causal organization, agency, phenomenal status, and capacity for welfare-relevant experience as distinct questions. Uncertainty should not be converted into either an unsupported declaration of sentience or an unsupported universal denial. ## 18. Discussion: What a Successful Program Would Add

The most productive outcome would not be a database row declaring that mint equals a million particular numbers. It would be a family of calibrated, context-bounded models that predict how qualitative distinctions change when their physical and relational support changes.

Such a program could clarify the difference between stable identity and changing character: how an experience remains mint-like while becoming stronger, less fresh, spatially displaced, or temporally extended. It could distinguish transformations of component magnitude from transformations of binding role. It could identify when apparently independent dimensions are actually coupled, when a correspondence fails across people or contexts, and when the same displayed quality profile conceals a continuation-relevant state difference. The latter result would distinguish a descriptive atlas from a sufficient state representation for intervention planning.

The architecture also makes explanatory overreach easier to detect. A lexical name would no longer masquerade as an experiential primitive. An intervention on a sensory input would no longer silently become proof of neural mediation. A reproducible simulation would no longer silently become a conscious realization.

## Mathematical Description Is Not Phenomenal Capture

### Public orientation

The Locus of Qualia asks what a rigorous description of a taste such as mint would need to preserve: intensity, quality, context, time, comparison, preparation, physiology, language, and subject-indexed report. Its mathematics organizes a research program. It does not capture what mint tastes like merely by assigning coordinates.

### Adversarial tests

1. **Coordinate-arbitrariness attack.** Axes reflect investigator choices, vocabulary, or model geometry. Coordinates need operational definitions, calibration, and sensitivity analysis.
2. **Metric-as-phenomenology attack.** Distance in a representation is treated as experienced similarity. Behavioral, linguistic, neural, and phenomenal relations must remain distinct.
3. **Mint-overfit attack.** A framework tuned to one worked domain fails for other modalities, cultures, preparations, or unfamiliar experiences. Cross-domain tests are required.
4. **Report-bias attack.** Language, expectation, memory, demand, and available categories shape reports. Report is evidence, not transparent access.
5. **Cross-subject identity attack.** Aligned coordinates are interpreted as identical qualia. Correspondence under a measurement profile does not establish phenomenal identity.
6. **Temporal-collapse attack.** Onset, adaptation, aftertaste, expectation, and memory are compressed into one point. The object of comparison must be time-indexed.
7. **Physiology-omission attack.** Preparation and semantics are modeled while receptor, neural, bodily, or clinical differences are ignored. Scope and omitted mechanisms must be explicit.
8. **Precision-theater attack.** More symbols and decimal values imply more knowledge than the evidence supports. Uncertainty and identifiability must accompany every estimate.

### Claim and evidence ceiling

The paper establishes a typed descriptive and experimental scaffold. Limited formal results hold inside declared definitions and assumptions. They do not prove a phenomenal manifold, recover subjective character, establish neural mechanism, or permit comparison of moral standing.

Empirical publication claims require current sensory-science, neuroscience, psychophysics, philosophy, and statistical sources; participant protocols; calibration; uncertainty; preregistration where appropriate; and independent replication. Author-provided references must be checked for exact support. No external research was added in this pass.
