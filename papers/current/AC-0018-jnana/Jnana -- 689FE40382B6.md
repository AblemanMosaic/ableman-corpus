## Abstract

Jnana is a candidate governed semantic compilation architecture that separates canonical meaning, admissibility law, constitutional amendment constraints, procedural execution, external effects, structural receipts, and derived projections. Its central question is not merely whether computation can run, but whether a proposed transition may be admitted, lowered, executed, allowed to affect a domain, committed to canonical state, and retained as precedent under declared authority and evidence.

The source paper presents this architecture through a biological metaphor: MCIR as semantic genome, MCIR-Law as developmental law, PEIR as regulated expression, Domain Effects as an immune membrane, ReceiptLog as structural memory, and state projections as phenotype. The metaphor is pedagogically strong, but it is not evidence of implementation or conformance. This revision makes the boundary contracts explicit, separates architecture from implementation, qualifies claims of semantic identity and negative-power guarantees, adds ambiguous-effect and recovery states, and locates Jnana within the larger Governed Semantic Kernel, Constitutional Computing, Governed Meaning Base, and semantic-language stack.

Jnana's strongest contribution is **layered constitutional containment**. A bounded semantic root and decidable admission regime can govern potentially expressive execution only if execution cannot silently widen meaning or authority, external effects cross explicit membranes, receipts remain independently checkable, and every conformance claim is limited to the verified vertical slice. Refusal is a valid outcome. So are deferment, explicit gap, and unresolved effect status. Persistence is earned by evidence; it is never inferred from narrative continuity.

## 1. Definition, Scope, and Claim Ceiling

**Jnana** is a candidate architectural configuration of a Governed Semantic Kernel in which semantic proposals move through distinct representation, law, execution, effect, evidence, and projection boundaries [JNANA-II].

The architecture proposes these roles:

1. **MCIR**: canonical governed meaning and transformation structure;
2. **MCIR-Law**: a bounded, versioned admission regime;
3. **Constitutional Invariants**: constraints on law change and authority widening;
4. **PEIR**: procedural lowering and expressive execution plans;
5. **Domain Effects**: capability- and jurisdiction-bounded crossings into external domains;
6. **ReceiptLog**: independently checkable structural memory;
7. **State Projection**: derived, reproducible views for people and agents.

The source and broader corpus also contain specifications, codebases, staged runtime packages, courts, replay reports, and integration candidates carrying the Jnana name. Their presence is evidence of an implementation program, not automatic proof that one current artifact conforms to this paper or realizes the whole architecture. Exact implementation identity, version-family boundaries, and conformance remain separately adjudicated.

Jnana is not presently established here as:

* a universally correct constitution;
* a complete implementation or released operating system;
* a proof that semantic equivalence is decidable in every domain;
* a guarantee of justice, freedom, safety, correctness, or non-coercion;
* a replacement for law, institutional authority, user consent, or domain evidence;
* permission for any implementation, deployment, effect, or binding;
* a claim that biological systems literally instantiate the architecture.

## 2. The Biological Metaphor and Its Boundary

The source calls Jnana **computer DNA with admissibility**. The analogy organizes the architecture:

- **Jnana concern:** MCIR  
  **Biological metaphor:** genome  
  **Architectural obligation:** canonical, typed, versioned meaning-bearing structure

- **Jnana concern:** MCIR-Law  
  **Biological metaphor:** developmental law  
  **Architectural obligation:** explicit admission rules and outcomes

- **Jnana concern:** Constitutional Invariants  
  **Biological metaphor:** inherited viability constraints  
  **Architectural obligation:** bounded amendment and authority widening

- **Jnana concern:** PEIR  
  **Biological metaphor:** regulated expression  
  **Architectural obligation:** lowering that cannot invent commitments silently

- **Jnana concern:** Domain Effects  
  **Biological metaphor:** membrane and immune response  
  **Architectural obligation:** explicit capability and effect jurisdiction

- **Jnana concern:** ReceiptLog  
  **Biological metaphor:** structural memory  
  **Architectural obligation:** tamper-evident evidence and lineage

- **Jnana concern:** State Projection  
  **Biological metaphor:** phenotype  
  **Architectural obligation:** derived views tethered to exact state and history

The metaphor helps readers see why representation, expression, effect, and memory should not collapse into one runtime. It should not carry proof. Biological evolution does not establish that Jnana's rules are correct, that a refusal is healthy, or that a mutation is illegitimate. Terms such as *cancer*, *apoptosis*, *immune*, and *organism* remain explanatory analogies unless a separately defined computational property replaces them.

The operative architectural question is therefore:

> Is this proposed transition admissible under the exact law, authority, evidence, state, and effect boundary selected for this Jnana instance?

That question is narrower and more testable than asking whether a meaning is "allowed to exist." Material that cannot enter canonical Jnana state may still exist as source, residual, rejected proposal, external fact, or unresolved gap. Non-admission must not erase it.

## 3. Architecture Versus Instance

The word **Jnana** currently spans several identity levels that must remain distinct:

* architectural concept;
* selected five-layer Governed Semantic Kernel configuration;
* specifications and constitutional codices;
* compiler, runtime, broker, court, or service candidates;
* deployment instances and their state;
* evidence packages, receipts, and review reports;
* pedagogical metaphors and paper claims.

A document with Jnana in its title is not thereby the architecture. A runtime sharing a source tree is not thereby a conforming implementation. A passing local court does not establish corpus-wide or cross-host conformance. A deployed instance is not identical to its specification or version family.

Every conformance statement should therefore identify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Claim = \langle &architectureVersion, profile, instanceConfiguration, \\
&artifactSet, environment, court, evidence, claimCeiling\rangle
\end{aligned}.
$$

Without these coordinates, "Jnana works" is not a testable claim.

## 4. The Layered Transition Model

Let a configured Jnana instance be:

$$
J = \langle M, L, K, P, E, R, Q \rangle ,
$$

where:

* $M$ is the MCIR grammar, canonicalization, and semantic root;
* $L$ is the selected MCIR-Law admission regime;
* $K$ is the constitutional and amendment kernel;
* $P$ is the PEIR lowering and execution regime;
* $E$ is the Domain Effect membrane and capability set;
* $R$ is the ReceiptLog and external evidence interface;
* $Q$ is the state-projection regime.

A proposed transition follows partial, independently receipted stages:

$$
Canon_M(source) \rightharpoonup m \mid GAP,
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
&Admit_{L,K}(m, state, authority, evidence) \rightharpoonup ADMIT \\
&\mid DEFER \\
&\mid REFUSE \\
&\mid GAP
\end{aligned},
$$

$$
Lower_P(m, decision) \rightharpoonup p \mid GAP,
$$

$$
Run_P(p) \rightharpoonup intents \mid EXECUTION\_UNKNOWN,
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
&Cross_E(intent) \rightharpoonup EFFECT\_COMMITTED \\
&\mid EFFECT\_REFUSED \\
&\mid EFFECT\_UNKNOWN \\
&\mid RECONCILIATION\_REQUIRED
\end{aligned}.
$$

Canonical state may record only what its evidence supports. A missing receipt cannot undo an effect that may already have occurred. It requires an unknown or reconciliation state, not a convenient claim that nothing happened.

The model makes the architecture's central separation explicit:

```text
source or proposal
  -> canonical candidate
  -> admission decision
  -> procedural lowering
  -> execution and effect intents
  -> domain-effect decisions and outcomes
  -> canonical commit or explicit ambiguity
  -> independent receipt lineage
  -> derived projections
```

## 5. MCIR: Canonical Meaning Without Semantic Omniscience

MCIR provides a closed, versioned structural locus for governance-relevant meaning [MCIR]. It can make a change to admissibility, authority, transformation semantics, or capability exposure mechanically visible.

Three source claims require precision.

First, hash equality establishes identity of canonical bytes under an exact grammar, schema, normalization regime, and version. It does not by itself establish semantic equivalence across grammar versions, profiles, contexts, or interpretation regimes.

Second, canonicalization does not eliminate all ambiguity. It may represent ambiguity, alternatives, unresolved references, gaps, and nonclaims explicitly. If source meaning cannot be represented without loss, the compiler must retain the source and emit a residual or gap record rather than declare non-existence.

Third, MCIR does not decide which governance is correct. It makes governance-bearing structure inspectable. Admission remains a separate operation.

Minimum MCIR obligations within Jnana include:

* exact grammar and schema identity;
* deterministic encoding and normalization;
* source and residual custody;
* semantic identity and reference resolution;
* governance-relevance rules;
* canonical test vectors and independent implementations;
* explicit migration across versions.

## 6. MCIR-Law and Constitutional Amendment

MCIR-Law evaluates proposals under an exact law head, jurisdiction, state, authority context, evidence bundle, and time. It should emit `ADMIT`,  
`DEFER`, `REFUSE`, or `GAP` with stable reason codes and a decision receipt.

A named law does not validate itself. The root of trust, authority registry, delegation chain, revocation state, and applicable jurisdiction remain external dependencies that must be bound and checked. A proposal cannot manufacture the authority that admits it.

Law change also needs a non-circular transition:

1. encode a candidate successor law under the current semantic grammar;
2. evaluate the amendment under the predecessor law and constitutional kernel;
3. bind the authorized actors, evidence, migration, and effective epoch;
4. activate the successor as a new law head;
5. retain the predecessor, decision receipt, incompatibilities, and rollback or recovery limits.

This is not "law judging itself" in the abstract. It is one exact predecessor regime deciding whether it authorizes a named successor transition. The initial constitutional root remains a declared boot commitment, not something the system can prove legitimate from within itself.

Constitutional Invariants are therefore entrenched constraints, not magical immutable truths. If they can change, the architecture must name the exceptional successor-epoch process. If they cannot change, the implementation must state what preserves that immutability and how incompatible communities or users may exit, fork, or reject the regime. Declared immutability does not itself establish legitimacy.

## 7. PEIR and Expressive Containment

PEIR is the procedural execution layer. It may be substantially more expressive than MCIR-Law, including Turing-complete computation, while remaining contained by decidable admission and effect boundaries [TAPD].

PEIR must not silently:

* invent meaning absent from the admitted artifact;
* widen authority, capability, jurisdiction, or effect scope;
* replace an admitted dependency or law head;
* reinterpret a refusal or gap as permission;
* suppress a residual, exception, or nonclaim;
* treat execution success as evidence of semantic correctness.

Lowering therefore needs a correspondence obligation. The lowered plan binds to the admitted semantic artifact, law decision, compiler identity, compiler configuration, target environment, and expected effect surface. Independent or diverse-build checks may strengthen evidence, but no compiler can prove its own trustworthiness merely by producing a receipt.

The precise relation among PEIR, GCML, GOML, GSOL, and other procedural or operational representations remains an author decision. A safe provisional distinction is that GOML represents governed objects and roles, GSOL represents governed operations, GCML proposes a machine-facing procedural contract, and PEIR names an execution-oriented lowering within Jnana. This is a comparison map, not an identity merger or final universal pipeline [GSOL] [GCML].

## 8. Domain Effects and Effect Ambiguity

Domain Effects form the boundary between computed intent and external consequence. Every effect request should identify:

* effect type and target;
* required capability and authority;
* jurisdiction and applicable law;
* preconditions and expected prior state;
* idempotency or replay class;
* timeout and ambiguity policy;
* evidence provider and trust class;
* compensation, rollback, or reconciliation path;
* result and receipt bindings.

An attested receipt supports a claim about an effect; it is not identical to the effect. An effect can occur without a durable receipt, a receipt can be wrong or forged, and an external system can return success without satisfying the intended semantic condition. Jnana must therefore represent `EFFECT_UNKNOWN` and

`RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED` rather than forcing every call into success or refusal.

Authority at the membrane should be explicit, minimal, scope-bound, time-bound, revocable, and non-transitive. The execution environment cannot inherit ambient authority merely because the program was admitted.

## 9. ReceiptLog as Externalized Structural Memory

ReceiptLog preserves proposed transitions, decisions, lowerings, effect attempts, observations, commits, refusals, gaps, contests, and revisions. Its most important property is not narrative completeness but independent checkability.

Minimum obligations include:

* append-only or equivalently tamper-evident lineage;
* exact bindings to subject, law, meaning, state, authority, evidence, environment, and time;
* privilege separation from the process being audited;
* stable event identity and ordering semantics;
* explicit replay class and nondeterminism disclosures;
* crash, truncation, duplication, and equivocation handling;
* external export and verification;
* retention, privacy, redaction, and selective-disclosure rules.

A receipt proves only what its evidence path supports. It cannot transform a bad law, stale authority, incomplete observation, or misconfigured verifier into a correct outcome [RWR] [THR].

## 10. Projection Is Not Truth

State projections are derived views over canonical state, receipts, evidence, and selected interpretation. They should bind to exact roots, projection code, configuration, time, and freshness.

A projection is discardable only if it is reproducible from retained authoritative inputs. If the inputs are unavailable, private, mutable, or nondeterministic, discardability is not assured. Interfaces must expose stale, partial, conflicted, and non-reproducible status rather than presenting a smooth view as truth.

The **Governed Meaning Base (GMB)** is the current name in the historical `CCF -> CMB -> GMB` lineage. GMB can retain Jnana specifications, semantic artifacts, laws, decisions, receipts, conflicts, evidence, implementation claims, and projections. It is a governed meaning and knowledge substrate, not automatically a Jnana runtime or admission authority. Hyperpedia can project these objects pedagogically while preserving custody and standing.

## 11. Negative Power and the Ableman Prime Directive

The source connects Jnana to the Ableman Prime Directive (APD): affected agents should be able to detect bindings, refuse or exit where refusal is lawful, and renegotiate applicability unless immutability is explicit [TAPD]. These capacities preserve **negative power**: the practical ability to prevent, withhold, refuse, contest, exit, or revise a binding.

Let a binding record be:

$$
\begin{aligned}
B = \langle &subject, object, rule, scope, authority, conditions, \\
&visibility, refusal, exit, contest, renegotiation, time\rangle
\end{aligned}.
$$

An APD-oriented conformance claim must show more than fields in a schema. It must test whether the affected agent can actually:

* discover which bindings apply;
* understand their source and consequence;
* invoke refusal, contest, or exit through reachable interfaces;
* avoid silent retaliation or catastrophic technical lock-in within the system boundary;
* carry material objections into successor decisions;
* determine when a binding is claimed to be immutable and challenge whether that claim applies.

Jnana alone cannot guarantee absence of external legal, economic, physical, or social coercion. It can expose and constrain bindings inside a declared socio-technical boundary, produce evidence of available pathways, and refuse to claim APD conformance where those pathways are absent or merely performative.

Explicit immutability is not enough. The authority to declare a binding immutable, the affected scope, the exit or fork options, and the successor-epoch path must also be visible. Otherwise "immutable" becomes a label for unreviewable power.

## 12. Refusal, Deferment, Gap, and Recovery

Refusal is an integrity-preserving outcome when the named law establishes that a proposal is inadmissible. It should include scope, reasons, evidence, law version, authority, time, and available escalation or revision paths.

Not every non-success is refusal:

* `DEFER` means specified evidence or authority may later resolve the decision;
* `GAP` means the representation, identity, law, or target is insufficient to decide;
* `EXECUTION_UNKNOWN` means the procedural result cannot be established;
* `EFFECT_UNKNOWN` means an external consequence may have occurred without adequate evidence;
* `RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED`  
  means canonical state must not pretend certainty until bounded recovery completes.

The biological term **apoptosis** is useful only as an analogy for an orderly, integrity-preserving stop. A refused proposal is not diseased, immoral, or false. It is inadmissible under one bounded regime and may remain available for correction, appeal, fork, or evaluation elsewhere.

## 13. Emergency and Break-Glass Semantics

"No temporary override" and "break glass" conflict unless emergency authority is defined carefully.

A constitutional break-glass path should be a predeclared capability with:

* narrow trigger conditions;
* named holders and delegation limits;
* exact effect scope and duration;
* unavoidable logging where technically possible;
* explicit ambiguity handling where logging may fail;
* automatic expiry and privilege contraction;
* recovery-epoch entry;
* independent post-event review;
* successor-state and restitution obligations.

If emergency action bypasses these rules, it is not a lawful Jnana transition merely because a later receipt describes it. The system should preserve the event as an exception or violation and must not retroactively launder it into ordinary authority.

## 14. Incremental Release and Honest Conformance

The source states that Jnana cannot be partially released. That is too strong. Components, specifications, prototypes, and bounded vertical slices can be released incrementally when their standing and omissions are explicit.

What cannot be partial is the **claim of complete Jnana conformance**. A release that lacks an effect membrane, independent receipts, amendment semantics, recovery states, or usable refusal paths must not imply those properties. Each release should declare:

* which layers and contracts are present;
* which profiles and configurations are supported;
* which threats and failure states were tested;
* which evidence is reproducible;
* which layers are simulated, stubbed, external, or absent;
* the maximum claim the evidence permits.

A minimal conformance candidate should be a complete low-consequence vertical slice rather than a large collection of unbound components.

## 16. Configuration Manifold

Jnana admits many possible configurations:

* centralized, federated, personal, institutional, and peer-to-peer authority;
* immutable-root, amendable-root, epochal, forkable, and plural constitutional regimes;
* deterministic, bounded-nondeterministic, replayable, and externally reconciled execution;
* capability, object, operation, role, and jurisdiction-based effect control;
* local, remote, hardware-bound, virtualized, and cross-substrate realization;
* public, private, selectively disclosed, and privacy-preserving receipts;
* strict, permissive, multi-court, appealable, and community-specific admission.

**CM1** contains every configuration satisfying the declared architecture and minimum layer contracts. It may still contain centralized, brittle, exclusionary, slow, expensive, surveillance-heavy, or capture-prone designs.

**CM2** prefers configurations that reduce ambient authority, single-writer capture, irrecoverable ambiguity, semantic loss, forced disclosure, catastrophic exit cost, and unverifiable claims while improving substitution, recovery, accessibility, privacy, forkability, independent verification, and user sovereignty.

**Instance Configuration** selects exact grammar, law, constitutional root, authority registry, compiler, runtime, effect adapters, capabilities, receipt store, projection code, environment, trust anchors, recovery policy, and conformance court.

## Jnana as Layered Constitutional Containment Architecture

The completed P1 corpus supports a compressed role assignment for Jnana. Jnana is the **layered constitutional containment architecture** that relates meaning representation, law, procedural lowering, effect mediation, receipts, and projection. It should not absorb the identities of its formats, kernels, enforcement profiles, implementation manuals, or successor proposals.

- **Adjacent work:** *Ableman Format Specification*  
  **Supplies:** Specification ecosystem and separations among domain physics, configuration, instance selection, realization, evidence, and revision.  
  **Jnana boundary:** AFS specifies artifacts and profiles; it is not automatically the Jnana runtime.

- **Adjacent work:** MCIR family  
  **Supplies:** Canonical Intermediate Representation for admitted meaning and obligations.  
  **Jnana boundary:** MCIR is a representation layer, not the whole architecture or its authority source.

- **Adjacent work:** *The Implementation Codex*  
  **Supplies:** Construction-facing translation from specification into bounded work and evidence.  
  **Jnana boundary:** Implementation guidance does not establish a conforming Jnana instance.

- **Adjacent work:** *Integrity Enforcement Layer*  
  **Supplies:** Refusal, verification, corruption handling, and effect-bound enforcement.  
  **Jnana boundary:** IEL is an enforcement profile within or beside a realization, not the whole Jnana identity.

- **Adjacent work:** *Universal Governance Kernel*  
  **Supplies:** Compact executable governance-kernel candidate and receipt/certification surfaces.  
  **Jnana boundary:** UGK may realize selected contracts; it does not inherit Jnana-wide conformance by name.

- **Adjacent work:** *Jnana II* and related successors  
  **Supplies:** Alternative or later transformation-substrate formulations.  
  **Jnana boundary:** Their exact version-family and successor relations remain open editorial questions, not automatic merges.

The minimum vertical-slice claim should bind six independently inspectable contracts:

$$
V=(Meaning,Law,Lowering,Effect,Receipt,Projection).
$$

A slice is internally coherent only when the output identity and obligations of each contract become declared inputs to the next, and when refusal or semantic remainder cannot be bypassed through another route. This does not prove completeness, security, usefulness, or system-wide conformance.

The GMB supplies durable meaning, sources, standing, conflicts, and revision. Jnana consumes or projects governed meaning through an executable architecture; it is not the GMB itself. The universal GSM should type `REPRESENTED_AS`,  
`ADMITTED_UNDER`, `LOWERED_TO`, `AUTHORIZED_FOR_EFFECT`, `OBSERVED_BY`, `RECEIPTED_AS`,  
`PROJECTED_AS`, and `REVISED_BY`, with exact versions and claim ceilings on every edge.

Corpus integration also adds a conformance rule: **a partial realization names the contracts it implements and must not use the umbrella name as evidence for omitted layers.** A reference implementation may demonstrate one route through the architecture without becoming the architecture's sole definition.

This integration establishes four editorial commitments: adopt layered constitutional containment architecture as Jnana's official corpus role; adopt the six-contract vertical slice; adjudicate the version and successor relations among Jnana, Jnana II, and nearby system families; and define the minimum evidence required before any implementation may claim bounded Jnana conformance.

The result is a clear corpus role: Jnana is the layered constitutional containment architecture through which proposals may be represented, admitted, lowered, mediated, observed, refused, and revised. It is not every language, representation, kernel, operating system, or social program that interfaces with it.

$$
\begin{aligned}

## 17. Failure Modes and Counterevidence

Jnana should weaken or fail under evidence that:

* the law or binding-visibility problem is undecidable in the selected root language;
* PEIR or a compiler can widen meaning, authority, or effects without changing the admitted roots;
* a mutable runtime can rewrite or equivocate about its ReceiptLog;
* an external effect occurs while canonical state records refusal or non-occurrence;
* break-glass authority becomes a routine bypass or cannot be revoked;
* constitutional roots can be changed by ordinary-law authority;
* law version, delegation, revocation, or environment is absent from a decision;
* hash equality is used as semantic equivalence across incompatible versions;
* source meaning or ambiguity is erased during canonicalization;
* affected agents cannot actually discover, refuse, contest, exit, or fork bindings;
* privacy or selective disclosure is incompatible with the required receipts;
* admission becomes centralized censorship or denial of service;
* the biological metaphor hides rather than clarifies operational semantics;
* the cost and latency of the machinery exceed the value of the governed domain;
* simpler capability, transaction, policy, and audit systems provide equivalent assurance;
* implementation candidates disagree on architecture, roots, or transition semantics;
* courts prove only local fixtures while broader conformance is claimed.

The deepest threat is **constitutional simulation**: every layer and receipt is present in name, but ambient authority, mutable roots, evidence substitution, or unverifiable effects make the claimed constraints non-binding.

## 18. Conformance and Evidence Program

A Jnana conformance program should require:

1. normative schemas for every layer and boundary;
2. independent MCIR parsers, canonicalizers, and test vectors;
3. explicit MCIR-Law decidability and resource bounds;
4. constitutional-root, amendment, epoch, delegation, and revocation tests;
5. compiler-correspondence and semantic-difference tests;
6. capability non-widening and ambient-authority tests;
7. effect success, refusal, timeout, crash, duplication, and ambiguity fixtures;
8. append-only, equivocation, truncation, privilege-separation, and export tests for receipts;
9. deterministic replay where claimed and nondeterminism disclosure elsewhere;
10. projection freshness, partiality, conflict, and reproducibility tests;
11. APD detectability, refusal, contest, exit, renegotiation, and forkability tests;
12. break-glass expiry, recovery, restitution, and post-event review tests;
13. cross-host, cross-runtime, and cross-substrate replay where claimed;
14. composition tests proving obligations survive module and adapter boundaries;
15. one independently reviewed low-consequence vertical slice;
16. claim-ceiling tests that refuse conclusions broader than the evidence.

Evidence should distinguish architecture, specification, implementation candidate, configured instance, executed test, witnessed effect, and published result. None can stand in for the others.

## Lawful Semantic Development With an Explicit Remainder

### Public orientation

Jnana treats computation as governed semantic development: a proposed meaning or transformation is represented canonically, judged for admissibility, conditionally realized, bounded at effects, and preserved through evidence and revision. Refusal can protect integrity. It cannot become an excuse for opaque control, permanent stagnation, or architectural claims beyond the demonstrated layer.

### Adversarial tests

1. **Canonical-meaning oracle.** Canonical encoding is treated as proof of correct meaning. It establishes reproducible representation under a profile, not truth or adequacy.
2. **Admission-authority capture.** One gate controls what meaning may develop. Law, issuer, jurisdiction, appeal, and plural branches must be explicit.
3. **Refusal-abuse attack.** The system calls every difficult or dissenting proposal inadmissible. Refusal requires reason, scope, evidence, contest, and revision route.
4. **Effect-escape attack.** Semantic admission appears governed while implementation reaches unmediated capabilities. Cross-layer correspondence and effect-boundary tests are required.
5. **Layer-absorption attack.** Jnana is claimed to replace the GMB, compiler, OS, courts, or domain systems. Interfaces and non-identity boundaries must remain explicit.
6. **Conformance-inflation attack.** A partial realization claims the whole architecture. Profiles must state implemented layers, omitted obligations, and evidence.
7. **Liveness failure.** Repeated adjudication prevents lawful work. Budgets, deferral, escalation, and terminal outcomes are constitutional requirements.
8. **Evolution-metaphor overreach.** Semantic variation and selection are presented as biological equivalence or progress. The analogy must not confer empirical or normative standing.

### Claim and evidence ceiling

The paper defines a layered constitutional containment architecture and conformance program. It does not establish a universal computation model, complete semantic correctness, implementation maturity, or superiority to other substrates. Source-reported realizations require exact artifact, environment, test, and independent reproduction bindings.

Claims about programming languages, formal methods, security, evolutionary systems, cognition, or performance require external evidence and comparison. Internal corpus relations establish intended composition only. No external research was added in this pass.

### Reader-facing consequence

The public promise is computation that can explain which meaning was proposed, under what law it was admitted, how it became an effect, why it was refused or revised, and which parts remain outside the demonstrated boundary.

## 21. Conclusion

Jnana's central insight survives scrutiny: semantic representation, law, execution, external effect, memory, and projection should not collapse into one opaque runtime. Their separation makes it possible to ask not only whether a computation ran, but which meaning was admitted, under what law, with what authority, through which lowering, across which effect boundary, and with what evidence.

The architecture becomes stronger when its metaphors are bounded. MCIR is not omniscient DNA; it is a versioned canonical representation. MCIR-Law is not self-legitimating sovereignty; it is a bounded admission regime. PEIR does not inherit ambient permission. A receipt is not an effect, and an effect is not always reversible. Negative power is not guaranteed by syntax. Partial implementations are valuable when they do not claim the whole.

Jnana is therefore best understood as a candidate architecture for **layered constitutional containment**: expressive computation remains possible, but meaning, authority, effects, and persistence must cross explicit, evidenced boundaries. Refusal may preserve integrity. Deferment and uncertainty may preserve honesty. Canonical state may advance only as far as its evidence allows.
