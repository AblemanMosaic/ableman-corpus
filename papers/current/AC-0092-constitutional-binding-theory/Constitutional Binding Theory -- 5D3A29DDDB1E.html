Constitutional Binding Theory

Governed Anchor Resolution Across Semantic Systems

A binding may be provisional, plural, contested, or refused.

Author
Adam Ableman
Constitutional Computing
Contact: ableman.research@gmail.com

Abstract

Semantic systems repeatedly reduce a field of possible interpretations into a structure that can guide further reasoning or action. Constitutional Binding Theory (CBT) describes a recurring form of that reduction: a system declares the relevant possibility manifold, resolves or constructs an anchor under explicit authority and criteria, crystallizes typed relations around that anchor, realizes only what the resulting constraints permit, and revises the binding when evidence changes.

The pattern appears at several scales in the corpus: lexical identity through Global Term Keys; compositional relations through semantic graphs; canonical machine-legible meaning through MCIR; procedural plans through execution representations; system configuration through constitutional specifications; and corpus topology through the Governed Meaning Base. These are proposed instances of a shared maneuver, not evidence that the systems are identical or that every semantic process has one architecture.

The central thesis is therefore bounded: actionable meaning can be made accountable through successive governed bindings whose alternatives, authority, provenance, uncertainty, and revision conditions remain inspectable. A binding may be provisional, plural, contested, or refused. Selection alone does not make it lawful, true, legitimate, or complete.

1. The Irreducible Maneuver

CBT proposes five recurrent operations:

  1. Declare the manifold. Identify the relevant alternatives, including unknown and presently inexpressible cases.
  2. Resolve or construct an anchor. Select, create, or retain one or more reference points under declared criteria and authority.
  3. Crystallize relations. Type the local structure that follows from the anchor without erasing excluded alternatives.
  4. Realize within scope. Interpret, store, plan, or act only within the binding’s declared jurisdiction.
  5. Observe and revise. Bind evidence, contradiction, appeal, expiration, and amendment to the exact binding.

The maneuver does not require a single winner. It may yield a distribution, an ambiguity set, a fork, an abstention, or a typed unknown. Some domains should delay collapse because the cost of a false anchor exceeds the cost of uncertainty.

2. A Binding Record

Let the possibility manifold at layer i be Mi and its candidate anchors be Ai ⊆ Mi. A governed binding record can be represented as:

Bi = (Mi, Ai, ai, Ci, Ui, Xi, Pi, Ri),

where ai is the selected anchor or anchor set, Ci the criteria and constraints, Ui the authority and jurisdiction, Xi the excluded or deferred alternatives, Pi provenance and evidence, and Ri revision conditions.

Admission requires more than selection:

$$ \begin{aligned} &Admit(B_i)=Authorized(U_i) \\ &\land CriteriaDeclared(C_i) \\ &\land ProvenanceBound(P_i) \\ &\land RevisionOpen(R_i) \end{aligned}. $$

This predicate still does not prove that ai is true. It states that the system may proceed under a declared, inspectable, revisable binding. A useful implementation should also carry confidence, contest, expiry, and the semantic remainder that the representation could not preserve.

3. Layered Instances

3.1 Lexical Binding

A surface form such as bank can denote a financial institution, river edge, aviation maneuver, or reserve. A Global Term Key can provide a stable candidate identity while retaining aliases, competing senses, invariant floor, provenance, and context. The token is not identical to the concept, and the chosen concept is not made true by the key.

3.2 Compositional Binding

Resolved terms do not determine a proposition. A semantic graph binds roles, predicates, modality, temporality, causality, scope, and implication. Multiple parses may remain admissible. The graph is a representation of a reading, not the utterance itself.

3.3 Canonical Meaning Binding

MCIR can transform admitted semantic structure into a canonical Intermediate Representation. Canonicalization is always relative to a declared schema and equivalence regime. Byte stability, structural equivalence, semantic equivalence, and behavioral equivalence remain different claims.

3.4 Procedural Binding

A procedural representation lowers a meaning-bearing specification into ordered, scoped, effect-bounded steps. Lowering can introduce choices and semantic remainder. It therefore requires obligations and evidence, not only translation.

3.5 System Binding

A constitutional system specification separates invariants, admissible configurations, preferred configurations, selected instance configuration, realized implementation, evidence, and revision. A selected configuration is not yet a working implementation.

3.6 Corpus Binding

The current architecture is the Governed Meaning Base (GMB) in the historical CCF -> CMB -> GMB lineage. GMB can retain sources, concepts, candidate identities, claims, conflicts, bindings, standing, evidence, and revisions. Inclusion in GMB does not grant truth, identity, or canonical standing.

4. Binding, Collapse, and Information Loss

The source describes binding as entropy reduction and crystallization. The metaphor is useful if bounded. A binding reduces the alternatives the system treats as actionable under one purpose, but it need not destroy the larger manifold. The system should retain:

  • which alternatives were considered;
  • which were excluded and why;
  • what remained unknown;
  • what representation loss occurred;
  • who had authority to decide;
  • what evidence could reopen the binding.

No thermodynamic or information-theoretic result follows from the metaphor without a formal probability space, measure, and transformation. CBT uses high entropy as a qualitative description of underdetermination unless a domain-specific measure is supplied.

5. Lawfulness Is Scope-Relative

A binding is constitutional only relative to a constitution, jurisdiction, authority, procedure, and subject. It may be procedurally admissible yet factually wrong, locally valid yet globally incompatible, or useful for one task and harmful for another.

Minimum legitimacy conditions are:

  • declared subject and jurisdiction;
  • identifiable authority and limits;
  • inspectable selection or construction criteria;
  • source and transformation provenance;
  • alternatives and uncertainty retained at appropriate depth;
  • effects attributable to the binding;
  • contest, rollback, and amendment routes;
  • evidence adequate to the claim made.

Calling a collapse constitutional cannot substitute for satisfying these conditions.

7. Relation to Computation

CBT does not oppose expressive computation. It distinguishes computational possibility from admissible continuation. A general-purpose substrate may support arbitrary programs, while a constitutional boundary restricts which programs, effects, and claims are admitted in a particular jurisdiction.

The relevant contrast is therefore not simply Turing-complete versus non-Turing-complete. It is:

Reachable ≠ Admissible ≠ Authorized ≠ Evidenced.

Containment, resource bounds, capability boundaries, type systems, proof obligations, and effect gates can constrain a Turing-complete interior. Whether any particular root is computationally universal is an implementation property requiring proof.

8. Primitives, Invariants, Dimensions, and Adjacencies

Primitives: manifold, candidate anchor, selected anchor, anchor set, criterion, authority, jurisdiction, binding, exclusion, remainder, provenance, evidence, contest, expiry, realization, and revision.

Invariants: a binding identifies its subject and scope; selection does not erase alternatives; authority does not imply truth; representation does not imply realization; realization does not imply success; evidence remains claim-scoped; revision preserves lineage; and no layer silently promotes its output into standing at another layer.

Dimensions: cardinality of anchors, confidence, reversibility, authority centralization, provenance depth, semantic loss, time horizon, context sensitivity, pluralism, contestability, evidence strength, and effect severity.

Adjacencies: Semantic Manifold Topology describes the shape and traversal of meaning spaces; MCIR supplies one canonical representation profile; Ableman’s Razor and the realization funnel separate stages of commitment; the GMB retains the resulting objects and disputes. These are typed interfaces, not identity mergers.

10. Realization Funnel

$$ \begin{aligned} &Possibility \\ &\rightarrow Specification \\ &\rightarrow CM_1 \\ &\rightarrow CM_2 \\ &\rightarrow IC \\ &\rightarrow IR \\ &\rightarrow Evidence \\ &\rightarrow Revision \end{aligned}. $$

  • Possibility: expose the broad manifold, including harmful, trivial, contradictory, and presently unknown possibilities.
  • Specification: define binding objects, jurisdictions, criteria, authority, loss, and revision semantics.
  • CM1: enumerate admissible binding mechanisms and configurations.
  • CM2: select preferable configurations under accuracy, pluralism, reversibility, cost, risk, and legibility criteria.
  • IC: bind an Instance Constitution or Instance Configuration for a particular semantic system.
  • IR: construct its Instance Realization or Implementation Realization.
  • Evidence: test resolutions, transformations, effects, failure detection, contest, and rollback.
  • Revision: amend the specification and bindings while preserving the earlier record.

In MCIR and comparable names, IR means Intermediate Representation. In this funnel, realization-stage

IR means Instance Realization or Implementation Realization. The senses must not be conflated.

Cross-Layer Anchor-Resolution Pattern

Across the completed P1 corpus, Constitutional Binding Theory (CBT) is best retained as the cross-layer pattern for resolving an anchor to a governed target under explicit context, law, evidence, remainder, and revision. It is not a claim that lexical, semantic, procedural, system, and corpus bindings are identical mechanisms or that every binding is constitutional.

  • Adjacent work: The Locus of Meaning
    Supplies: A relational-constitutional account of semantic binding, jurisdiction, and consequence.
    CBT boundary: CBT abstracts the anchor-resolution maneuver across layers; it does not replace the broader meaning theory.

  • Adjacent work: Meaning as Binding
    Supplies: The compact semantic-binding thesis.
    CBT boundary: CBT generalizes a governed resolution pattern beyond semantic meaning while preserving domain-specific differences.

  • Adjacent work: Global Term Key: Constitutional Lexical Identity Through Disambiguated Semantic Binding
    Supplies: A registry and resolver for lexical identities and senses.
    CBT boundary: GTK is one lexical realization of the pattern.

  • Adjacent work: The Ableman Format Specification (AFS)
    Supplies: Profiles, schemas, and conformance claims for governed artifacts.
    CBT boundary: AFS can encode binding obligations; CBT supplies the abstract resolution relation.

  • Adjacent work: Meta-Binding as Operational Invariant
    Supplies: Obligations governing transformations of other bindings.
    CBT boundary: Meta-binding constrains how bindings survive change; CBT defines the basic anchor-resolution case.

A portable binding case is:

$$ \begin{aligned} B= (&anchor, anchor\_identity, context, law, resolver, \\ &candidates, target, evidence, remainder, standing, \\ &revision) \end{aligned}. $$

Anchor identity, candidate generation, resolution policy, selected target, evidence, and current standing remain separate. A resolver can deterministically produce the wrong target under a defective law. Two layers may instantiate the same abstract record while requiring different admissibility tests, evidence, consequences, and failure behavior.

The universality claim should therefore be comparative and defeasible. CBT is strengthened when the record exposes previously hidden common obligations and predicts failures across independently analyzed domains. It should be narrowed when the abstraction erases domain-specific constraints, adds no testable consequence, or merely renames ordinary lookup, linking, dispatch, or reference resolution.

and REBOUND_BY_REVISION. Recursive binding is represented through typed relations, not presumed uniform implementation.

This integration establishes four editorial commitments: adopt cross-layer anchor-resolution pattern as CBT’s official corpus role; adopt the binding-case record; make universality a defeasible comparative claim; and require domain-specific evidence and failure semantics for every claimed instance.

Infrastructure resolves identities, requests, entitlements, priorities, routes, and remedies under rules that may shape practical continuation. Concrete Profiles of the Anchor-Resolution Pattern

The frozen P3 field contains several downstream specializations of Constitutional Binding Theory. GOV uses it for source-to-record resolution; GTK uses it for lexical and semantic identity; CHC uses it to interpret canonicalized commitments; CGI uses it for requests, entitlements, routes, and remedies; boundary accountability uses it for concrete-to-abstract effect mapping; and shared-experience bridges use it for permissioned, directional participant resolution.

These uses support a comparative kernel:

$$ \begin{aligned} B^*= (&anchor, anchor\_identity, context, jurisdiction, law, \\ &resolver, candidates, target, evidence, concretization, \\ &permissions, perspective, remainder, standing, revision) \end{aligned}. $$

They do not support one uniform mechanism. A lexical resolver, source pointer, policy router, effect concretizer, and intersubjective bridge require different admissibility tests, consequences, authorities, privacy constraints, and refusal behavior. The theory’s universality claim is strongest when expressed as a reusable question set and record shape, not a claim of implementation equivalence.

Reverse dependency also exposes three additions. First, resolution must record who or what bears the consequence of a wrong target. Second, permissions and perspective are first-class for human-facing or participant-indexed bindings. Third, negative outcomes such as AMBIGUOUS,
WITHHELD, CONFLICT, OUT_OF_SCOPE,
and REMAINDER are lawful binding results rather than failures to force a target.

Binding Makes a Choice Accountable, Not True

Public orientation

Constitutional Binding Theory describes a recurring maneuver: declare alternatives, resolve or construct an anchor under stated criteria and authority, type relations around it, realize only within scope, and revise when evidence changes. The maneuver can produce plural, provisional, contested, refused, or unknown bindings. Selection is not truth, legitimacy, completeness, or lawful authority by itself.

Adversarial tests

  1. Manifold-closure attack. The declared alternatives are treated as complete despite unknown, inexpressible, excluded, or newly emerging possibilities.
  2. Anchor-truth attack. A selected name, identity, ontology node, configuration, or plan acquires truth because it is stable and useful.
  3. Authority-circularity attack. The binding process validates the authority that authorized the binding.
  4. Premature-collapse attack. A single anchor is chosen where ambiguity, plurality, contest, or delayed decision is safer and more faithful.
  5. Relation-inheritance attack. Relations around an old anchor migrate to a successor, alias, translation, or merged node without revalidation.
  6. Cross-layer-uniformity attack. Lexical, graph, compiler, workflow, system, and corpus bindings are treated as identical mechanisms because they share a pattern.
  7. Recursive-amplification attack. An early defective binding constrains later manifolds and becomes harder to challenge at every layer.
  8. Universality-by-example attack. Several corpus examples establish a universal semantic law without negative cases, rival patterns, or external comparison.

Claim and evidence ceiling

The paper specifies an accountable anchor-resolution pattern and cross-layer research program. It does not establish universal recurrence, Turing-like completeness, information-theoretic law, semantic truth, legitimate authority, complete manifolds, or identity among its example systems. Scope-relative lawfulness remains relative to an independently justified authority and domain.

Validation requires negative cases, rival binding models, explicit unknowns, delayed and plural outcomes, authority audits, propagation tests, cross-domain preservation contracts, a preregistered corpus, and current external research. None was performed in this pass.

Reader-facing consequence

CBT is useful when every actionable meaning can answer: what alternatives were considered, what anchor was chosen or refused, who could choose, what information was lost, which relations followed, where the choice applies, and how evidence can reopen it.

Conclusion

Meaning does not move directly from ambiguity to effect. It passes through bindings that select, relate, constrain, and expose a path for revision. CBT’s useful claim is not that every semantic system performs one literal collapse. It is that actionable semantic commitment should carry its manifold, anchor, authority, provenance, exclusions, effects, and amendment route with it.

That formulation makes binding inspectable and corrigible. It also leaves room for abstention, plurality, and reopening when the evidence changes.
